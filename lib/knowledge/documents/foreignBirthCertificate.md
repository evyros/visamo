# Birth certificate (תעודת לידה)

The original, from where they were born.

The foreign partner's birth certificate. Every couple needs it, however and wherever you married.

## How to get it

It comes from the country where the foreign partner was born, by today's
borders: someone born in Soviet Kyiv was born in Ukraine, so the
certificate is from Ukraine.

1. Get the certificate from the country of birth.
2. Have it certified in that country: an apostille, or consular
   legalization if the country isn't in the Apostille Convention (see
   [certification.md](../certification.md)).
3. Have it translated, if it needs a translation (below).

Some countries issue a multilingual or international birth certificate. It
can save the translation only when its fields are in English (Austria, the
Netherlands, Sweden). A multilingual extract in other languages, such as
Spain's, still needs a translation and an apostille:
Israel isn't one of the countries that accept these extracts without them.
Below, the countries where getting it has a catch.

### Brazil

A full birth certificate (certidão de nascimento de inteiro teor), from the
registry office (cartório), on paper: the online one may not be accepted.

### China

Someone born before about 1996 usually has no birth (medical) certificate
(出生医学证明): a notary office issues a notarized birth certificate (出生公证),
based on the household register (户口本) and the parents' documents. Then the
apostille.

### Czech Republic

Get a new birth certificate (rodný list) from the registry (matrika) of the
place of birth, and say it's for use abroad: the original from the year of
birth usually can't get an apostille. It needs a higher certification (vyšší
ověření) from the regional office before the apostille.

### Denmark

A birth and baptism certificate (fødsels- og dåbsattest) if baptized, or a
certificate of personal data (personattest) if not, from the parish office,
in English, with a handwritten signature.

### France

The full copy of the birth certificate (copie intégrale d'acte de
naissance): it names the parents and shows any marriage or divorce in its
margin. It comes from the town hall (mairie) of the place of birth: online,
by post, or on the spot. A French citizen born abroad gets it from the
Central Civil Status Service (Service central d'état civil) in Nantes,
online or by post, in about a month. Order a new one for the apostille.

Since May 2025, the apostille comes from the notaries' apostille centres,
not the court of appeal. You apply online and send the paper certificate by
post, or go to a centre in person. It takes about 3 working days. The
apostille is electronic: ask for it on paper, with a stamp and handwritten
signature, since Misrad Hapnim asks for the original.

### Germany

The international birth certificate (internationale Geburtsurkunde), from
the registry office (Standesamt) of the place of birth, not the residents'
registration office. You can order it online or by post from abroad; it
usually takes up to 2 weeks, plus shipping.

The apostille comes from an authority of the German state where it was
issued, and which one differs by state (in North Rhine-Westphalia, the
district government, Bezirksregierung): ask the registry office. No other
certification is needed first. Mail to Israel is slow, so many have the
certificate sent to family in Germany, who get the apostille and send it on.

### Italy

An extract of the birth record (estratto per riassunto dell'atto di
nascita), ideally with the parents' names (con paternità e maternità), from
the town hall (comune) where the birth was registered. It also shows what
was later noted on the record, such as a marriage. Ask for it on paper,
signed by hand, for use abroad: a certificate downloaded online, or sent by
email with a digital signature, can't get an apostille. The Italian embassy
in Israel doesn't issue birth certificates, even for Italians registered
abroad (AIRE): ask the comune, which can post it to you. The apostille comes
from the prefecture (prefettura) of the province the comune is in, and many
accept it by post, with a prepaid return envelope.

### Japan

There's no birth certificate as such: the certificate of acceptance of the
birth registration (受理証明書 for the 出生届), from the municipality where it
was registered. Only the person who registered the birth, usually a parent,
can ask for it.

### Mexico

Ask the civil registry for a birth certificate "for apostille" (acta de
nacimiento para apostilla), with a handwritten signature: one from a machine
or online can't be apostilled. The apostille comes from the same state.

### Philippines

The birth certificate from the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA). It's
in English. The apostille comes from the Department of Foreign Affairs
(DFA). For Israel, it now issues only an electronic one: the certificate and
its apostille come as files by email, and printing them makes them invalid.
The foreign partner can order both online from Israel, with an ID check.
Misrad Hapnim usually asks for the original on paper, so the clerk may push
back on the files. Explain that for Israel, the Philippines issues it only
this way: there's no paper option.

If some entries are blurred or unreadable, add a copy from the local civil
registrar to the request. A misspelled name, or a wrong day or month of
birth, is corrected by a petition to the local civil registrar, or to a
Philippine consulate when the person lives abroad. A wrong year of birth
needs a court.

### Portugal

A birth certificate (certidão de nascimento), on paper rather than online. It's
issued only in Portuguese.

### Russia

Many people with an old Soviet certificate get a new copy (повторное
свидетельство) from the civil registry office (ЗАГС) of the place of birth,
with its apostille from the regional registry archive.

### South Africa

The unabridged birth certificate, from the Department of Home Affairs, or
through the South African embassy in Israel. It can take from 6 months to
several years: order it first.

### Spain

The full birth certificate (certificación literal), from the civil registry
(Registro Civil). The multilingual extract (certificado plurilingüe), in
Spanish and French, still needs a translation and an apostille for Israel.

### Thailand

The birth certificate (สูติบัตร), or a certified copy of it from a district
office (สำนักงานเขต or ที่ว่าการอำเภอ): any district office for someone born
from 1980 on, the one where the birth was registered for someone born
earlier. A lost certificate isn't issued again: get the certified copy
instead. The district office can also issue the copy in English. A Thai
living abroad can authorize a relative in Thailand to get it, by signing the
authorization at a Thai embassy.

Thailand isn't in the Apostille Convention, so it's translated into English
and certified by the Consular Affairs Department of the Thai Ministry of
Foreign Affairs, then by the Israeli embassy in Bangkok, by appointment.
Certify the Thai document together with its English translation, not the
translation alone. If the name has changed since birth, the ministry also
asks for the name change certificate (ใบสำคัญการเปลี่ยนชื่อ). The Thai
embassy in Israel can't do any of these steps.

### Ukraine

If the certificate was issued after 1998, or is lost or worn, get a new
copy (повторне свідоцтво про народження) with its apostille.

- **In Ukraine:** any civil registry office (ДРАЦС) issues it, not only the
  one where the birth was registered, usually the same day. Someone in
  Ukraine can apply for you with a notarized power of attorney. Ukrainian
  citizens can also order it online, through Diia.
- **The apostille:** you can ask for it at any civil registry office too. It
  takes a few working days.
- **From Israel:** through the consular department of the Ukrainian embassy
  in Tel Aviv, by appointment. It can take months. Ukrainian men aged 18 to
  60 need a valid military registration document for consular services.
- **Born in an occupied area:** if the birth record isn't in Ukraine's
  registry, it has to be restored first. That takes up to 3 months in
  Ukraine, or 6 months through a consulate.

### United Kingdom

The full birth certificate, with the parents' details, not the short one.
Every UK birth certificate is a certified copy of the register entry, so a
new one ordered today counts as the original. Order it from the General
Register Office (GRO) if the birth was in England or Wales, from National
Records of Scotland (through ScotlandsPeople) for Scotland, or from the
General Register Office for Northern Ireland (GRONI). All three take orders
online and can send the certificate abroad.

The apostille comes from the Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office
(FCDO), straight on the certificate: no notary or solicitor first. A birth
certificate can't get an e-apostille, only the paper one, sent by post, and
it can take up to 25 working days plus delivery. A certificate issued years
ago can be refused if the registrar's signature can't be checked; a newly
ordered one avoids that.

### United States

A certified copy, from the vital records office of the state of birth. That
state's apostille authority adds the apostille.

## What it should include

- **The original, and 2 copies**.
- **The certification from the country of birth:** an apostille or
  consular legalization.
  - **From the former USSR:** an original issued up to 1998 needs no
    certification (§ד.2.ה). One issued later does. See
    [former-ussr.md](../former-ussr.md).
- **Both sides**, when the back has anything on it. Certificates from the
  former USSR often have stamps on the back, such as a citizenship stamp,
  and the apostille can be placed there too.
- **A certified translation**, unless it's in Hebrew or Arabic. In practice,
  an English certificate is usually accepted without one, but the clerk can
  still ask you to translate it.

## Questions

**My partner was born in the Soviet Union. Which country is the certificate from?**
The country the birthplace is in today. Someone born in Soviet Kyiv was
born in Ukraine, so the certificate is Ukrainian.

**Does a Soviet-era birth certificate need an apostille?**
Not if it's the original and it was issued up to 1998 (§ד.2.ה). A
certificate issued after 1998, including a new copy of an old record, needs
the certification like any other document from abroad. Many people with an
old Soviet certificate (the green booklet) get a new copy (повторное
свидетельство) from the civil registry office (ЗАГС) of the place of birth
anyway, with its apostille: then it's certified like any other document.

**My partner's birth certificate is in English. Do we need to translate it?**
By law, yes: only Hebrew and Arabic are accepted without a translation. In
practice, most clerks accept English, especially a short document like this
one. Be ready to translate it if your clerk asks.

**My partner's birth certificate is multilingual. Does it still need a translation?**
Only if its fields aren't in English. Multilingual extracts, such as
Spain's in Spanish and French, are accepted without translation only in the
countries that signed the convention on them, and Israel didn't: translate
it, and get the apostille, like any other certificate.

**Does the translation also need an apostille?**
No. Only the original certificate is certified.
