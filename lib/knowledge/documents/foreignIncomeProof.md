# Salary slips of the foreign partner (תלושי שכר של בן/בת הזוג הזר/ה)

The last 3-12 salary slips. If self-employed: an accountant's signed confirmation of the income reported for the last year.

The foreign partner's payslips (tlushim, תלושי שכר), if they're employed
(שכיר). If they don't get payslips, they show their income in another way,
depending on their situation (below).

## What it should include

- **The last 3 payslips,** the latest from the last month or two. If you
  can, it's best to bring 6 to 12 months. It isn't a requirement, though.
- **A translation**, if they aren't in Hebrew, Arabic or English. Make it
  yourselves: no notary is needed, unless the office asks (see
  [certification.md](../certification.md)).

## If you don't have it

- **Self-employed (עצמאי):**
  - an accountant's confirmation of the income reported for the last year
    (אישור רואה חשבון על ההכנסות שדווחו בשנה האחרונה);
  - the latest annual tax assessment from the Tax Authority (שומה שנתית
    אחרונה ממס הכנסה);
  - the confirmation of opening a business file, osek murshe or patur
    (אישור פתיחת תיק עוסק מורשה/פטור).
- **Other income** (a scholarship, a National Insurance (Bituach Leumi)
  allowance such as disability, a pension, or any other regular income): a
  confirmation from whoever pays it, for example the university or National
  Insurance, or bank statements showing the deposits. It should show the
  income is current, not one that ended long ago.
- **Not working and no income:** a clear, signed letter explaining their
  situation (for example, a student, between jobs, or a homemaker), stating
  that the Israeli partner financially supports your shared life in Israel.
  With it, one of you shows you can support your life in Israel: a savings
  account, bank statements, or anything else that shows it.

## Questions

**My partner is employed. Can we bring a tax assessment (shuma) instead of payslips?**
No. A shuma is only for the self-employed, never for employees: an employee
brings payslips.

**My partner doesn't work. What do we bring?**
If they have an income anyway, like a scholarship or an allowance, a
confirmation from whoever pays it, or bank statements showing the
deposits. If
they have no income, a clear, signed letter explaining their situation,
stating that the Israeli partner financially supports your shared life in
Israel. With it, one of you shows you can support your life in Israel: a
savings account, bank statements, or anything else that shows it.
