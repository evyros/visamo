# Personal status (מצב אישי)

The documents must show, for the country:

What the foreign partner shows about their civil status (single, divorced,
widowed or married). Every couple needs it, including those married in
Israel. What it has to show depends on your case:

- **Their status now,** issued in the last 6 months (§ד.2.ה). From their
  country, and in practice also from each other country they're a citizen
  of, and each country they lived in for 6 months or more in a row, like the
  police certificate. Married to each other, it's needed even if the marriage
  isn't registered in their country: then it may still say single (see the
  marriage certificate).
- **Married to each other: their status before the marriage** (single,
  divorced or widowed), which shows they were free to marry you. From their
  country. A marriage certificate from their own country that shows their
  status before the marriage covers it.

A divorce decree, or a previous spouse's death certificate, is a document of
its own, for each previous marriage. So is a married foreign partner's
affidavit about their children from previous relationships, or that they
have none.

## How to get it

Many countries don't issue a "civil status certificate" as such. What
counts is an official document from the foreign partner's country that
states their status, or that no marriage is registered for them. Each
country calls it something else, and some have no national document at
all. Below, what the common countries issue.

**Get it before the wedding.** In several countries (Japan, Singapore, the
Czech Republic, the United Kingdom), the document that shows someone is single
can only be had before the marriage is registered, or can't get an apostille
after it. If you plan to marry, get it, with its apostille, first: you also
need it to register the marriage in Israel. If you're already married and
can't get one, use the affidavit (below).

### Argentina

A certificate of single status (certificado de soltería, or constancia
negativa de matrimonio), from the civil registry of the province, in person
by appointment. The apostille comes from the foreign ministry (Cancillería),
online, or in Buenos Aires from the notaries' association (Colegio de
Escribanos).

### Australia

A single status certificate, from the Births, Deaths and Marriages office of
the state. It isn't issued online: in person, by post, or through someone you
authorize. It covers a search period you choose (in New South Wales, 10 or 20
years: choose one back to age 16), and can take about 8 weeks even with
priority service, so order it early. The apostille comes from the Department
of Foreign Affairs and Trade (DFAT), at a passport office in Australia, or at
the Australian embassy in Tel Aviv by appointment.

### Austria

A partial extract on the existence of a marriage or registered partnership
(Teilauszug gemäß §58 PStG), or a civil-status certificate
(Personenstandsurkunde), from the registry office (Standesamt). It can be
issued as an international, multilingual certificate. The apostille comes from
the town hall (Rathaus) in Vienna, or the authority of your region.

### Belgium

An extract from the population register (uittreksel uit het
bevolkingsregister, extrait du registre de la population) that shows the civil
status (burgerlijke staat, état civil), from the municipality where they live,
or online with an e-ID. The apostille comes from the Federal Public Service
Foreign Affairs; some municipalities add it automatically when you order
online.

### Bolivia

A certificate of civil status (certificado de estado civil, or de soltería),
from the civil registry (SERECÍ), in person. It's valid for only 3 months. The
apostille is ordered at SERECÍ and arrives by email.

### Brazil

A negative marriage certificate (certidão negativa de casamento): ask the
registry office (cartório) where the birth was registered for a search of
every registry office in Brazil, from the year of birth. Or a public
declaration of civil status (declaração pública de estado civil), made at the
registry office with two witnesses. A full, updated birth certificate
(certidão de nascimento de inteiro teor) also notes any marriage or divorce,
but it's the birth certificate, not this document. Any registry office puts
the apostille on Brazilian documents.

### Canada

There's no national certificate: marriages are registered by each
province and territory. The province's vital statistics office issues a
marriage search letter, which says whether a marriage is registered there.
It covers only that province.

### Chile

There's no single-status certificate: someone not registered as married or in
a civil union is single, and the civil registry issues nothing to say so. Use
an affidavit of single status (declaración jurada de soltería), made at a
Chilean notary with two witnesses and apostilled. For the status before the
marriage, the affidavit says you were single until the wedding, names your
spouse with their passport or Teudat Zehut number, and is made with the
marriage certificate as proof.

### China

A notarized certificate of unmarried status (未婚公证, or 单身证明), from a
notary office, based on the household register (户口本), which shows the
marital status. It has to be issued in China: a declaration from the Chinese
embassy in Israel has been refused. China joined the Apostille Convention in
November 2023: the document is notarized, then apostilled by the foreign
ministry or a local foreign affairs office.

### Croatia

A certificate of free marital status (potvrda o slobodnom bračnom stanju),
from any registry office (matični ured), in person, usually on the spot. Get
the paper original, not a digital copy from e-Građani. The apostille comes from
the municipal civil court.

### Cuba

A certificate of single status (certificación de soltería), from the civil
registry (Registro Civil): any registry office, or a Cuban consulate for a
Cuban living abroad. In Cuba it's valid for a year, but Misrad Hapnim asks
for one issued in the last 6 months. Divorced, a divorce certificate
(certificación de divorcio), whether the divorce was in court or before a
notary; widowed, a certificate of widowhood (certificación de viudez). A
registered common-law union (unión de hecho afectiva) isn't a civil status
in Cuba: the certificate still says single. To marry abroad, the registry or
a Cuban consulate also issues a certificate of capacity to marry abroad
(certificación de capacidad legal para contraer matrimonio en el
extranjero).

Cuba isn't in the Apostille Convention, and Israel has no embassy in Cuba,
so the legalization goes on through Canada (see
[certification.md](../certification.md)): the Cuban Ministry of Justice
(MINJUS), then the Canadian embassy in Havana, then Global Affairs Canada,
and last the Israeli embassy in Canada. It's filed through an international
law firm in Cuba, such as the CJI, or through a Cuban consulate abroad.

### Czech Republic

There's no civil-status certificate. Bring one of these:

- an extract from the population register (výpis z registru obyvatel) showing
  the civil status (osobní stav), from a city hall's registry (matrika), with
  a stamp that can be apostilled: say it's for use abroad;
- before marrying, the certificate of legal capacity to marry (vysvědčení o
  právní způsobilosti k uzavření manželství), in 4 languages including
  English;
- after marrying, without either of these, a declaration in front of a notary
  in Israel.

Some documents need a higher certification (vyšší ověření) from the regional
office (krajský úřad) before the apostille, which the foreign ministry gives.

### Denmark

A certificate of personal data (personattest), which says unwed or married, or
a residence certificate (bopælsattest) that shows the civil status
(civilstand). Order it in English, with a handwritten signature and the
signer's name: a digitally signed one can't get a physical apostille. The
apostille comes from the Ministry of Foreign Affairs in Copenhagen.

### France

A certificate of civil status (certificat de célibat), from the town hall
(mairie) of the place of birth, against a full copy of the birth certificate
issued in the last 3 months. Few town halls issue it today, so ask for it
together with the birth certificate. The full copy of the birth certificate
(copie intégrale) also shows any marriage or divorce in its margin. Since
May 2025, the apostille comes from the notaries' apostille centres, not the
court of appeal: apply online and send the paper certificate by post, or go
to a centre in person.

### Germany

An extended registration certificate (erweiterte Meldebescheinigung) that
includes the marital status (Familienstand), from the citizens' office
(Bürgeramt) of the town where they're registered. The basic certificate
doesn't show the marital status: ask for it to be included. It's issued in
German only.

**After deregistering (Abmeldung):** a German who no longer lives in Germany
gets it by post from the office of the last town they were registered in. Ask
for it to include the date they moved out and the address abroad. Germany
keeps no register of Germans living abroad, so it shows their marital status
only as of the day they left. Order it now, with a new apostille, and add an
affidavit about their status since then (see "If you don't have it", below).

### Hungary

A certificate of civil status (családi állapot igazolás), from the district
government office (kormányhivatal), online through Ügyfélkapu, or at the
Hungarian embassy. It shows only the current status: for the status before
the marriage, ask the clerk to write it in. The apostille comes from the
Ministry of Foreign Affairs, or the embassy.

### Iceland

A certificate of marital history (hjúskaparsöguvottorð), from Registers
Iceland (Þjóðskrá). Order it in English, and have it delivered on paper: don't
print it at home. The apostille comes from the Ministry for Foreign Affairs,
which can also send it to Israel.

### India

There's no national certificate. Use the affidavit (below).

### Italy

A certificate of free status (certificato di stato libero), from the town
hall (comune) where they're registered, or from the Italian consulate for
Italians registered abroad (AIRE). The apostille comes from the prefecture
(prefettura) of the province that issued it.

### Japan

A single status certificate (独身証明書), from the municipality where the
family register (戸籍) is kept. It can only be had, and apostilled, before the
marriage is registered in Japan. After the marriage, the family register
(戸籍全部事項証明) shows the status. The apostille comes from the Ministry of
Foreign Affairs, by post.

### Mexico

A certificate that no marriage is registered (constancia de inexistencia de
registro de matrimonio), or of single status (constancia de soltería), from
the civil registry of the state. Say it's for an apostille. Each state sets its
own requirements, and its validity can be as short as 30 days. The apostille
comes from the same state.

### Nepal

An unmarried certificate (अविवाहित प्रमाणित, abivahit pramanit), from the ward
office of the municipality or rural municipality.

Nepal isn't in the Apostille Convention, so it's legalized (see
[certification.md](../certification.md)): the foreign ministry's Department
of Consular Services in Tripureshwor, Kathmandu, then the Israeli embassy in
Kathmandu. Before the foreign ministry, the local government that issued it
has its chief administrative officer, or the head of its registration
section, verify the signature: the foreign ministry certifies a local
document only then. The Israeli embassy's legalization is filed through VFS
in Kathmandu: you hand in the original there, with the fee, and don't visit
the embassy.

### Netherlands

An extract from the population register (uittreksel BRP) that includes the
marital status (burgerlijke staat), from the municipality where they're
registered. Someone who has left the Netherlands gets an extract from the
non-residents' register (RNI) instead. Any court (rechtbank) adds the
apostille, without an appointment.

### Peru

A certificate that no marriage is registered (constancia negativa de
inscripción de matrimonio), from RENIEC, the national registry: it's what
Peru issues when a certificate of single status (constancia de soltería) is
asked for. It covers the records in RENIEC's system. Ask for it at a RENIEC
office or a MAC center, on paper: it can also be requested online, but then
it comes by email. The apostille comes from the foreign ministry
(Cancillería).

### Philippines

A certificate of no marriage (CENOMAR), from the Philippine Statistics
Authority (PSA). The apostille comes from the Department of Foreign Affairs.
For Israel, it now issues only an electronic one: the certificate and its
apostille come as files by email, and printing them makes them invalid.
Misrad Hapnim usually asks for the original on paper, so the clerk may push
back on the files. Explain that for Israel, the Philippines issues it only
this way: there's no paper option.

### Portugal

Portugal issues no civil-status certificate. Go to a notary for a certificate
of life and identity (certificado de vida e identidade), and make sure it says
single (solteiro/a). A narrative birth certificate (certidão de nascimento
narrativa) records any marriage or divorce, but doesn't say "single" in words,
and Misrad Hapnim (משרד הפנים) may not accept it as proof of status. The apostille comes from
the regional prosecutor's office (Procuradoria-Geral Regional).

### Russia

A certificate of no registered marriage (справка об отсутствии факта
государственной регистрации заключения брака, form 15), from any civil
registry office (ЗАГС) in Russia, through Gosuslugi, or at a Russian
consulate. At the registry office in person, it's usually issued the same
day. The apostille is put on it at the civil registry office.

### Singapore

There's no certificate. Before marrying, search the Registry of Marriages
online: it shows "no marriage record found". Once married, or for the status
before the marriage, use a lawyer's affidavit of single status. The apostille
comes from the Singapore Academy of Law.

### Slovenia

A certificate from the civil register showing the status as single (potrdilo
o vpisu v matični register kot samski), from the administrative unit (upravna
enota), on the spot. The apostille comes from the district court.

### South Africa

A letter of non-impediment, from the Department of Home Affairs, at a branch in
South Africa. It can take about 6 months, and is valid for 6 months. Check
first that Home Affairs records the right status. Instead, many use an
affidavit made in front of a South African notary, apostilled. The apostille
comes from the Department of International Relations (DIRCO) in Pretoria, or
the High Court for a notary's document.

### Spain

A certificate of life and civil status (certificado de fe de vida y
estado), from the civil registry (Registro Civil). It's issued in person. The
apostille can be done on the spot when you collect the police certificate
(see it): bring this one with you.

### Sweden

An extract from the population register (utdrag om folkbokföring), in
English, "with all relations" (med alla relationer), from the Tax Agency
(Skatteverket). It serves as the birth certificate and this document at once.
Any notary public (notarius publicus) adds the apostille.

### Switzerland

A certificate of civil status (Personenstandsausweis, certificat individuel
d'état civil), from the civil registry office (Zivilstandsamt) of the place of
origin (Heimatort); for a foreign national, of the place of residence. It's in
5 languages, English included. The office can add the apostille; otherwise the
cantonal chancellery (Staatskanzlei) or the Federal Chancellery does.

### Tanzania

A certificate of single status (also called a letter of no marriage), from
the Registrar General of Marriages at RITA, the Registration, Insolvency and
Trusteeship Agency. A Tanzanian citizen applies for it online, through eRITA,
and it costs 200,000 shillings. For someone from Zanzibar, the registrar of
marriages is the Zanzibar Civil Status Registration Agency (ZCSRA).

Tanzania isn't in the Apostille Convention, so it's legalized (see
[certification.md](../certification.md)): Tanzania's Ministry of Foreign
Affairs and East African Cooperation, then the Israeli embassy. Before the
ministry, the office that issued the document verifies it (RITA or ZCSRA): the ministry
certifies a document only after that. Israel has no embassy in Tanzania: its
embassy in Nairobi, Kenya, also serves Tanzania, and the chain ends there.
Hand in the original at the embassy in Nairobi, by appointment; someone else
can hand it in for you.

### Thailand

A certificate of marital status (หนังสือรับรองสถานภาพการสมรส, also called
ใบรับรองโสด), from the district office (สำนักงานเขต or ที่ว่าการอำเภอ). It's
issued only in Thailand: a Thai living abroad can authorize someone there to
get it, by signing the authorization at a Thai embassy. Thailand isn't in
the Apostille Convention, so it's translated and certified by the Consular
Affairs Department of the Thai Ministry of Foreign Affairs, then by the
Israeli embassy.

### Turkmenistan

A certificate of marital status (справка о семейном положении), on whether
a marriage was registered while living in Turkmenistan. From abroad, it's
asked for through a Turkmen embassy or consulate, in person or with a
notarized power of attorney, with the birth certificate and any marriage or
divorce certificate. It takes 2 to 3 months, and until it's issued, the
person it's for mustn't travel to Turkmenistan.

Turkmenistan isn't in the Apostille Convention, so it's legalized (see
[certification.md](../certification.md)), ending at the Israeli embassy in
Ashgabat.

### Ukraine

An extract from the State Register of Civil Status Acts (витяг з Державного
реєстру актів цивільного стану громадян) showing that no marriage record
exists, from any civil registry office, wherever they live, or at a
Ukrainian consulate.

### United Kingdom

There's no national certificate of civil status. The only UK document that
says someone is free to marry is a certificate of no impediment (CNI), and
it's issued only for a marriage being planned abroad: you give notice of the
marriage at your local register office, the notice is posted for a month, and
then the certificate is issued. If you plan to marry abroad, get it first.
Otherwise, use the affidavit (below).

### United States

There's no national certificate: marriages are registered by each state
and county. Ask the vital records office of the state you live in for a
letter of single status (also called an affidavit of single status): a letter
on its letterhead saying a search found no marriage record for you in that
state. Not every office knows the document, so explain what you need. That
state's apostille authority (usually the Secretary of State) adds the
apostille. If you can't get one, use the affidavit (below).

## What it should include

- **Issued in the last 6 months.** Counted from the date it was issued, or
  from the date an affidavit was signed. Some countries set a shorter validity
  of their own (Bolivia, some Mexican states).
- **For married couples:** the status before the marriage, and the status now
  (above).
- **The certification:** an apostille, or consular legalization if the
  country isn't in the Apostille Convention (see
  [certification.md](../certification.md)).
- **A notarized translation (תרגום נוטריוני) into Hebrew**, by a notary in
  Israel, unless it's in Hebrew or Arabic: a translator's translation, even a
  certified one, will be rejected. It's an official document, unlike bills, letters or bank
  statements, which you can translate yourselves. In practice,
  an English document is usually accepted without one, but the clerk can
  still ask you to translate it.

## If you don't have it

- **If there's no way to get one from the country:** a notarized or consular
  affidavit (תצהיר) about their status. It can be signed in front of a notary
  in Israel, an Israeli consul abroad, their country's consul in Israel, or a
  notary in their country, with that country's apostille (see
  [certification.md](../certification.md)). By the procedure, the office
  doesn't have to accept an affidavit if the country does issue a document:
  the office knows which countries do.
- **If the country can only show their status up to a past date:** for
  example, they deregistered or moved away, and the country's register
  stopped following their status then. Bring the strongest official document
  the country issues anyway, even though it shows their status only up to
  that date. It's still issued in the last 6 months, with a new apostille.
  Add an affidavit (תצהיר) about their status from that date until now: the
  document covers the years it can, and the affidavit covers the rest. Here
  the affidavit adds to the document; it doesn't replace it.
- **One affidavit for everything:** if the foreign partner can't get one of the
  documents, one affidavit can declare it all: their status now, for a married
  couple their status before the marriage, and their children.
- **Declare the children in it too.** Whenever the foreign partner makes an
  affidavit about their status, it also lists their children from previous
  relationships, or states that they have none. A married foreign partner
  declares their children in an affidavit anyway. A common-law couple doesn't
  have to bring one, but in practice the office can still ask about children
  later in the process: in the same affidavit it costs nothing more, and
  answers that question before it's asked.
- **If your marriage certificate is from the foreign partner's country** and
  shows their status before the marriage, it can replace the document of their
  status before the marriage.

**The statement.** For a foreign partner who isn't married. Fill in the
details in square brackets, and leave the signature empty until they're in
front of the lawyer or notary. Line c is for a country that can only show
their status up to a past date: leave it out if the country issues nothing.
Divorced or widowed, replace line b with the date the last marriage ended
(the divorce decree or death certificate is still a document of its own).
With children, replace line d with each child: their full name, date of
birth, and who has guardianship of them. Married, the affidavit about
children from previous relationships has the statement, and it declares the
marriage too.

Statement

I the undersigned [Title] [Full Name] holder of [Country] Passport number [Passport Number], have been instructed to tell the entire truth and nothing but the truth and should I fail to do so, will be subject to penalties applicable by law, and hereby declare the following:

a. I was born on [Date of Birth] in [Place of Birth, Country].

b. I have never been married, and I am not married now.

c. On [Date] I moved from [Country] to [Country], and I have lived there since. My civil status until that date is shown in the [Document's Name] issued by [Authority] on [Date of Issue]. Since that date and until today, I have not married anyone, in any country.

## Questions

**My partner's country doesn't issue a civil-status certificate. What do we bring?**
First, look for what the country does issue: an extract from its
population or civil register, or a record search showing no marriage is
registered (see the countries above). If there's no way to get one, the
foreign partner signs a notarized or consular affidavit about their status.

**We're married. Why two documents about the status?**
One shows the foreign partner was free to marry you; the other shows their
status now. If they can't get one of them, a single affidavit can declare
both, and their children too.

**Can the embassy of my partner's country in Israel issue it?**
Some do, but offices have refused documents issued by a foreign embassy in
Israel and asked for ones from the country itself. Prefer a document from
the country.

**My partner was married before. Does this replace the divorce decree?**
No. The divorce decree, or the previous spouse's death certificate, is a
document of its own. This one shows their status now.

**Does it need to be issued in the last 6 months even if nothing changed?**
Yes. The status is checked as of now, so an older document isn't
accepted, even if it's otherwise correct. The document of the status before
a marriage is the exception: it shows the status at that time. If the
country can only show the status up to a past date, the document is still
issued in the last 6 months, and an affidavit covers the time since.

**I left my country years ago, and it can only confirm my status up to then. What do I bring?**
The strongest document your country issues, issued in the last 6 months
with a new apostille, even though it shows your status only up to the day
you left. Add an affidavit about your status from that day until now, which
also declares your children, or that you have none (see the statement
above). A German who deregistered gets the extended registration certificate
from the last town they were registered in (see Germany, above).
