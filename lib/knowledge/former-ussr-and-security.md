# The former USSR, and the security check

## The former USSR

The 15 former Soviet republics: Armenia, Azerbaijan, Belarus, Estonia,
Georgia, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Latvia, Lithuania, Moldova, Russia,
Tajikistan, Turkmenistan, Ukraine and Uzbekistan.

- **Citizens of these countries, and people born in them, are referred to
  Nativ** (לשכת הקשר נתיב) for an opinion (5.2.0008 §ד.4). You don't bring a
  document for it, but you take part: a questionnaire and an interview
  (below).
- **Original birth certificates from the former USSR issued up to 1998 need
  no authentication** (§ד.2.ה).
- Country names follow today's borders: someone born in Soviet Kyiv was born
  in Ukraine.
- An Israeli partner who immigrated or became a citizen in the last 10 years
  is checked with Nativ (if from the former USSR) or the Jewish Agency
  (elsewhere) (§ה.2(3)). Again, the office does this.

### The Nativ step

Nativ is a government office, part of the Prime Minister's Office, whose
main role is examining immigration (aliyah) applications from the former
USSR. It's the authority on Soviet and post-Soviet records and documents.

- **When:** for common-law couples, it comes before the B/1 visa. Married
  couples get the B/1 visa first, but have to complete this step before they
  can move up to A/5 (temporary resident).
- **How:** once you're both in Israel, Misrad Hapnim usually tells you to
  fill in an online Nativ questionnaire. After you send it, Nativ calls you
  both to a short interview at its offices in Jerusalem.
- **The wait:** waiting times for a Nativ interview are long. If you haven't
  heard back from Nativ within a month, contact them and ask about the
  status of your application.

## The security check

- Applicants who need a security check fill in a **full CV form** (§ד.2.ט).
  It's not a job CV: it's a history of your employment and life, for
  security clearance.
- The procedure refers to "risk countries" without listing them. In
  practice, this applies to people from sensitive countries such as
  **Russia, Ukraine and the Arab countries**, by citizenship or by country of
  birth.
