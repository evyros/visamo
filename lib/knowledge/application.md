# Filing the application

You file the application online, with all the documents. Misrad Hapnim (משרד הפנים)
reviews it, asks for anything missing, and calls you to a first appointment
(see [first-appointment.md](first-appointment.md)). If you both live abroad,
you start at the consulate instead, and file online after you arrive (see
[both-abroad.md](both-abroad.md)).

## Before you file

- **Get the documents from abroad first.** They take the longest, and some
  branches reject an application that's missing them instead of giving you
  time to complete it. Apply when the file is complete.
- **The foreign partner's passport** has to be valid for at least 2 more years
  (see the foreign passport).
- **Update the Israeli partner's address.** The application goes
  automatically to the branch of the address registered in their Teudat
  Zehut. If you moved, update the address (שינוי כתובת) before you file, or
  the file goes to the wrong branch.

## The online form

The application is on the government's service page (see
[sources.md](sources.md)).

1. Choose the type: **married**, or **shared life** (חיים משותפים) for a
   common-law couple. Married couples then choose a marriage to a foreigner,
   a same-sex marriage, or a marriage in El Salvador.
2. **The Israeli partner logs in** with the national identification system
   (gov.il). If they aren't registered to it yet, they register first. Their
   details and their branch are filled in automatically.
3. Fill in the form and upload the documents, each in its field. Forms AS/1,
   AS/3 and AS/6 can be downloaded inside the form. Passport photos go in the
   field for frontal photos of the couple (תמונה חזותית של בני הזוג).
   The form also asks for form MR/6, the notice of change in civil status.
   If the Israeli partner is already registered as married, upload their
   appendix (ספח) that shows it instead.
4. You can save the form and come back to it: you get a link by SMS or email.
5. **Pay the fee** for opening the file at the end. In 2026 it's 1,085 NIS,
   for married and common-law couples alike.

**Uploading:** PDF or JPEG only, one file per field. To put several
documents in one field, merge them into one PDF. All the files together have
to stay under about 40 MB: compress large photos and scans.

After you pay, you get a confirmation by email with a reference number. Use
it, with your ID or passport number, whenever you contact the office.

**If you can't afford the fee:** in extreme cases you can ask for an
exemption under procedure 5.1.0003, in person at the branch, with full proof
of your financial situation.

## After you file

- **A foreign partner abroad waits abroad.** From the moment you file, they're
  likely to be refused entry if they try to visit as a tourist, even from a
  visa-exempt country: they're marked as someone waiting for an entry permit.
  They come once the permit is granted (see
  [entry-permit.md](entry-permit.md)).
- **A foreign partner in Israel stays in Israel.** They can leave, but they
  won't be let back in until the entry permit is granted, unless they have a
  valid visa to return on.
- **The Israeli partner should be in Israel.** The office is unlikely to move
  the file forward while the Israeli partner is abroad for long.

## Waiting for an answer

Misrad Hapnim has no deadline for answering. As of 2026, the first answer
takes from 3 weeks to more than 4 months, depending on the branch, and the
appointment it gives may be months later.

- **No answer after 3 weeks:** email your branch with both your names, your
  ID and passport numbers, the reference number, and a short question. Many
  branches don't answer emails; then go in person, at the walk-in hours.
- **The call center (\*3450)** doesn't book visa appointments, and its staff
  aren't trained on visas: don't rely on what it tells you about the process.

The answer is one of two things:

- **A request for more documents,** usually by email. Send them by email to
  your branch, with the files attached, unless they tell you otherwise.
- **A first appointment,** sometimes with a list of documents to bring.

## Missing documents: the 45 days

If a document, a translation or a certification is missing, the office tells
you which, by email and by registered mail, and gives you **45 days**
(calendar days) to bring it. You can ask for more time. Until it's in, the
file doesn't move; if it doesn't come, the office can close the file.

**If the office closes the file:** it sends a letter saying why. You have
**21 days** to object to it in writing, for example when it says a document
is missing that you already sent. Keep the confirmation of every document you
send, and copies of the files, so you can show what you sent and when.

**When a document can't be had at all:** since July 2026, the regional
director can let you start the process without it, in exceptional cases. You
ask in writing, in detail, and show that exceptional circumstances kept you
from getting it, that you really tried, and official proof of both. Asking
for asylum isn't such a circumstance on its own. It lets you start: you still
have to bring the document before the process ends.
