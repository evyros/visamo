# Which languages notaries in Israel translate

A document from abroad that isn't in Hebrew, Arabic or English is translated
by a notary in Israel, who knows both its language and Hebrew, or, only for a
language no notary in Israel knows, abroad into English (the rules are in
[certification.md](certification.md)). Which one applies depends on the
document's language, not on the country that issued it: a Swiss document in
German follows the rule for German.

## Notaries in Israel translate it

For these languages, a notarized translation (תרגום נוטריוני) into Hebrew,
by a notary in Israel, is required. A translation made abroad, even by a
notary and with an apostille of its own, will be rejected.

- **Many notaries:** Russian, Ukrainian, Belarusian, French, Spanish,
  Portuguese, Italian, German, Dutch, Yiddish, Romanian (and Moldovan, which
  is the same language), Polish, Bulgarian, Georgian.
- **A few notaries,** so you may have to travel to one: Hungarian, Greek,
  Czech, Lithuanian, Persian (Farsi), Amharic, Chinese, Afrikaans, Turkish,
  Azerbaijani, Uzbek.
- **Only one or two notaries in the whole country,** so contact them early:
  Slovak, Serbian, Croatian, Bosnian, Latvian, Kazakh, Tigrinya, Swedish,
  Danish, Norwegian, Finnish, Icelandic, Lingala.

To find one, search the Ministry of Justice's notary register (מאגר
נוטריונים, on gov.il) by the language: it shows each notary's city.

## Any other language

No notary in Israel translates it. The translation is made abroad, into
English, and certified on its own, as [certification.md](certification.md)
explains.
