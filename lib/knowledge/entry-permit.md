# The entry permit

Before the foreign partner moves to Israel, they need an entry permit (היתר
כניסה): Misrad Hapnim's approval to come to Israel and continue the process
here. People often call it the "hazmana" (הזמנה, the invitation), and also
the "asmachta" (אסמכתא). If the foreign partner is already in Israel, the
process starts there instead: see [first-appointment.md](first-appointment.md).

This is for when you mean to start the process. For a visit only, where the
foreign partner goes home at the end of it, see [visiting.md](visiting.md).

## Two ways to get it

- **The Israeli partner lives in Israel:** you file the application online,
  with the entry permit application (form AS/1), and the Israeli partner
  goes to the first appointment alone. The branch grants the permit at the
  appointment, or by phone or email within a few days to a few weeks. See
  [application.md](application.md) and
  [first-appointment.md](first-appointment.md).
- **You both live abroad:** you ask for it at the Israeli consulate in your
  country, before anything is filed in Israel, and you file after you
  arrive. See [both-abroad.md](both-abroad.md).

## Coming as a tourist and filing here

The foreign partner can come to Israel as a tourist when the purpose is a
visit (see [visiting.md](visiting.md)). If the purpose is to stay and live
together in Israel, the recommended way is the entry permit: you ask for it
while the foreign partner waits abroad, and they come once it's granted.

Being apart for months while you wait is hard, and many couples skip it: the
foreign partner comes to Israel as a tourist, and you start the process once
they're here. It's possible, but less recommended: the process expects a
foreign partner who lives abroad to wait abroad until the entry permit is
granted, and the office may look more closely at your case (below).

In practice, a foreign partner who comes alone as a tourist, with a return
ticket, is usually let in at border control. Border control looks at their
previous visits (how often they came and how long they stayed), their return
ticket and their hotel booking. If it suspects they came to stay in Israel,
or to start the application here, they're refused entry (see "If entry is
refused at the border" in [visiting.md](visiting.md)).

Never come on a tourist invitation (Misrad Hapnim's approval of a visit in
advance) to start the process: see "Never start the process after a tourist
invitation" in [visiting.md](visiting.md).

- **If you both live abroad:** don't fly in together as tourists. In
  practice, a foreign partner who arrives with the Israeli partner without
  the permit is likely to be suspected of coming to stay, stopped at border
  control, and flown back.
- **Once you've filed:** the foreign partner can't come to Israel before
  the entry permit is granted, not even for a visit. They're likely to be
  refused entry at the border, and trying can also harm your application:
  see [application.md](application.md).

### If the foreign partner lives abroad but is in Israel as a tourist

A couple can still file while the foreign partner is in Israel on a tourist
visa (except when they came on a tourist invitation). File before it
expires. Misrad Hapnim should extend their visa while
the application is processed: ask for it at the branch's walk-in hours,
early in the morning. See "On another visa" in
[first-appointment.md](first-appointment.md).

However, the office always sees it as a problem that you didn't go through
the formal procedure. There are no formal consequences, but it may look more
closely at your case. If they came on a tourist invitation, don't file
during that visit: see "Never start the process after a tourist invitation"
in [visiting.md](visiting.md).

This is only before you've filed: once you have, the foreign partner can't
come until the entry permit is granted (above).

## How long it takes

**If the Israeli partner lives in Israel:** from filing until the permit,
it depends on your branch's backlog, how complete your file is, and the
foreign partner's country. In practice, the faster branches grant it usually
within 3 to 4 weeks, and busy branches take 2 to 5 months. It can't be
predicted: plan for a few months.

**Do we have to wait apart all that time?** It's hard to spend months apart,
waiting for an answer. In practice, if your relationship rests on strong
ground, with proof that you've lived together abroad (a short-term lease,
shared expenses and the like), you can try to speed it up:

1. Instead of filing online first, the Israeli partner goes to the branch
   without an appointment. Come early in the morning, before it opens.
2. Explain your situation, and bring all the proof with you.
3. Ask them to grant the entry permit, or even a B/1 visa (ב/1), so the
   foreign partner can come to Israel and continue the process here.

The branch may refuse, and tell you to file online and go through the usual
process.

## Traveling with the permit

**If the permit came from your branch:**

- **Citizens of a country exempt from a visa to Israel** can fly as soon as
  they have the permit, with an ETA-IL (the electronic travel authorization
  every visa-exempt visitor needs). In the ETA-IL application, choose
  "Visit (tourism)" as the purpose and "up to 90 days" as the length of stay:
  the Population and Immigration Authority confirmed this for partners with an
  entry permit. In practice, no written proof of the permit is needed at the
  border.
- **Citizens of a country that needs a visa to Israel** go to the Israeli
  consulate where they live, to get a B/2 visa (ב/2) in their passport.
- **When to come:** the procedures set no deadline, but offices usually
  expect the foreign partner to enter within 3 months of the permit. Tell
  your clerk the date you plan to arrive, and ask them to confirm it.

**If you both live abroad:** the consulate puts the entry visa in the
foreign partner's passport: see [both-abroad.md](both-abroad.md).

With the entry permit, airlines let the foreign partner board, even on a
one-way ticket.

## After the foreign partner arrives

- **If the permit came from your branch:** they enter Israel on a B/2 visa
  (ב/2), as a status in between. Tell your branch as soon as they arrive, by
  email or in person. The office then calls you in to get the B/1 visa
  (ב/1), or sets a date for an interview. An interview is usually set only
  once the foreign partner is in Israel.
- **If you both live abroad:** you file the full application online after
  you arrive: see [both-abroad.md](both-abroad.md).
