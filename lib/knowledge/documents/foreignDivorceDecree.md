# Divorce decree (פסק דין לגירושין)

The final divorce decree of each of the foreign partner's previous marriages that ended in divorce.

For each of the foreign partner's previous marriages that ended in divorce:
the final divorce decree, which shows the divorce is complete and can no
longer be appealed. It's asked for as well as the proof of civil status:
that one shows their status, and the decree is the proof of each divorce
itself.

## How to get it

It comes from the court or authority that granted the divorce, in the
country where it was granted. That isn't always the foreign partner's
country: a divorce granted elsewhere comes from there, and is certified
there. Below, what the common countries issue.

### France

The divorce judgment, from the court that granted it, with a certificate of
non-appeal (certificat de non-appel) from the court of appeal's registry,
showing no appeal was filed in time. The marriage certificate with the
divorce noted in its margin also shows the divorce.

### Germany

A certified copy (Ausfertigung) of the divorce decree (Scheidungsbeschluss),
from the family court (Familiengericht), with the note that it's final
(Rechtskraftvermerk). The court issues a new copy from its file if the old
one is lost.

### Philippines

There's no divorce in Philippine law. A marriage ends there by annulment or
a declaration of nullity: the court decision, with its certificate of
finality, and the marriage certificate from the Philippine Statistics
Authority (PSA), annotated with it. In the Philippines, a divorce granted
abroad counts only once a Philippine court recognizes it and the marriage
certificate is annotated.

### Russia

A divorce certificate (свидетельство о расторжении брака), from the civil
registry office (ЗАГС). A divorce granted by a court is registered there too,
and the certificate is issued after it.

### Ukraine

A divorce certificate (свідоцтво про розірвання шлюбу), from the civil
registry office, for a divorce registered there. A divorce granted by a
court is proven by the court's decision, once it has come into force.

### United Kingdom

The final order, called the decree absolute for divorces before April 2022,
from the court. It's the last stage: the conditional order (decree nisi)
doesn't end the marriage. For an apostille, it needs the court's original
seal; a printout or photocopy is first certified by a notary or solicitor.

### United States

A certified copy of the final divorce decree (the judgment), from the clerk
of the court in the county where it was granted, with the clerk's signature
or seal. A photocopy or a printout can't get an apostille. In some states,
the county certifies the clerk's signature before the state's apostille.

## What it should include

- **One for each divorce.**
- **The final decree:** the document that ends the marriage, not a step on
  the way. Where the country notes it separately, its proof that the
  decree is final (a note of finality, a certificate of non-appeal).
- **The certification** of the country where the divorce was granted: an
  apostille, or consular legalization if that country isn't in the
  Apostille Convention (see [certification.md](../certification.md)).
- **A certified translation**, unless it's in Hebrew or Arabic. In practice,
  an English document is usually accepted without one, but the clerk can
  still ask you to translate it.

## Questions

**My partner divorced in a different country from their nationality. Where does the decree come from?**
From the country where the divorce was granted, and it's certified there,
not in the country of their nationality.

**My partner was divorced more than once. Do we bring all of them?**
Yes: a decree for each previous marriage that ended in divorce.

**Doesn't the proof of civil status already show they're divorced?**
Not enough. At most it says "divorced": not which marriage ended, when, or
that the divorce is final. In many countries it only says that no marriage
is registered, and it covers only that country's records, so a divorce
granted elsewhere doesn't show on it at all. The decree is the proof of
each divorce itself.

**We lost the divorce decree. What now?**
Ask the court that granted the divorce for a new certified copy. Courts
keep the file, and in the US and Germany, for example, they issue a new
copy from it.
