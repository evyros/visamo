# Israeli Citizenship for Partners of Israelis: Eligibility and Application

Getting Israeli citizenship is the FINAL step in the gradual partner visa process. If you’ve made it here, you and your partner have already spent years building your life in Israel with a partner visa – now, you’re in the home stretch!

**Not there yet?** Check out our [step-by-step guide to getting your first partner visa](https://aic.org.il/partner-visa-in-israel-step-by-step-guide-to-your-first-visa/) and come back to this article when the time is right. 🙂

In **all international couples** who complete the gradual process, the non-Israeli partner can naturalize and become an Israeli citizen. This article explains who qualifies, when they qualify, and the steps required to apply for and obtain Israeli citizenship.

The eligibility rules and application procedures vary for heterosexual married couples, same-sex married couples, and unmarried couples. Despite these differences, **all partners of Israelis can eventually obtain Israeli citizenship**.

If you do not wish to naturalize and become an Israeli citizen, you have the option to apply for permanent residency instead.

This article is based on procedures [5.2.0008](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/policy/status_foreigner_married_to_israeli_procedure/he/5.2.0008.pdf) + [4.4.0001](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/policy/citizenship_request_procedure/he/4.4.0001.pdf) for heterosexual married couples, and on procedures [5.2.0009](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/policy/israelis_couples_status_procedure/he/5.2.0009.pdf) + [4.4.0001](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/policy/citizenship_request_procedure/he/4.4.0001.pdf) for homosexual married couples and non-married couples. We encourage you to read the relevant procedures fully for better understanding.

## Eligibility for Israeli Citizenship

The eligibility rules below are specific for international couples in Israel who are undergoing the gradual process (partner visa/spouse visa procedures). This article does not include information about citizenship eligibility in other cases!

### Time Requirements

Heterosexual married couples:

- Can **apply** for Israeli citizenship after completing 3 years and 9 months with an A5 visa (temporary resident) that was obtained in the family reunification gradual process. Meaning, contact PIBA 3 months before completing the required 4 years period on an A5 visa.
- OR can apply for Israeli citizenship after obtaining a Permanent Resident status.
- Required stay in Israel of at least 3 years out of the 4 years that preceded the application.
- Required continuous stay in Israel in the 2 years that preceded the application (other than “short vacations”).

Homosexual married couples and unmarried couples:

- Can apply for Israeli citizenship after getting a permanent resident status – Once having the permanent residency, [the citizenship application can happen immediately without any waiting period](https://aic.org.il/aic-wins-piba-exempts-couples-from-1-year-delay-to-citizenship/).
- Required stay in Israel of at least 3 years out of the 5 years that preceded the application.

**Pay Attention!** The time requirements are the strictest requirements for citizenship and they mainly affect the first group. You can see the official time criteria above, but many branches would go above and beyond with delaying your application, mostly due to long stays abroad. Some branches would even “count” the days you spent abroad in the last 4 years and refuse to schedule a citizenship meeting before you “complete” those days in Israel. This is against the procedure, as short vacations are allowed.

### Additional Requirements

Heterosexual married couples:

- Physically staying in Israel
- Settled in Israel
- Lack of criminal/security prevention

Homosexual married couples and unmarried couples:

- Hold a Permanent Resident status
- Physically staying in Israel
- Settled in Israel
- Lack of criminal/security prevention
- Eligible to stay in Israel as a permanent resident
- “Some” knowledge of Hebrew – checked by a short, informal small talk, while submitting the application (basic knowledge is enough). **Married** couples are exempt from this requirement according to [procedure 4.4.0004](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/policy/citizenship_request_for_permanent_residents_with_israel_citizen/he/4.4.0004.pdf).

The slight differences in requirements result from the different sections of the [Citizenship Law](https://www.nevo.co.il/law_html/law00/84250.htm) under which the application is submitted. The first group applies under Section 7, while the second group applies under Section 5. These differences are minimal, other than the requirement to hold Permanent Resident status.

### Important Information About Eligibility

- Partners of Israelis (both married and unmarried) are categorically exempt from the requirement to renounce their other citizenship(s) when applying for Israeli citizenship (it is still important to check the policy of the other country about dual citizenship).
- A person who previously had a permanent residency status or Israeli citizenship revoked or previously received such status fraudulently – **cannot** apply for Israeli citizenship.
- If there is any doubt about the sincerity of the relationship or the conditions detailed above are not met, this may delay the application process for citizenship or result in the denial of the application.

**Not all countries allow dual or multiple citizenships**. For partners of Israelis, the prevention is not on Israel’s side. Check the policy of your other countries of citizenship before applying

## Breakdown of Steps

1. Schedule a Meeting
2. Citizenship Application Meeting
3. Receive An Answer
4. Receive Citizenship
5. Apply for an Israeli Passport

### Step 1: Schedule Your Citizenship Application Meeting

#### Heterosexual married couples

3 months before completing 4 years with an A5 visa, contact your branch via the [online visa appointment scheduling form](https://aic.org.il/how-to-book-an-appointment-to-piba-visa-department/) (not to be confused with the online application form).

1. Fill in your personal details in Step 1.
2. In Step 2, choose “citizenship application” – **בקשה לאזרחות**.
3. Choose “Citizenship application for partners (after completing the partner status process / changing from permanent residency to citizenship” – “**בקשה להתאזרחות לבני זוג (בסיום הליך של קבלת מעמד עקב זוגיות/ שינוי מעמד מקבע לאזרח לבן זוג לאזרח ישראלי)**“.
4. The PIBA office will be automatically determined based on the city you entered in Step 1.
5. In the details, you can write the following text:

> על פי סעיף ז.1 לנוהל 5.2.0008, עליי להודיע על בקשתי להתאזרחות עד 3 חודשים לפני השלמת 4 שנים במעמד תושב ארעי. מכיוון שאשלים 4 שנים במעמד תושב ארעי בחודש \_\_\_\_\_, אני מבקש/ת כעת לתאם תור להגשת בקשה להתאזרחות בהתאם לנוהל. בתודה מראש,

In addition to scheduling your citizenship meeting, pay attention to the expiration date of your A5 visa. If the visa is close to expiring, it is important to continue renewing it, even while your citizenship application is in progress. The renewal can be done during the same meeting as your citizenship application or separately, depending on the discretion of the attending branch

#### Homosexual married couples and non-married couples (permanent residents)

There are a few options for the scheduling:

1. If you already have permanent residency – follow the appointment booking procedure above (do not include the above-suggested text).
2. During the meeting of getting your permanent residency, ask to schedule your citizenship application meeting.
3. Ask in advance to apply for citizenship in the same meeting when you are scheduled to get your permanent residency (this should be allowed for homosexual married couples, in accordance with the verdict in supreme court petition [בג”ץ 5075/15 עמותת אבות גאים נ’ שר הפנים](https://aic.org.il/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/%D7%91%D7%92%D7%A5-%D7%A2%D7%9E%D7%95%D7%AA%D7%AA-%D7%90%D7%91%D7%95%D7%AA-%D7%92%D7%90%D7%99%D7%9D.pdf)).

### Step 2: Citizenship Application Meeting(s)

You’ve made it!

You will need to attend the citizenship application meeting together, as you have in previous years, where an up-to-date and thorough examination of your relationship will take place.

#### Required Documents

The citizenship application requires special documentation, one or more of the documents are **affidavits that have to be signed by a lawyer in advance**.

Contact PIBA for clear instructions about the necessary documentation for your citizenship application. Depending on your situation, you are likely to be required to submit some of the following documents:

- [Form AS6 (טופס אש/6)](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/generalpage/visas_forms/he/AS6.pdf)**\*** – Citizenship application form for married couples (only married couples who apply directly from A5 visa)
- [Form AZ21 (טופס אז/21)](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/generalpage/services_forms/he/az21.pdf) – Citizenship application form (general)
- [Form AZ28 (טופס אז/28)](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/generalpage/services_forms/he/AZ28.pdf)**\*** – Naturalization declaration
- [Form AZ29 (טופס אז/29)](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/generalpage/services_forms/he/AZ29.pdf) – Declaration of other citizenships
- [Center of Life documents and relationship proofs](https://aic.org.il/list-of-documents-required-for-the-partner-visa-application-renewal/) – Depending on when your last yearly meeting took place, you may need to submit the normal Center of Life documents and relationship proofs.

\*These forms includes affidavits that [require signature validation](https://aic.org.il/affidavits-in-israel/) (by a Israeli lawyer or other).

#### Interview

A shared interview is a mandatory requirement of the citizenship application process. Some branches may hold the interview during the same meeting of the citizenship application meeting while others may separate them into two different appointments.

You and your partner will be interviewed separately and asked about your relationship. The interview is similar to the shared interview that is done at the beginning of the process or the yearly ones. Citizenship interviews, however, tend to be significantly longer and more in-depth.

Make sure you carefully read our article about [the shared interview, your rights during the interview, instructions, and tips.](https://aic.org.il/the-partner-visa-interview-everything-you-need-to-know/)

#### Payment

- Married couples (end of gradual process): **810 NIS** \[2025\]
- Permanent residents (after gradual process): **200 NIS** \[2025\]\*

According to the relevant law and regulation, the fees should be charged upon the granting of the citizenship. Practically, PIBA tends to charge this fee during the submission of the application (usually, at the interview meeting).

\*Couples who ended their process in permanent residency status already the paid the end-of-process fee when they finished their gradual process!

### Step 3: Receive a Decision

You will **not** receive an answer regarding your citizenship application “on the spot” (unlike some other applications). The citizenship application is under the jurisdiction of the branch manager or the visa department team lead, and not a simple clerk.

There are 2 possible outcomes to your application:

1. **Approval** – If your citizenship application is approved, you will be notified by post, email or phone call. You will be summoned to a meeting of finalizing the naturalization.
2. **Refusal** – A refusal letter specifying the reasons for the refusal will usually be sent by post. The refusal may include a decision to extend the A5 visa for another year (for married couples who don’t yet have PR status) or a closing notice for the entire file. **A refusal decision can be appealed.**

Waiting times for a decision on a citizenship application vary significantly between PIBA branches, depending on their workload and priorities. Expect several months of waiting time.

### Step 4: Become a Citizen!

If your application has been approved, you will be summoned to your attending branch of the Population Authority to formally receive your citizenship and undergo the “Loyalty Declaration Ceremony”. Remember, both you and your partner **must be present at this meeting** with your Israeli IDs and foreign passport.

**Did you know?** You are allowed to bring relatives and friends to this meeting, to be a part of the ceremony!

Here are the steps you’ll undergo at the office:

1. You will receive a number in a specific queue for naturalizations (“A” number).
2. You will be called to a clerk or a manager and will have to show your ID and foreign passport, including the Israeli ID of the Israeli partner.
3. You will be called to the office manager’s room to perform the loyalty declaration.
4. The foreigner will be handed a paper with the Hebrew declaration to be read out loud **in Hebrew**. The declaration reads:

> **“אני, (שם המתאזרח/ת), מצהיר/ה שאהיה אזרח/ית נאמנ/ה למדינת ישראל”  
> ****I, (your name), declare that I will be a loyal citizen to the State of Israel”  
> “Ani, (your name), matzhir/matzhira she’eheye ezrach/ezrachit ne’eman/ne’emana lemedinat Israel”**
>
> The declaration in Hebrew, translated to English and transcribed (Hebrew pronunciation)

Congratulations! You (or your partner) are now a new Israeli citizen!

After the ceremony, you will be sent to a clerk for processing a new ID and an Israeli passport (see below).

You will also receive a **citizenship certificate**, which you may need to present to your country of origin.

### Step 5: Applying For An Israeli Passport

During the same visit to the office, right after the declaration, you will be sent to a clerk to submit a request for your first Israeli biometric passport.

If you have a chance, we recommend paying for the passport [online](https://govforms.gov.il/mw/forms/biopassport%40piba.gov.il), where you can enjoy a reduced fee.

**Passport fee at the branch:** 300 NIS \[2025\]  
**Online winter fee:** 175 NIS  
**Online summer fee:** 280 NIS

#### Validity of First Passport After Naturalization

According to procedure 3.3.0004, as a naturalized citizen, your first passport will be valid for 1 year only. Such limitations are imposed on every new citizen (including Olim), in order to discourage the behavior of getting a passport and then immediately leaving Israel.

**Did you know?** AIC is looking into combating the policy of the short-term first passport for naturalized partners of Israelis, as they have proved their center of life in Israel for years, before even being eligible for the passport.

Read about the renewal of a first passport and the implications of leaving Israel after naturalization in our dedicated article (coming soon! In the meantime, visit the FAQ’s below).

## Citizenship FAQs

Can my citizenship be revoked once granted?

Yes, only if the citizenship was granted under **false pretenses** or in extreme cases of disloyalty to the State of Israel. False pretenses mean that, at the time of granting the citizenship, the information available to PIBA was false (e.g., a fake relationship, a couple that had already broken up, etc.).

The time PIBA has to make a false pretenses claim is not limited. However, after 3 years have passed, the Minister of the Interior would be required to petition the court to revoke the citizenship.

If I leave Israel, or if my relationship ends, will my citizenship be taken away?

No, your Israeli citizenship cannot be revoked based on the amount of time you spend outside of Israel, or the future status of your relationship (as long as it was not given under false pretenses in the first place).

How long does it take to receive Israeli citizenship?

All couples must complete the [entire gradual process](https://aic.org.il/the-gradual-process-birds-eye-view/) to be eligible for Israeli citizenship. In case of same-sex couples or non-married couples, the citizenship application can only be made after receiving a Permanent Resident status.

Once you meet the eligibility criteria, the processing time for the citizenship application varies between branches but typically takes several months.

What if my home country doesn’t allow dual citizenship?

If your country does not allow dual citizenship, you have two options:

Option #1 – You can apply for permanent residency in Israel instead.

Option #2 – You can renounce your foreign citizenship and become an Israeli citizen. Procedures for renouncing citizenship vary by country, so check the procedure with your country of origin.

What happens to my Israeli passport if I leave Israel?

PIBA might not renew your Israeli passport if you leave Israel for an extended period of time, and give you a Laissez-Passer (‘Teudat Maavar’) instead. The citizenship will not be harmed.
