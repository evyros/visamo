# Status application for a married spouse (form AS/6) (בקשה למעמד לבן/בת זוג נשוי/אה (טופס אש/6))

The application for status for a foreign spouse married to an Israeli, with the declarations that go with it.

Form **AS/6** (אש/6), "Application for status in Israel for a foreign spouse
married to an Israeli" (בקשה לקבלת מעמד בישראל לבן זוג זר הנשוי לישראלי),
is the status application for a married couple (5.2.0008 §ד.2.א). The
declarations in it are filed with it, as part of it: each partner's
declaration (5.2.0008 §ד.2.ז), and the landlord's affidavit if you rent.

**The form comes in two formats,** and its pages come in a different order,
with different page numbers: in one, the application is on page 2, in
another on page 3. So find each part by its title, not its page number.

The form has these parts:

- **The checklist letter** ("הנדון: בקשה לקבלת מעמד בישראל לבן / בת זוג זר/ה
  הנשוי/אה לישראלי/ת"): the list of documents to attach (below).
- **The application:** both partners' details, and any children coming with
  the foreign partner (below).
- **The applicants' declaration** (הצהרת המבקשים): the people who can vouch
  for you, and the declaration you both sign (below).
- **The Israeli partner's declaration** (הצהרת בן הזוג המזמין), signed in
  front of a lawyer.
- **The foreign partner's declaration** (הצהרת בן הזוג המוזמן), signed in
  front of a lawyer.
- **The landlord's affidavit** (תצהיר נספח להסכם שכ"ד - בני זוג), if you
  rent: signed by your landlord in front of a lawyer.

## How to get it

The form is on the Population and Immigration Authority's site (see
[sources.md](../sources.md)). It's in Hebrew, so it needs no translation.

1. Fill in the application, and both sign the applicants' declaration.
2. **Each partner's declaration:** tick (☒) the box next to each statement
   you declare, write the place and the date, and sign it **in front of a
   lawyer (עו"ד) or a Misrad Hapnim registrar (רשם)**, who confirms the
   declaration at the bottom of the page.
3. **If you rent:** your landlord fills in their affidavit and signs it
   in front of a lawyer or a registrar, the same way. Attach **a scan of the
   landlord's Teudat Zehut** to it.

**If the foreign partner is abroad and the Israeli partner is in Israel:**
the Israeli partner files the form with their own parts, and the foreign
partner fills in and signs their declaration once they arrive in Israel.
**If you both live abroad,** you file together once you're both in Israel
(see [both-abroad.md](../both-abroad.md)), so you fill in and sign both
declarations then.

## What it should include

### The checklist letter

Its title starts "הנדון: בקשה לקבלת מעמד בישראל…", and it lists the documents
to attach, under "פירוט המסמכים". It's the branch's letter to you: nothing to
fill in. Its note matters:

- the documents are a condition for handling the application, and more may
  be asked for after you file;
- bring copies of the original documents, as well as the originals, which
  the clerk checks;
- every public document needs a notarized translation into Hebrew, except
  from Arabic and English (see [certification.md](../certification.md)).

### The application

The part with the sections "פרטי המזמין/ה" (the Israeli partner's details)
and "פרטי המוזמן/ת" (the foreign partner's details).

- **What you're asking for:** permanent residency (בקשה לישיבת קבע בישראל)
  or naturalization (בקשה להתאזרחות בישראל, under section 7 of the
  Citizenship Law). A citizen's spouse of a different sex can ask for
  naturalization. A same-sex spouse gets permanent residency first, and can
  apply for citizenship after it; a permanent resident's spouse gets
  permanent residency (see [process.md](../process.md)).
- **The warning above it:** getting Israeli citizenship this way may affect
  the foreign partner's other citizenship. Check it with the clerk and with
  their country's representative before you file. The foreign partner
  declares this in their own declaration.
- **The Israeli partner's details:** their status (citizen or permanent
  resident), ID number, family and first name, father's, grandfather's and
  mother's names, date of birth, gender, civil status and date of marriage,
  religion and nationality (לאום), any previous family or first name, date of
  entry to Israel and of getting status, citizenship, occupation, address
  and phone numbers.
- **The foreign partner's details:** passport number and its expiry, an ID
  number of the Palestinian territories if they have one, family and first
  name, father's, grandfather's and mother's names, date of birth, gender,
  civil status, religion, nationality and citizenship, date and place of
  entry to Israel, status in Israel and its expiry, address abroad, relation
  to the Israeli partner, and phone numbers.
- **Children coming along** (קטינים נלווים, under 18): for each, their ID or
  passport number, names, the other parent's name, place and date of birth,
  gender and date of entry. Below it, whether the other parent's consent is
  attached, and how (see [children.md](../children.md)).
- **The foreign partner's photo,** in its box (תמונת המוזמן/ת).

### The applicants' declaration

The part titled "הצהרת המבקשים".

- **People who can give details about you,** filled in by the Israeli
  partner: each person's ID number, family and first name, relation,
  address (optional field) and phone, with a copy of their ID.
- **The declaration**, by both of you:
  - the details in the application are true, and are the basis for deciding
    it;
  - you'll report any change in your circumstances while it's handled, and
    during the graduated procedure if it's approved;
  - you'll update the Authority on your address and any change to it, and
    the Authority may check further, including visiting your home: not
    cooperating can close the application, or cancel or not renew the
    foreign partner's visa;
  - false or incomplete details, including hiding details or not reporting a
    change, can lead to cancelling any status given (section 11 of the Entry
    into Israel Law, 1952) and to a removal order against the foreign
    partner (section 13);
  - false details or a forged document are a criminal offense, and the
    Authority may report it to the police.
- **Both your signatures,** each with your name, ID number and the date.
- **The Israeli partner's photo,** in its box (תמונת המזמין).
- **The office's part** (received by, the clerk's name, notes, the receipt):
  leave it empty.

### The Israeli partner's declaration

Titled **הצהרת בן הזוג המזמין** ("declaration of the inviting partner"). In
it, the Israeli partner declares a list of statements about the marriage,
and that everything they submit is true.

- **A tick next to each statement**. By ticking, the Israeli partner
  declares that:
  - the marriage is real, and wasn't made to get status in Israel;
  - they aren't registered as married (other than to their partner) in the
    population register of any country;
  - they undertake to take in the foreign partner, and to cover the costs of
    their stay, housing and living in Israel, without the foreign partner
    needing help from the authorities in Israel or any other body, subject
    to the law;
  - the foreign partner is their only spouse, and they're married only to
    them;
  - the foreign partner isn't married to anyone else, and they're the
    foreign partner's only spouse.
- **An explanation for any statement they can't declare**, in the lines
  under the list, with the details or the reason.
- **The place, the date and the Israeli partner's signature.**
- **The lawyer's or registrar's confirmation** (אישור קבלת ההצהרה): their
  name and license number, the Israeli partner's name and the ID it was
  checked against, a statement that the Israeli partner was warned to tell
  the truth and confirmed the declaration in front of them, and their stamp
  and signature.

The application number box (מספר בקשה) at the top can stay empty if you
don't have a number yet.

### The foreign partner's declaration

Titled **הצהרת בן הזוג המוזמן** ("declaration of the invited partner"). In
it, the foreign partner declares a list of statements about themselves and
the marriage, and that everything they submit is true.

- **The foreign partner's details:** first name, last name and ID number (מ"ז).
- **A tick next to each statement**. By ticking, the foreign partner declares
  that:
  - they were told that getting Israeli citizenship may cancel their other
    citizenship, and were advised to check this with their country's
    authorities before deciding. If the application is approved, they'll be
    an Israeli citizen by naturalization, under sections 5–8 of the
    Citizenship Law, 1952;
  - they intend to settle in Israel;
  - they aren't addicted to hard drugs or alcohol;
  - they don't have a disease that could endanger public health in Israel;
  - they haven't committed a criminal offense (other than traffic offenses),
    have no pending case or arrest warrant, and aren't wanted by the police
    or security authorities of any country;
  - they haven't acted against the Jewish people or the security of the
    State of Israel;
  - they haven't been a member or a supporter, directly or indirectly,
    including financially, of a body, organization or movement acting
    against the State of Israel, its residents or its citizens;
  - they've never had a deportation or removal order from Israel, and no
    authority has asked them to leave Israel;
  - the marriage is real, and wasn't made to get status in Israel;
  - they aren't registered as married (other than to their partner) in the
    population register of any country;
  - the Israeli partner is their only spouse, and they're married only to
    them;
  - the Israeli partner isn't married to anyone else, and they're the
    Israeli partner's only spouse;
  - they know the Population and Immigration Authority doesn't have to give
    status in Israel to children from a previous relationship, or to anyone
    else.
- **An explanation for any statement they can't declare**, in the lines
  under the list, with the details or the reason.
- **The place, the date and the foreign partner's signature.**
- **The lawyer's or registrar's confirmation** (אישור קבלת ההצהרה): their
  name and license number, the foreign partner's name and the ID it was
  checked against, a statement that the foreign partner was warned to tell
  the truth and confirmed the declaration in front of them, and their stamp
  and signature.

### The landlord's affidavit

**If you rent:** titled "affidavit annexed to the lease – couples" (תצהיר
נספח להסכם שכ"ד - בני זוג). In it, the landlord declares that they own the
home, that it's rented to you, who else lives there, and who pays the bills.
Like the lease, it's asked for when you live together, or have lived
together, and whenever the Israeli partner lives in Israel. Not needed if you
own your home.

- **The landlord's details:** first name, last name and ID number (מ"ז).
- **Whose application it supports:** the tenant's name and ID number, and
  the application number and filing date, if you have them.
- **The home:** its address, that it's registered in the landlord's name
  (for a building with several homes, which one), and its size.
- **That it's rented to you,** and who else lives there: other families,
  floor by floor, with their names, ID numbers, relation, and who pays their
  rent.
- **Who pays each bill,** the landlord or you: electricity, water, phone and
  arnona, and how the payment is made.
- **The landlord's civil status,** number of children under 18, and
  occupation.
- **That since the lease was signed, you live only there,** and that the
  landlord will tell the Population and Immigration Authority if you leave.
- **The date, and the landlord's name and signature.**
- **The lawyer's or registrar's confirmation** (אישור קבלת ההצהרה): their
  name, license number and the date, the landlord's ID it was checked
  against, and their stamp and signature.
- **A scan of the landlord's Teudat Zehut**, with it.

## If you don't have it

**The landlord's affidavit:** in some cases, Misrad Hapnim waives it when
there's a strong reason you can't get it. Even so, it's strongly recommended
to bring it.

## Questions

**Our form's pages are in a different order. Is it the wrong form?**
No. AS/6 comes in two formats, with the pages in a different order. Find
each part by its title: the application ("פרטי המזמין/ה", "פרטי המוזמן/ת"),
the applicants' declaration ("הצהרת המבקשים"), and the three declarations.

**Do we ask for permanent residency or naturalization?**
It depends on the Israeli partner's status, and on your marriage. A citizen's
spouse of a different sex can ask for naturalization. A same-sex spouse gets
permanent residency first, and can apply for citizenship after it. A
permanent resident's spouse gets permanent residency.

**Who are the people who can give details about us?**
People who know you and can tell the Authority about you, your family and
your partner. The Israeli partner lists them, each with a copy of their ID.

**What if I can't tick one of the statements in a declaration?**
Don't tick it. Write the details or the reason in the lines under the list
("אם אינך יכול/ה להצהיר…").

**What happens if something in a declaration isn't true?**
The declarations say that false or incomplete details, including leaving
out something relevant or details from the documents you submit, can lead
to cancelling any visa or status given, and to a removal order from Israel
against the foreign partner (sections 11 and 13 of the Entry into Israel
Law, 1952). Giving false details or a forged document is also a criminal
offense.

**The declarations are written in the masculine. Do they apply to me?**
Yes. The form says the masculine wording is only for convenience, and it's
meant for women and men alike.

**Do the declarations have to be signed in front of a lawyer?**
A lawyer, or a Misrad Hapnim registrar: the confirmation at the bottom of
each declaration can be signed by either. It is a requirement. A lawyer
might charge a few hundred shekels.

**My partner is still abroad. Do they sign their declaration there?**
No. They sign it once they're in Israel, in front of an Israeli lawyer or a
Misrad Hapnim registrar. Until then, the Israeli partner files the form
without it. If you both live abroad, you file together once you're both in
Israel, with both declarations.
