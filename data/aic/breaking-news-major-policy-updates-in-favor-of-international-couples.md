# Breaking News: Major Policy Updates in Favor of International Couples

This week, PIBA released two major updates to the partner visa protocols:

- [Protocol 5.2.0008](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/policy/status_foreigner_married_to_israeli_procedure/he/5.2.0008.pdf) (נוהל הטיפול במתן מעמד לבן זוג זר הנשוי לאזרח ישראלי), which applies to married couples;
- [Protocol 5.2.0009](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/policy/israelis_couples_status_procedure/he/5.2.0009..pdf) (נוהל הטיפול במתן מעמד לבני זוג של ישראלים, לרבות בני אותו מין), which applies to non-married couples and same-sex couples.

Prior to this latest update, the spouse visa protocol 5.2.0008 had been more recently updated and reviewed. As a result, many of the current revisions to protocol 5.2.0009 were intended to align it with the spouse visa protocol.

Despite this, the July update also introduce several brand-new policies that apply to both procedures, with some very good news. Let’s dive in!

## The Big News: Visas Can Now Be Granted for Up to Two Years at a Time

According to the new policy, PIBA is now authorized to grant B1 and A5 visas for up to two years at a time, instead of only one year. Visas with longer validity will be allowed starting **from the second visa onward**, while the first visa in the process will remain for one year only, as before. This update applies to both married and non-married couples throughout the gradual process.

### How will this affect my process?

It is important to remember that the duration of the gradual process is **measured by time** and not by the number of visas. The duration of the gradual process remains the same: 4 years with an A5 visa for married couples\*; 3 years with a B1 visa and additional 4 years with an A5 visa for non-married couples.

This is what the gradual process could look like under the new policy:

- **Married couples** – Married couples will be able to receive a first A5 visa for one year, then a second A5 visa for up to two years, and a final A5 visa for one to two years.
- **Non-married couples** – Non-married couples will be able to receive a first B1 visa for one year, then a second B1 visa for up to two years, a first A5 visa for up to 2 years, and a final A5 visa for up to 2 years.

This is a significant change in favor of international couples, and it follows [AIC’s suggestions that were presented at the parliament in the past months](https://aic.org.il/aic-brings-partner-visa-procedures-to-the-knesset-watch-now/). As AIC argued, longer-term visas during the process will reduce the number of in-person meetings at the office and help relieve the currently unreasonable waiting times.

Additionally, this change offers a significant cost benefit for couples by reducing the need for frequent visa extensions, which cost over ₪205 each (₪410 including intervisa). It also saves applicants considerable time and effort.

\*Married couples spend an interim period of up to 6 months with a B1 visa, **outside of the gradual process’ timeline.** This is interim status covers the waiting time for the shared interview.

## Regional Director Authorized to Waive Document Requirements

According to the updated policy, the Regional Director may approve an exemption from a required document and allow an applicant to begin the gradual process without it, in exceptional cases. The applicant must submit a detailed request and demonstrate that exceptional circumstances prevented them from obtaining the document, show genuine efforts to obtain it, and provide official evidence supporting their claims. PIBA further clarifies that filing an asylum application alone is not considered an exceptional circumstance.

This exemption applies only to entering into the gradual process. The applicant must still submit all required documents before the process can be completed.

This represents a positive development for applicants. While similar flexibility may have been applied informally in the past, the fact that PIBA now has clear, official authority to waive document requirements in exceptional cases – thereby allowing applicants to enter the gradual process – marks an important step toward greater flexibility.

## Naturalization Applications Now Under the Authority of Deputy Manager

The spouse visa protocol now authorizes the deputy office manager to process and approve citizenship applications at the end of the gradual process (other than in cases where the applicant is Palestinian).

This may relieve waiting times for citizenship decisions, which have previously been under the sole authority of the office manager.

## Heavier Restrictions on Partners of Israelis who Stay in Israel Illegally

PIBA has updated the rules that protect applicants without a valid visa from deportation. Previously, foreign partners of Israeli citizens who were staying in Israel without valid status were generally protected from deportation while their partner visa application was being processed and until a decision was made. PIBA could not require them to leave Israel as a condition for continuing the application, unless a deportation order had already been issued by a court.

According to the updated policy, couples where the foreign partner is staying in Israel without valid status may still attend the partner visa interview, as long as no deportation order has been issued. However, PIBA has added a new condition: **if it is found that the relationship started only after the foreign partner’s visa expired**, the foreign partner may be required to leave Israel as a condition for continuing the partner visa process. The policy also states that if the interview shows that the relationship is genuine, the foreign partner will not be required to leave Israel.

It is still unclear how PIBA will apply this new rule in practice, especially in cases of genuine couples who met while the foreign partner was already staying in Israel without valid status. AIC will continue monitoring this issue and [share updates as more information becomes available.](https://aic.org.il/subscription-to-newsletter/)

As before, PIBA will not process a partner visa application if the foreign partner is already subject to a court-issued deportation\* order or if deportation enforcement measures are already underway. In those cases, the partner visa processing will only start once the foreign partner leaves Israel.

\*These restrictions do not apply to people who were refused entry and were deported at the Israeli border.

## In-Person Marriages in El Salvador Now Follow the Standard Protocol

According to the updated policy, marriages conducted in El Salvador where both parties were physically present at the ceremony will now be treated the same as any other marriage. Previously, all marriages from El Salvador were classified as proxy marriages, since El Salvador permits weddings without the physical presence of one or both partners.

This specific update is a notable achievement for AIC. Several months ago, the organization was contacted by a couple who had married in El Salvador in person and encountered difficulties finalizing their process. After AIC raised the matter with PIBA headquarters, their case was resolved. The protocol has now been updated accordingly.

## Ban on Boycott Supporters, Holocaust and October 7th Massacre Deniers

The Entry Into Israel law, which dictates Israeli immigration policy, bars foreign individuals who express themselves in certain ways from receiving status in Israel. This includes those who:

1. Publish a public call for a boycott of the State of Israel or pledge to participate in such a boycott;
2. Publish statements denying the Holocaust, denying the October 7, 2023 massacre; or
3. Publish statements supporting the prosecution of Israeli citizens in a foreign country or in an international court for actions they took as part of their duties in the Israel Defense Forces or in one of the security branches of the State of Israel.

This ban is not new and has been part of protocol 5.2.0008 for a while. However, it has recently been expanded due to legislation changes, and has now been adapted to procedure 5.2.0009. It applies to all foreigners who apply for status in Israel, including tourists.

## Technicalities and Additional Policy Updates

The new policies include updated information about bureaucratic technicalities, instructions to PIBA officials, legal definitions and more. They also include additional policy updates about more minor subjects that apply to certain groups that were not covered in this article.

## Summary

This update includes a major improvement of the visa policy, that surprised even us! Many of these changes signal a positive shift in PIBA’s approach toward international couples and reflect meaningful efforts to improve efficiency, and enhance the quality of services provided to applicants.

AIC welcomes the implementation of this policy update and commends PIBA for taking steps toward a more flexible, practical, and service-oriented approach. We believe that these updates – particularly the option to issue longer-term visas – **will help reduce waiting times, ease the stress and uncertainty experienced by applicants, and streamline the gradual process.**

We would specifically like to thank the Director of the Visas and Status Division, Mrs. Ronit Alian, the Director of the Population Administration, Mr. Yoel Lipovitzky, and Director General, Mr. Boaz Yosef, for their leadership and brave decision-making.

## What’s next?

We encourage couples who have a visa renewal in the coming days to ask for a two-year renewal instead of only one year. Feel free to print the new protocol and refer the clerks to it if needed. We expect it will take some time until clerks fully implement the new policies. It is also important to note that PIBA does not **have to** provide a two-year visa, but it is worth trying.
