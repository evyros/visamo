# Official Documentation from Singapore

This post was written by a volunteer community member. While our volunteers do their best to share the most accurate information, there still may be errors or inaccurate details in this post. Please keep that in mind while reading!

This post includes detailed instructions on how to obtain and authenticate the official documentation and certifications from abroad, that are needed to apply for a partner or spouse visa in Israel. The basic personal documents you will be looking for are birth certificate, certificate of non-criminal record, current and/or previous personal status certificate (martial status), and name change certificate. Complete documentation about the foreign partner from their home country is prerequisite of the gradual process, and without it one cannot start the partner visa process and get a status in Israel based on their relationship.

## Birth Certificate from Singapore

Name in Singapore:  Birth Certificate

**How to obtain?**

Apply to extract a copy from Immigration & Checkpoints Authority (ICA) website.

The general processing time for a birth extract application is within six working days.

You will receive a digital birth extract if a digital birth certificate was earlier issued in respect of the birth record. Otherwise, you will receive the extract in hardcopy by registered mail.

**Cost: $40**

**[Link](https://www.ica.gov.sg/documents/birth/apply_extract)**

## Name change certificate from Singapore

\* Please note – Name change certificate is not necessary if you did not change your first / last name.

Name in Singapore: change / adding of marriage name

**How to obtain?**

In Singapore, it’s possible to add marriage name in bracket ( ) onto your passport and ID, on top of your existing name. This is a free service as far as I remember. The only cost you are paying for is the cost of a new passport.

For instance, what I did was to add another name with my husband surname in bracket. My family name is “Li”. Most Singaporean Chinese has a Chinese name on top of an English one, so it is totally up to you if you still want to retain your Chinese name / Surname in the bracket. I find it very useful and cost-effective to do it this way because I get to retain my family and original name, and I get to choose which name (original or after marriage name) I want to use when purchasing flight tickets for example. With PIBA (Misrad Hapnim), it is also easier to use “Josephine Nerel” which obviously is easier for them to pronounce and understand.

*Example*:

Original Name on ID and passport: Li Dongyan Josephine

New passport name: Li Dongyan Josephine (Josephine Nerel)

An addition of a name in brackets isn’t a “name change”, and therefore you will not get any name change certificate when doing this process.

If you really want to change your original name for good, then you will have to engage a lawyer to do a “deed poll”. This is a chargeable service.

**Cost:** cost of a new passport **SGD $70-$80** depending on whether you do it online or in person / replacement of ID also known as IC by Singaporean at **$60**.

cost of engaging a lawyer for deed poll approximately **$100** (varies from one lawyer to another) and if you want the new name to be reflected on the passport, you obviously also have to pay for the cost of a new passport + ID as well.

**[Link](https://www.mfa.gov.sg/Overseas-Mission/Ho-Chi-Minh-City/Consular-Services/Change-of-Name-in-Passport)**

## Personal status certificate from Singapore

Name in Singapore: There is no such certificate in Singapore.

**How to obtain?**

Since there is no such certificate in Singapore, you have to retrieve a record that you are not married (before you actually get married) from Registry of Marriage (ROM). All marriages in Singapore are recorded in this Registry. This is also where one gets married officially in Singapore.

Singapore offers an online system to search for existing marriage records and the registry will reflect “no marriage record found if you aren’t married at the moment. Once you are married, the registry will obviously reflect you are married. In case you are already registered as married and you need a certificate to prove your previous status, the best bet would be to get a lawyer to do a single/not married affidavit, which is chargeable by one’s lawyer.

**Cost: $35** when you do the search on ROM website / single affidavit charges vary from one lawyer to another

[**Link**](https://www.rom.gov.sg/emrs/rom_emrs_paid_start.asp)

## Certificate of Non-Criminal Record from Singapore

Name in Singapore: Certificate of Clearance (COC)

**How to obtain?**

Singaporean citizens who have access to Singpass have to apply for the COC online via Singpass after logging in. COC office does not accept walk-in applications anymore.

Generally, the processing time for a COC is within 5 working days. Once your COC application has been accepted, you will receive an email notification. Failure to provide the required documents may result in delays or rejection of the application. 

\*Non-Singaporean citizens who need to issue a COC from Singapore are required to submit a fingerprint impression in order to apply for COC.

**Cost: $55-$60**

**[Link](https://eservices.police.gov.sg/content/policehubhome/homepage/certificate-of-clearance-hub.html)**

## Verifying the documents in Singapore

All official documentation from abroad that needs to be presented in Israel, must be verified by the country of its origin in order to be valid outside of it, in Israel. Many countries use “apostille” for this purpose. An apostille is usually a document, issued by an authorized authority which authenticates the documents in question.  This is used to demonstrate that they are valid documents that can be recognized in other countries.

As of 16 September 2021, Singapore has officially adopted the Hague Convention, and it hence becomes possible to obtain an apostille for official documents. Apostilles are issued by Singapore’s designated competent authority, the Singapore Academy of Law (SAL).

### Legalization of Singapore Government Documents

(eg. Marriage Certificate, Birth Certificate etc.)

Bring the original documents issued by the Singapore Government Departments in their original text to the Singapore Academy of Law (SAL) for legalization. Please visit the SAL’s legalization portal at [legalisation.sal.sg](https://legalisation.sal.sg/) for more information.

Outlining the steps to obtain an apostille for official documents:

First, submit a request on the SAL website, and make payment online. An invoice will be sent to the registered email address. Next, visit the SAL office in person on weekdays only (the office is not open on weekends) and bring along the necessary official documents you wish to have legalized.

**Cost: $30 excluding GST**

[**Link**](https://legalisation.sal.sg/Legalisation/LegalisationInfo)
