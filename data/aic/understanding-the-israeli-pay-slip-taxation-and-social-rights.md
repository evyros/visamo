# Understanding The Israeli Pay Slip, Taxation, and Social Rights

Understanding the Israeli pay slip might seem like an overwhelming task, especially if you’re new to the country and don’t know Hebrew that well. Israel is known for having a complex taxation system, and navigating it can be a challenge.

Don’t worry! The following article provides a detailed explanation of the Israeli pay slip, taxation, national insurance premiums, as well as some basic information on your social rights. It will help you better understand what you are entitled to as an employee in Israel, and specifically as a B1 or A5 visa holder.

Let’s dive right in!

## The Pay Slip (Tlush Maskoret – תלוש משכורת)

In Hebrew, “pay slip” translates to “Tlush Maskoret” or “Tlush Sakhar”. The pay slip is also sometimes referred to simply as “tlush”. Issuing a detailed monthly pay slip is mandatory for all employers by law. By law, the salary of monthly employees must be paid no later than the 9th of the following month. The pay slip has to be issued and given to the employee in the same timeframe.

An employer that is failing to pay/issue a pay slip is breaking the law and you can [submit an official complaint](https://govforms.gov.il/mw/forms/WorkRightsViolationComplaints@labor.gov.il) about them to the Labor Regulation and Enforcement Branch.

The pay slip is always divided into two sections at least. The payments section and the deductions section.

## Payments

Here’s a breakdown of what you’re likely to find in the payments section of your pay slip and what each item means:

### Base Salary (שכר יסוד)

The base salary should be the main component of your payments section. The rest of the payments will be additions to the base salary that you get either by law or by an agreement with your employer.

It’s important to note that your pension and unemployment deposits by the employer are calculated as a percentage of the base salary only. Therefore it’s important to make sure this salary represents your actual work.

### Overtime hours (שעות נוספות)

According to Israeli law, employees are entitled to an additional payment at a higher rate, for the time they worked outside of their “standard” workday hours. The definition of standard workday hours is complicated and changes depending on the characteristics of the workplace.

In general, employees that are working 5 days a week, are entitled to over-hours payment starting from the first minute they worked over 8 hours and 36 minutes in one day (not including a break of 24 consecutive minutes at least).

For the first 2 hours after the standard hours – the employee is entitled to a rate of 125% per hour.

From the 3rd hour after the standard hours – the employee is entitled to a rate of 150% per hour.

There are many rules and different classifications for overtime payment. The above example doesn’t necessarily apply to you. Check an elaborate explanation by Kol Zchut (in Hebrew) about [the calculation method of overtime payment in Israel.](https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/%D7%92%D7%9E%D7%95%D7%9C_%D7%A2%D7%91%D7%95%D7%A8_%D7%A9%D7%A2%D7%95%D7%AA_%D7%A0%D7%95%D7%A1%D7%A4%D7%95%D7%AA)

In the pay slip, over-hours (in their corresponsive rates) appear as separate payments.

### Global Over Hours (ש.נ. גלובלי)

Instead of paying overtime rates as stated above, in some cases and only if the employee agreed to it, employers can pay an additional monthly flat rate, which is meant to represent an expected amount of over-hours that the employee works during the entire month.

An additional flat-rate salary is added, as agreed with your employer in your employment contract, to cover the payment for any over-hours you have worked during the month.

### Transportation (נסיעות)

According to Israeli law, employers must compensate employees for the cheapest monthly transportation pass for each employee or approximately 22.60 NIS per day (whichever is lower). For example, if you live and work in the wider Tel Aviv area, it’s about 213 NIS. 

### Convalescence Pay (דמי הבראה)

Employees that are employed for more than 1 year, are eligible for convalescence pay (“Dmei Haavra”) for all the time they worked with the employer. Dmei Havraa is usually paid once a year (July or August salary), some employers pay it proportionally every month.

The value of Dmei Havraa is calculated by “days”, and the amount of days you get paid for depends on your seniority in the company, as follows:

| Employee Seniority | Number of convalescence days |
| --- | --- |
| 1 Year (only once completing it) | 5 Days |
| 2-3 Years | 6 Days |
| 4-10 Years | 7 Days |
| 11-15 Years | 8 Days |
| 16-19 Years | 9 Days |
| 20 Years + | 10 Days |

One day is equivalent to **378 NIS (private sector)** or **471.4 NIS (public sector)** (as of 2023).

### Additional Payments

Other payments you may find in the payments section are:

1. Meal Value (שווי ארוחות) – If your work contract includes meals or meal compensation (for example via TenBis), your payslip will have this section. This is most usually a theoretical section – you are not actually getting paid – that is only used for the calculation of taxes and fees.
2. Car / mobile phone value – another theoretical section.
3. Bonuses.
4. Additions – Risk addition, seniority addition, living cost addition.

## Deductions

### Income Tax  (מס הכנסה) 

Income Tax in Israel is counted according to your income, and is staggered as follows (2023 tax rates): 

| Annual Income | Monthly Income | Tax Rate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Up to 81,840 ₪ | Up to 6,790 ₪ | 10% |
| 81,841 – 116,760 ₪ | 6,791 – 9,730 ₪ | 14% |
| 116,761 – 187,440 ₪ | 9,731 – 15,620 ₪ | 20% |
| 187,441 – 260,520 ₪ | 15,621 – 21,710 ₪ | 31% |
| 260,521 – 542,160 ₪ | 21,711 – 45,180 ₪ | 35% |
| 542,161 – 698,280 ₪ | 45,181 – 58,190 ₪ | 47% |
| 698,281 ₪ and above | 58,191 ₪ and above | 50% \* |

\*Additional 3% tax payment for individuals with an annual income over 698,280 ₪.

**In conclusion – the higher tax “steps” are paid only for the corresponding part of your salary, not from the 1st shekel.**

**Did you know?** If you are working for two different employers in the same tax year, you might need to do “tax coordination” (תיאום מס). Israeli residents (A5) can apply for it online, otherwise, you need to go in person to the [Income Tax Department](https://www.gov.il/he/service/scheduling-appointment-taxes) (מס הכנסה).

#### Tax Credit Points

Every employee in Israel, including foreign employees and partners of Israelis with B1 visa, are entitled to tax credit points.

Tax credit points are worth money – this money is deducted from the final amount of income tax that you’re due to pay. **Each tax credit point equals 235 NIS per month** (2023)**.**

- **B1 partner visa holders** – 1 tax credit point (as a base)\*.
- **A5 visa holders** – 2.25 tax credit points (as a base – equal to Israeli residents)
- **Women** – +0.5 tax credit point

\***Did you know?** Foreign workers on B1 visas in the sector of caregiving are entitled to 2.25 tax credit points (as a base), instead of only 1.

There are many more criteria that entitle additional tax credit points in a variety of situations, mostly related to the number of children and marital status. You can use [this calculator](https://secapp.taxes.gov.il/srsimulatorNZ/#/simulator) to find out if you’re entitled to any other credit points (the calculator is relevant for residents only).

Visit the Kol Zchut website (in Hebrew) for more [information on tax credit points and how they affect your final income tax deduction.](https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/%D7%A0%D7%A7%D7%95%D7%93%D7%95%D7%AA_%D7%96%D7%99%D7%9B%D7%95%D7%99_%D7%9E%D7%9E%D7%A1_%D7%94%D7%9B%D7%A0%D7%A1%D7%94)

#### Income tax refund

The Income tax that workers pay is calculated annually. That means, that if a monthly employee earns 10,000 NIS brutto, they will have a tax deduction in their payslip which is calculated **as if they are going to have this income for the entire year**.

What happens though, if an employee only worked for one month during the entire year, and for the rest of the year was unemployed? The tax that was deducted in this one single payslip from his 10,000 NIS income, did not consider that this would be the only yearly income.

In this case, the employee was deducted too much tax, and he is entitled to a tax refund.

These are the situations in which an employee may deserve a tax refund (read more [here](https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/%D7%94%D7%97%D7%96%D7%A8_%D7%9E%D7%A1_%D7%94%D7%9B%D7%A0%D7%A1%D7%94)):

1. The employee wasn’t working for the entire year/was on unpaid leave (חל”ת)
2. The employee didn’t pursue the tax relief or credit points that they deserve: for example, a credit due to a donation, credit points for a bachelor’s degree, or a child with a disability.
3. The employee is employed in several workplaces and hasn’t done tax coordination (תיאום מס).
4. The employee had a child during the tax year (an event that entitles both the father and the mother to additional credit points)
5. When an employee contributes money to retirement insurance voluntarily (in addition to the deduction in the payslip).
6. Changes to the salary – big bonuses or reducing the number of working hours.

If you think you are entitled to a tax refund, we suggest consulting an accountant or the bookkeeper at your workplace.

### Bituach Leumi/National Insurance (ביטוח לאומי)

- **B1 visa holders** – Although most B1 visa holders are not eligible for most of the coverage that the Israeli national insurance offers, there is a mandatory minimum coverage and therefore a very small deduction is made to national insurance by law**.**  
    Read our [explanation on why B1 visa holders need to pay the Bituach Leumi payments.](https://aic.org.il/why-am-i-paying-national-insurance-on-a-b1-visa/)  

- **A5 visa holders** – A5 visa holders are Israeli residents and their national insurance payments and benefits are equal to those of Israeli citizens (it is mandatory to go through a [residency determination procedure at Bituach Leumi](https://aic.org.il/bituach-leumi-eligibility-and-registration-for-new-residents/) after getting the A5 status).  
    The payment is as follows:

From the part of your salary **up to **7,122**** **NIS** (2023), the fee is as follows:

|  | Employer | Employee | TOTAL |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| National Insurance fee | % 3.55 | % 0.4 | % 3.95 |

For the part of your salary **from 7,122 NIS and up to 47,465 NIS** (2023), the fee is as follows:

|  | Employer | Employee | TOTAL |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| National Insurance fee | % 7.6 | % 7 | % 14.6 |

### Health Tax (מס בריאות)

**B1 visa holders are not eligible for public health insurance and therefore do not pay the health tax.**

A5 visa holders, like Israeli citizens, get access to public health insurance through the Israeli National Insurance (Bituach Leumi) and therefore have to pay the mandatory health tax.

The deductions for health tax are as follows:

From the part of your salary ****up to **7,122**** **NIS**** (2023), the fee is as follows:

|  | Employer | Employee | TOTAL |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| National Insurance fee | – | % 3.1 | % 3.1 |

For the part of your salary **from 7,122 NIS and up to 47,465 NIS** (2023), the fee is as follows:

|  | Employer | Employee | TOTAL |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| National Insurance fee | – | % 5 | % 5 |

### Mandatory Retirement Insurance or Equivalent (ביטוח פנסיוני)

According to Israeli law, every employee from the age of 20 (female) or 21 (male) is entitled to retirement insurance. There are 3 types of retirement insurance:

- Pension fund – קרן פנסיה- Keren Pensia
- Manager’s insurance – ביטוח מנהלים – Bituach Menahalim
- Provident fund – קופת גמל – Kupat Gemel

Read about the [different types of retirement insurance here](https://shomershekalim.com/types-of-pension-plans/?fbclid=IwAR2BFuKxCCLscWYYoczdXrenwa0M79F3n67nxTNxgeT5RNvmdHecfhcMgIc).

**Pay attention** – **B1 partner visa** holders are entitled to retirement insurance or equivalent! If the employer cannot start an insurance plan for them, they must deposit money to an equivalent savings account. If your employer is not depositing money to a retirement savings account for you (on a B1 visa or otherwise) they are breaking the law. Read more about [foreign workers’ right to retirement insurance here](https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/%D7%91%D7%99%D7%98%D7%95%D7%97_%D7%A4%D7%A0%D7%A1%D7%99%D7%95%D7%A0%D7%99_%D7%9C%D7%A2%D7%95%D7%91%D7%93_%D7%96%D7%A8).

For **restricted B1 visa** holders (in certain fields such as construction, institutional caregiving, industry, hospitality, etc), the pension deposits are replaced with deposits to their security deposit. The security deposit money will be released once they leave Israel permanently or when they become Temporary Residents (A5 visa). Read more [about security deposits here](https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/%D7%9E%D7%A9%D7%99%D7%9B%D7%AA_%D7%9B%D7%A1%D7%A4%D7%99_%D7%94%D7%A4%D7%99%D7%A7%D7%93%D7%95%D7%9F_%D7%A2%D7%9C_%D7%99%D7%93%D7%99_%D7%A2%D7%95%D7%91%D7%93%D7%99%D7%9D_%D7%96%D7%A8%D7%99%D7%9D).

If the employee does not have existing retirement insurance, the employer has to open an insurance plan to start depositing to it after the employee completes 6 months at the job.

If the employee already has retirement insurance, the employer has to make the mandatory deposits there from day 1 at the job. The deposits are done retroactively, after completing 3 months at the job or at the end of the tax year (calendar year).

#### Contributions to the retirement insurance

By law, both the employer and the employee have to deposit money into the retirement insurance funds. Current minimum contributions are:

- **12.5% by the employer** – 6.5% for pension payment; 6% for severance payment (see below).
- **6% by the employee** – for the pension payment.

Some insurance agents or company representatives don’t know how to deal with retirement insurance for B1 Visa holders. Don’t be alarmed and don’t take no for an answer. Companies that are confirmed to allow B1 visa holders to have retirement insurance plans are Migdal, Clal, Menorah Mivtahim, HaPhoenix, and more.

## Social Rights

### Vacation days and holidays pay (חופשה שנתית ודמי חגים)

**Vacation days** – Every employee in Israel, regardless of visa status, is entitled to vacation days. The number of vacation days depends on whether you’re a full-time employee or part-time and your seniority in the company.

The minimum number of vacation days dictated by law is as follows:

|  | Workplace of a 5-day workweek | Workplace of a 6-day workweek |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Net vacations days per year for a full-time\* monthly worker | 12 | 14 |

\*Part-time employees get vacation days in a proportional amount to the scope of their job.

\*Hourly/daily paid workers are entitled to vacation days only after working for 75 consecutive days in the same year or during 2 consecutive years with the same employer, or if they have a work contract of over 74 consecutive days. Read more about the [vacation time for hourly/daily paid workers here.](https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/%D7%97%D7%95%D7%A4%D7%A9%D7%94_%D7%A9%D7%A0%D7%AA%D7%99%D7%AA_%D7%9C%D7%A2%D7%95%D7%91%D7%93%D7%99%D7%9D_%D7%91%D7%A9%D7%9B%D7%A8_%D7%A9%D7%A2%D7%AA%D7%99_%D7%90%D7%95_%D7%99%D7%95%D7%9E%D7%99)

Seniority in the workplace of over 6 years grants the employee additional mandatory vacation days. Read more [here](https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/%D7%97%D7%99%D7%A9%D7%95%D7%91_%D7%9E%D7%A1%D7%A4%D7%A8_%D7%99%D7%9E%D7%99_%D7%94%D7%97%D7%95%D7%A4%D7%A9%D7%94_%D7%94%D7%A9%D7%A0%D7%AA%D7%99%D7%AA_%D7%9C%D7%A2%D7%95%D7%91%D7%93%D7%99%D7%9D_%D7%91%D7%9E%D7%A7%D7%95%D7%9E%D7%95%D7%AA_%D7%A2%D7%91%D7%95%D7%93%D7%94_%D7%A9%D7%91%D7%94%D7%9D_%D7%9E%D7%95%D7%A0%D7%94%D7%92_%D7%A9%D7%91%D7%95%D7%A2_%D7%A2%D7%91%D7%95%D7%93%D7%94_%D7%91%D7%9F_5_%D7%99%D7%9E%D7%99%D7%9D).

**Using the vacation days** – Usually, vacation days are granted gradually throughout the year, a bit more than a day in every payslip. The employee has to ask for permission to use the vacation days and it’s up to the employer to decide. For every vacation day redeemed, the employee will be paid a full day’s salary for every vacation day redeemed.

“**Forced” vacation** – employers are entitled to decide when the employee goes on vacation unless the employee doesn’t have enough vacation days available. Read more [here](https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/%D7%97%D7%95%D7%A4%D7%A9%D7%94_%D7%91%D7%99%D7%95%D7%96%D7%9E%D7%AA_%D7%94%D7%9E%D7%A2%D7%A1%D7%99%D7%A7).

**Vacation day accumulation** – Unless stated otherwise in the work contract, an employee that redeemed at least 7 vacation days in a year, can accumulate the rest of the unused vacation days for the next year, up to 2 additional years.

**Did you know?** Vacation days in the workplace can be negotiated, or improved by employers in order to attract employees. The numbers you see here are the minimum mandatory days per year.

**Holidays** – In addition to vacation days, employees in Israel are entitled to full payment for 9 holidays per year. Each employee is entitled to payment for the holidays of their religion OR the Jewish holidays (they can choose). For more [information about holiday payments, click here.](https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/%D7%93%D7%9E%D7%99_%D7%97%D7%92%D7%99%D7%9D)

### Sick days (ימי מחלה)

By law, all employees (part-time, full-time, or hourly paid) are entitled to 1.5 sick days per month or 18 days per year, up to a maximum accumulation of 90 days. You usually get no payment on your first sick day, only 50% payment on the second and third sick days, and 100% payment starting on the fourth day – some companies have other policies (100% payment from the first day on) if stated in the contract. 

You need to send your employer an official doctor’s sick note. You can redeem your sick days also for taking care of close family members.

### Severance Pay (פיצויי פיטורים “Pitzuim”)

**Did you know?** Even an employee that is not eligible for a severance payment, can still get the severance portion of their retirement insurance when quitting (this is usually referred to as “Section 14” (סעיף 14 – Seif Arba-Esre). This option is mandatory for all workers who started working in 2014 and after.

An employee that was fired ([and in some specific cases also an employee that quit](https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/%D7%A4%D7%99%D7%A6%D7%95%D7%99%D7%99_%D7%A4%D7%99%D7%98%D7%95%D7%A8%D7%99%D7%9D_%D7%9C%D7%A2%D7%95%D7%91%D7%93_%D7%A9%D7%94%D7%AA%D7%A4%D7%98%D7%A8)), is entitled by law to severance pay. Since 2014, employers are obligated to deposit a big part of the severance payment in advance, to the retirement insurance plan of the employee (see above).

The severance payment is calculated as follows: a number of months worked \* 8.33% of **your last salary** (unless your salary decreased).

Nowadays, a big part of the severance payment is already deposited monthly by the employer to your retirement insurance (see above). The usual monthly deposit done by the employer is 6% of your salary. That leaves us **with a gap of 2.33% that the employer will have to complete to your severance pay.**

**For example** – an employee’s last salary was 10,000 NIS, and they had been working for 15 months. They will be entitled to get 15 (months) \* 233 NIS (2.33% of last salary) = 3,495 NIS as one-time severance payment (in addition to the severance money that is already deposited in your retirement insurance plan, that you will be able to withdraw).

\*To clarify – an employee that did not get fired, will not be eligible for this extra 2.33%, they will only be able to withdraw the Pizuim portion of their retirement insurance and nothing else. This additional sum is only relevant to employees who were fired or quit in special circumstances (see the link above), or when the employer agreed to add the extra 2.33% as a “perk” in the employment contract (called “Hesder Meitiv” הסדר מיטיב).

For any working month in which a 6% deposit was not made, the employee will be eligible for the full 8.33% of their last salary, to be added to their severance pay. For example, if an employer started depositing the 6% severance portion only after 6 months of work, when fired, the employee will be eligible to get severance pay for these months too.

This is a very general and non-specific explanation of severance payment and eligibility. To find out about your specific case, visit the Kol Zechut [severance pay knowledge base](https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/%D7%A4%D7%99%D7%A6%D7%95%D7%99%D7%99_%D7%A4%D7%99%D7%98%D7%95%D7%A8%D7%99%D7%9D).
