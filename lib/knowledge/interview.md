# The interview

The interview is the office's main way to check that your relationship is
genuine. Each of you answers questions about your daily life, your partner
and your relationship, and the answers are compared. The office sometimes
calls it a hearing (שימוע).

## When there's one

- **Common-law couples:** before the first B/1 visa, once you're both in
  Israel. Ask for it at the appointment where you show the original documents
  (5.2.0009, clause 10, page 16); otherwise the office sets a date, which at
  busy branches can be up to a year away. See
  [first-appointment.md](first-appointment.md).
- **Married couples:** not before the B/1. The first interview comes before
  the A/5 visa.
- **A simultaneous interview** (ראיון סימולטני), when the foreign partner is
  still abroad: the Israeli partner at the branch, the foreign partner at the
  Israeli consulate where they live, at the same time. It's sometimes asked
  for before the entry permit.
- **Both abroad:** an interview at the consulate, for the entry permit,
  doesn't replace the interview in Israel. You go through it after you arrive.
- Later, there's an interview at the renewals too.

## How it works

Interviews follow procedure 5.1.0013, the Population and Immigration
Authority's procedure for conducting interviews.

- **Separately.** Even when it's called a shared interview, you're usually
  questioned one at a time, while the other waits outside. That's normal.
- **The language:** usually English for the foreign partner and Hebrew for the
  Israeli partner. If either of you isn't fluent in these, tell the office as
  early as you can, preferably months ahead. The office arranges a translator,
  sometimes by phone, or a clerk who speaks your language. You can't bring
  your own translator.
- **Someone with you:** the procedure lets you bring a person to accompany
  you, a lawyer for example.
- **The record:** the clerk types your answers, in Hebrew. At the end you're
  asked to sign it. If you don't read Hebrew, ask the clerk to translate it
  for you before you sign.
- **The questions** are about your daily life, how you met, your families,
  your friends, your work and money, and your plans. Expect, for example, how
  you met, your partner's family members and what they do, a typical day and
  weekend, your last holiday together, your home, your bank accounts, and the
  names and phone numbers of friends you share (you can check your phone).

## Tips

- **Tell the truth.** The clerks are good at catching lies, and a lie can
  sink a genuine file.
- **It's fine not to remember.** Say you aren't sure, rather than guess: a
  wrong answer stated firmly does more harm than "I don't remember".
- **Give enough detail.** "A green dress with flowers" matches your partner's
  "a floral dress" better than "a green dress" does.
- **Answer what you're asked,** slowly and clearly, so the clerk can write it
  down.
- **Bring documents.** Bring the center-of-life documents and the evidence of
  your relationship that you've gathered since you last sent documents,
  unless the office told you otherwise.

## After it

The interview is usually the last step before the decision. Some offices
decide on the spot, others take weeks or months; ask the clerk when to
expect it, and write to the office if you've heard nothing after 3 weeks. If
the application is refused, ask for the interview record: the office gives
it only to those who ask.
