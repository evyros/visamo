# The foreign partner's children moving to Israel

"Accompanying minor" (קטין נלווה) in 5.2.0008: a child under 18 from the
foreign partner's previous relationship whose status is being arranged with
them. Children who stay behind don't add documents.

## Documents (§ד.2.י, §ה.2(9))

- **Birth certificate**, original, certified, translated if needed; lists
  both parents; 2 copies (AS/6).
- **Passport**, valid for at least 2 more years.
- **Police certificate for each child aged 14 or older.**
- **The other parent**, depending on their situation:
  - **Alive and with parental rights:** both of these (practice):
    - **a consent letter signed by the other parent**, agreeing to the move;
    - **an official document with the other parent's current address**, since
      the ministry also contacts them itself, through the consul when they're
      abroad (by registered mail when they're in Israel; then no address
      document is needed).
  - **Sole custody:** the original, certified court ruling, **and proof the
    child actually lives with the parent** (§ה.2(9)י).
  - **Died:** the other parent's death certificate (not confirmed by a source).
  - **Not listed on the birth certificate:** nothing more.

## When the other parent doesn't reply

- **The app follows practice: no reply counts as an objection** (practice).
- **The procedure's text says the opposite.** 5.2.0008 (edition 16)
  §ה.2(9)ו: "ככל שלא מתקבלת תגובת ההורה הנוסף בתוך 6 חודשים ממועד שליחת ההודעה -
  ייחשב הדבר כהיעדר התנגדותו", meaning no reply within 6 months counts as no
  objection.
- While waiting (6 months), the child can get a B/2 permit, not A/5.
- If the other parent objects to the move, the child's status isn't upgraded
  to A/5. If they object only to citizenship, the child gets A/5 but not
  citizenship while under 18.
- The consent document's description tells couples that no reply within 6
  months counts as an objection.

## Skipping the wait for the ministry's contact

The wait for the ministry to reach an other parent abroad through the consul
can be avoided (practice), if the other parent gives their consent in one of
these ways:

- **At the Israeli consulate:** they go to the consul and sign a declaration
  that they consent.
- **Through a local notary:** they sign a consent in front of a notary where
  they live, and the document is apostilled (or legalized, in a country
  outside the Apostille Convention; see certification.md).

The consent document's description tells couples about both.

## School records

The AS/6 checklist asks for school confirmations and report cards "of
children in the education system from age 6". Neither source says whose
children. The procedure (§ה.2(7)) says the office may check with the
Ministry of Education as part of the center-of-life check. So it's evidence
the household lives in Israel: **any child living with the couple in
Israel**. In our data that's the couple's children together, and the foreign
partner's children who are moving. The Israeli's own children from before
would count too, but onboarding doesn't ask about them. Agreed with the
product owner; not confirmed by a source.

## Onboarding's option wording

"The other parent agrees to the move" stays. Consent is really required
(practice), even though the ministry also contacts the parent.
