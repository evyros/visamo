# Partner Visa Recommendation Letters Explained

Recommendation letters are one of the crucial documents to present when applying for a partner visa in Israel. They are one of the main pieces of evidence that PIBA checks when determining the honesty of a relationship between an Israeli and a foreigner.

Recommendation letters raise a lot of questions among couples that are initially applying for (or renewing) a partner visa in Israel. What exactly does a recommendation letter have to say? How long should it be? What should it look like?

In this article, we’ll cover all of these and other frequently asked questions about Israeli partner visa recommendation letters. Let’s dive right in!

## What is a recommendation letter and why is it necessary?

Recommendation letters are short letters written by family members and/or friends of the couple applying for a partner visa in Israel. They are used as proof that the relationship between the Israeli and a foreigner is real and honest.

Based on an honest relationship, the foreign partner has a right to get legal status in Israel. Initially, they will be issued a B1 General visa that will allow them to work in Israel. At a later stage, it will lead to Israeli residency and citizenship.

To better understand the whole Israeli partner visa process, please visit: [“The Gradual Process – A Partner Visa in Israel – an Overview”](https://aic.org.il/the-gradual-process-birds-eye-view/).

## Who can write a recommendation letter?

PIBA only accepts recommendation letters written by Israeli citizens.

It is recommended to include “different types” of people to write recommendation letters for you – for example, family members (close and far), friends from different circles, etc.

You don’t need to add letters from non-Israeli people, such as the family of the foreign side or friends from abroad.

## What does the letter need to include?

There isn’t any set format for the letter.

According to the official procedures (5.2.0008 and 5.2.0009), the recommendation letters must include the **contact details of the writer**, and a **copy of their Israeli ID** attached.

Some PIBA branches, but not all of them, also require the **writer’s signature** on the letter.

In addition to that, make sure that the letter includes a date, the full names of the couple, and the name of the writer.

## How many letters should you submit?

PIBA is asking to submit **a minimum of 3 recommendation letters** for the initial application, from Israeli citizens who are familiar with the couple and can attest to the existence of the relationship and its honesty.

## What should the recommendation letter say?

As we mentioned earlier, **there is no prescribed format for this letter, so it should be original and reflect the writer’s own style and words**. We strongly advise against using a template or copying content.

In general, the recommender should clarify their relationship with the Israeli partner, how they know them, and their connection to the non-Israeli partner. They should mention where and when they first met you as a couple or when they first became aware of your relationship. The content of the letter will vary significantly depending on whether the foreign partner resides in Israel.

**If the foreign partner lives in Israel,** it is advisable to choose recommenders who regularly interact with you as a couple. These can include family members, friends, or colleagues. In addition to the general information, they can describe their interactions with you as a couple – when they last met you and under what circumstances (for example, “We went out to dinner last Friday” or “They came over for Passover dinner”). They can also comment on the foreign partner’s adjustment to life in Israel, their Hebrew studies, or their job situation – details that someone close to you would naturally know.

**If the foreign partner does not yet reside in Israel**, it’s best to select recommenders who are aware of your relationship, especially those who have met the foreign partner in person or virtually. This might include close friends or family who can describe when they first learned about the relationship and share details about how it came to be (for example, “they met in Colombia while \_\_\_\_ was traveling after military service”). If the recommender has met the foreign partner before, they should mention those meetings and share their impressions of the relationship based on those. They can also mention if they have seen photos of the couple together or interacted with the foreign partner via video call.

In all letters, it is recommended that the writer add their “personal impression” of the relationship, its integrity, and its honesty. For example, a writer can provide their insight by saying, “I can see that both of them are very happy with each other,” “I know they are a serious couple and plan to start their family in Israel,” or even something more straightforward like, “As their friend, I can testify that their relationship is real and sincere.” It is also recommended to include something about the couple’s life in Israel or their intentions for the future in Israel.

## What should the recommendation letter look like?

The recommendation letters must include the writer’s **contact details** and have a **copy of their Israeli ID attached**. Other than that, there’s no set format! But here are some key points to consider:

- **Handwritten vs. Printed** – The letters can be either handwritten or printed. Some branches or clerks require handwritten letters, while others prefer printed ones for easier reading. It’s unpredictable, but we recommend starting with a printed letter unless instructed otherwise.
- **Length** – There is no required length or word count. In general, a 2–3 paragraph letter should be sufficient.
- **Official vs. Freestyle** – There doesn’t seem to be an advantage to any particular style. Using sophisticated vocabulary or a formal structure is not mandatory. It’s important that the letter will be coherent and understandable.
- **Signature** – Many offices require the letter to be signed.
