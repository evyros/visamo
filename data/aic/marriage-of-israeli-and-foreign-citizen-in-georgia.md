# Marriage of Israeli and foreign citizen in Georgia

Below you can find a description of the procedure for a civil wedding in Sweden, between an Israeli citizen and a foreign citizen. As both parties of the marriage are not local to Georgia, these guidelines and recommendations can accommodate various types of international couples in Israel. Please be advised that as of December 2021, Georgia doesn’t marry homosexuals couples.

This post was written by a volunteer community member. While our volunteers do their best to share the most accurate information, there still may be errors or inaccurate details in this post. Please keep that in mind while reading!

## Why get married in Georgia?

1. Quick and easy process: Tourists can get married in one day, and there is no need to prepare any paperwork in advance. In particular, there is no need to arrange a document certifying one’s ‘single status’, that in some case can be tricky to obtain from Israel.
2. Well-connected: Georgia is only 2.5 hours by air from Israel, with several daily, non-stop flights between the two countries.
3. Budget-friendly: For travelers from Israel, Georgia is very affordable, and even those on the tightest of budgets can make it work. The marriage registration itself is free, and everything else—meals, hotels, taxi rides etc – is very economical.
4. Gorgeous country: Georgia is a beautiful, tourist-friendly country that boasts of snow-capped mountains, sandy beaches, and millennia of civilisational history. There is something here for everyone. If you want to get married in a romantic location or extend your wedding day into a weekend trip or even a mini-honeymoon, Georgia has a lot of options.

## Civil wedding in Georgia – Overview

Here’s a quick summary of the marriage procedure in Georgia:

1. Fly to Georgia with a valid passport.
2. Upon landing in Georgia, get a local number on which you can receive text messages
3. On your wedding day, go to the Public Service House (PSH) in your city.
4. Translate and notarise your passports
5. Arrange for two witnesses (you can ask the same people who notarised your passports). 
6. Submit the documents at the PSH
7. Wait for verification
8. After verification, sign the marriage document, and receive your marriage certificate.
9. Get your original marriage certificate translated into English and apostilled.

Below, you can find the detailed instruction, including some tips and recommendations!

## Step 1: Fly to Georgia

Yes, getting on a plane and flying to Georgia is, indeed, the first step of the process because there is really nothing to be done while you are in Israel/your home country.

You do not need to make any prior bookings or appointments, you do not need to send any paperwork to anyone in Georgia or get your documents verified in advance. And you certainly do not need to pay thousands of shekels to an agent.

During the peak of the Covid-19 pandemic, there was a period of time when appointments were, in fact, required to enter the ‘Public Service Hall’ in Tbilisi where the marriages are registered; but since October 25, 2021, this is no longer necessary. Couples can just walk into the building and register their marriage as before.

Importantly, there is no need for a document from your government certifying your status as a single person who is eligible to marry. This document, variously known as the ‘single certificate’ or ‘certificate of no-impediment to marriage’, is often required when marrying abroad (and also for the non-Israeli partner to get a partner visa in Israel). However, many countries do not readily issue such a document (although, Israel does—in the form of the ‘Tamzit Rishum’). This means that the non-Israeli partner usually has to spend a lot of time, money and effort to procure the ‘single certificate’ from their home country before they can get married. Georgia, however, does not require this document. 

The only document required for the marriage registration is a **valid passport**. Those who have been married before also need to present their divorce certificates.

## Step 2: Land in Georgia and buy a Georgian SIM card

You will need a local Georgian number for the marriage registration process. Each partner does not need to have their own number, but make sure you have at least one active local number on which you can receive text messages.

SIM cards can be bought at the airport for about **30 Georgian Laris** (1GEL=1.13NIS approx.) though they are possibly cheaper in the city. The kiosks at Tbilisi airport where the SIM cards are sold seem to be open 24/7. A passport is required for SIM registration. The SIM cards are already activated and ready for use as soon as you put it in your phone, but you need an unlocked phone.

## Step 3: On your wedding day, go to the Public Service Hall (PSH)

Most foreigners marry in the capital city of Tbilisi, but you can get married in any major city you like. This article is based on personal experience in Tbilisi, but the basic process is the same in all cities.

Every major city has a ‘Public Service Hall’— a large government building where a variety of bureaucratic services are offered to the public. This includes marriage registration, but also several other services like passport issuance and property registration.

All services are under one roof, which means there is no special area for marriages. There are no ceremonies here, no vows, no exchange of rings, no flowers and champagne. When your time comes, you and your partner will just sign one piece of paper in front of a clerk, that’s it. Keep this in mind when you plan your day, especially if you want to wear some sort of wedding clothes or have a ceremony.

The PSH in Tbilisi is centrally located and easy to find. It is a beautiful, modern building with a lovely riverside view. On the inside, the PSH looks like a swanky bank or a corporate office, not a rundown government building. Of course Georgian is the national language, but there is ample signage in English and most of the staff speak either English or Russian, so it’s not impossible to navigate. 

While most tourists get married in the PSH without ceremony, there are other options as well. If you want, you can have a ceremony of your choice at a different location, and arrange for a government official to be at your venue for the paper-signing.

## Step 4: Translating and notarizing your passports

This is the only significant paperwork that needs to be done for the marriage registration. You can do it on the day of the wedding or earlier if you have the time.

Right outside the PSH in Tbilisi, there are dozens of ‘agents’ just standing around, waiting for clients. They will approach you and you can pick whoever you like. Russian-speakers are most easily available, but there are English-speaking agents as well. The vast majority of them work for the notary-publics whose offices are located right across the street from the PSH.

Once you have selected an agent, they will take you to their notary office where your passports will be translated and notarized on the spot. This should not take more than 30 minutes to an hour, depending on how busy the office is on that day.  
  
**Cost for translation and notarization of passports: 50 GEL**

## Step 5: Finding two witnesses

You will need a total of two witnesses who will co-sign the marriage document. These witnesses can be anybody at all—your friends, family members, staff members of your hotel, your tour guide, your agent or notary public, or even a passer-by from the street. They do not need to know you or your partner personally, and they may be Georgian or non-Georgian citizens. If they are foreign citizens, then their passports will also have to be translated and notarized (as in Step 4). Georgia citizens only need to present their national ID.

Unless you already have in mind specific individuals you want as witnesses, it is recommended to just let your agent arrange the witnesses. Your agent will be with you throughout the process, and just before the final paper-signing, s/he will bring in a friend or colleague and together they will be your two witnesses.

**Witness fee: 50 GEL per witness.**

## Step 6: Submit your documents

Now that your documents are ready, your agent will take you back across the street to the PSH. At the PSH, get a token and wait your turn. When your turn comes, submit your translated and notarised documents to the clerk. At this time, you will also be asked to sign one document in Georgian, stating that you are single and eligible to marry. The bride will also be asked if she wishes to change her family name. Whatever she chooses will be mentioned in the marriage certificate. The whole process shouldn’t take more than 30 minutes to an hour, depending on how busy the office is on that day, and your agent should be with you the whole time to help.

## Step 7: Verification

After submission, your documents will be verified. This usually takes about 3 hours, giving you ample time to get a nice lunch or even go back to your hotel nearby to get some rest.

After your documents have been verified, you will receive an SMS on your Georgian number. This means you are ready to be married. Ideally, you want to be back at the PSH by the time you receive this SMS because you are already in the second half of the work day and the PSH closes around 6pm.

As soon as you receive the SMS, take another token and wait your turn to be called to the clerk. If you are running short on time or if it’s taking longer than usual to get the SMS, talk to your agent and see if s/he can push things along a bit. Also, you can take the token before you get the SMS; just make sure you have the SMS by the time your turn comes.

## Step 8: Sign the marriage papers

When your turn comes, go to the clerk with your two witnesses, show her/him the SMS, sign the papers, and you are married!

You will be given two original marriage certificates in Georgian right away. The marriage registration process itself is free of cost.

You should be able to complete Steps 2 to 8 within 24 hours, assuming you land in Tbilisi in the wee hours of the night and start your day early enough. However, if you want, you can also break up the process and do it over two days.

## Step 9: Translate and apostille your marriage certificate

After the paper signing, give the marriage certificates to your agent for them to be translated into English and apostilled. The documents can be picked up the next day if you pay for express service, or you can save some money and pick them up a few days later.

**Cost of translation and apostille: 175GEL for one set** (this was the one-day rate).
