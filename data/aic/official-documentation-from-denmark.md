# Official Documentation from Denmark

This post was written by a volunteer community member. While our volunteers do their best to share the most accurate information, there still may be errors or inaccurate details in this post. Please keep that in mind while reading!

This post includes detailed instructions on how to obtain and authenticate the official documentation and certifications from abroad, that are needed to apply for a partner or spouse visa in Israel. The basic personal documents you will be looking for are birth certificate, certificate of non-criminal record, current and/or previous personal status certificate (martial status), and name change certificate. Complete documentation about the foreign partner from their home country is prerequisite of the gradual process, and without it one cannot start the partner visa process and get a status in Israel based on their relationship.

## Birth Certificate from Denmark

Name in Denmark: Fødsels- og dåbsattest (baptism and birth certificate) or personattest (certificate of personal data)

**How to obtain?**

In Denmark, the exact document that you need depends on whether you were baptized or not.

If you were baptized, you need the fødsels- og dåbsattest (baptism and birth certificate).

If you were not baptized, you need the personattest (certificate of personal data).

You can get both documents online with a digital signature on [Borger.dk](https://www.borger.dk/Handlingsside?selfserviceId=fcc8eb1f-f962-4d3b-954d-758a1489caa1), but since PIBA may be not too fond of digital documents, you can contact the church office (kirkekontor) of the Danish parish (sogn) you are currently living in if your official address is still in Denmark. If your official address is abroad, you can contact the church office of the last Danish parish that you lived in.

Call or email the relevant church office to set up a time where you can pick up your fødsels- og dåbsattest or personattest in English. Make sure that the document comes with a wet/physical signature and the name of the person who signed it. It is required in order to get a physical Apostille stamp on the document.

**Cost:** Both documents are **free of charge.**

**Links: [Link 1](https://www.borger.dk/Handlingsside?selfserviceId=fcc8eb1f-f962-4d3b-954d-758a1489caa1)** and **[Link 2](https://www.borger.dk/Handlingsside?selfserviceId=7c9db71f-960f-44d0-a3d3-c9b790c6c6a8)**

## Name change certificate from Denmark

\* Please note – Name change certificate is not necessary if you did not change your first / last name.

Name in Denmark: Bopælsattest (residence certificate)

**How to obtain?**

You can either order the certificate in English on [Borger.dk](https://www.borger.dk/Handlingsside?selfserviceId=61228215-fdea-4454-8276-b080539350f5) or go to any citizen service center (Borgerservice) in Denmark to get the document physically. If you get the document online, you need to go to a citizen service center (Borgerservice) to get a wet/physical signature and the name of the person signing it on it as it is a requirement to get a physical Apostille stamp on the document.

Wherever you get the document, make sure your name history (navnehistorik) appears on it. Then, the document will show your current and previous names and the dates of the name changes.

**Cost: The document costs DKK 75.**

[**Link**](https://www.borger.dk/Handlingsside?selfserviceId=61228215-fdea-4454-8276-b080539350f5)

## Personal status certificate from Denmark

Name in Denmark: Personattest (certificate of personal data) or bopælstattest (residence certificate)

**How to obtain?**

To show whether you are single or married, you can use the personattest (certificate of personal data) or the bopælstattest (residence certificate).

See how to get the documents further up on this page.

The personattest (certificate of personal data) will automatically either say “unwed” or “married”.

When you order the bopælstattest (residence certificate), make sure your marital status (civilstand) appears on the document. It will either say “single” or “married.”. If you are married, your wedding date will appear on the document and you can choose to add the name and birthdate of your spouse.

**Costs:** The personattest (certificate of personal data) is **free of charge**, while the bopælstattest (residence certificate) costs **DKK 75**.

**Link: [Link 1](https://www.borger.dk/Handlingsside?selfserviceId=7c9db71f-960f-44d0-a3d3-c9b790c6c6a8)** and **[Link 2](https://www.borger.dk/Handlingsside?selfserviceId=61228215-fdea-4454-8276-b080539350f5)**

## Certificate of Non-Criminal Record from Denamrk

Name in Denmark: Straffeattest (certificate of criminal record)

**How to obtain?**

You can get your straffeattest (certificate of criminal record) in English online on [Borger.dk](https://www.borger.dk/Handlingsside?selfserviceId=862067d1-74e0-4109-8a28-01a994310049), but since Misrad Hapnim is not too fond of digital documents, you can show up at any Danish police station and ask for the document in English.

Make sure that the document comes with a wet/physical signature and the name of the person who signed the document. It is required in order to get a physical Apostille stamp on the document.

**Cost:** The document is **free** of charge.

## Verifying the documents with apostille

All official documentation from abroad that needs to be presented in Israel, must be verified by the country of its origin in order to be valid outside of it, in Israel. Many countries use “apostille” for this purpose. An apostille is usually a document, issued by an authorized authority which authenticates the documents in question.  This is used to demonstrate that they are valid documents that can be recognized in other countries.

### Obtaining apostille in Denmark

**How to obtain?**

You can either go to the Danish Ministry of Foreign Affairs in Copenhagen physically or send your document to them by mail.

If you go there physically, you do not need an appointment. You can show up at the Danish Ministry of Foreign Affairs on Asiatisk Plads 2 in Copenhagen Monday to Friday 9.00-12.00 and Tuesdays and Thursdays 13.30-15.30.

You can get up to 7 documents stamped on the spot. If you have more documents, you can pick them up stamped on the following working day.

If you choose to send your document to the Danish Ministry of Foreign Affairs, you need to pay for the stamp [here](https://dys.um.dk/Shop/purchase) and follow the steps. Send the letter to the following address:  
  
Udenrigsministeriets Legalisering  
Asiatisk Plads 2  
1448 København K

In order to get a physical Apostille stamp on your document, it must come with a wet/physical signature and the name of the person who signed the document.

**Cost:** An Apostille stamp costs **DKK 210**.
