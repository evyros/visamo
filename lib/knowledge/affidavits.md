# Making an affidavit in Israel

What an affidavit is, who can confirm one and when it's accepted: see
[certification.md](certification.md). This is how to make one, and what it
costs.

## How to make one

1. **Make sure you understand it.** You must understand what your affidavit
   says. If it isn't in a language you read, have it translated for you
   before you go.

   **Which language.** Write it in Hebrew and English side by side, so both
   you and the clerk can read it. An affidavit in English only can be
   accepted, but it's up to the clerk who handles your file. If it's in
   Hebrew only and you don't read Hebrew, the lawyer translates it for you
   before you sign, and adds a line to the affidavit saying they did.
2. **Write it, or fill in the details, but leave the signature empty.** Never
   sign it on your own: you sign only in front of whoever confirms it.
3. **Go to someone who can confirm it**: any Israeli lawyer, the magistrate's
   court (no appointment needed: walk in during opening hours) or, if it has
   to be notarial, a notary.
4. **Show who you are**, with an identity document: an Israeli ID (תעודת
   זהות) or a passport.
5. **You're warned to tell the truth**, and that not doing so is punishable
   by law. You may be asked to read the statement, or to confirm you know
   what it says.
6. **Sign it in front of them.**
7. **They fill in the confirmation** at the bottom with their details, then
   sign and stamp it. Your affidavit is ready.

## What it costs

- **A lawyer who agrees to do it for free:** nothing.
- **The magistrate's court**, at the secretariat or through the duty lawyer:
  a fixed fee of 49 NIS per signature.
- **A private lawyer:** usually 200–250 NIS, plus VAT.
- **A notarial affidavit:** 200 NIS for the first affiant and 80 NIS for
  each additional one, plus VAT.

These prices are only for confirming the signature. Writing the affidavit,
its wording, preparing documents or any other legal service can cost more.
