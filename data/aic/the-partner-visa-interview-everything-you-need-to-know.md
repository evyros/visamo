# The Partner Visa Interview: Everything You Need To Know

A shared interview is one of the main (and some would say, scary) steps on the way to a partner visa in Israel.

The interview is the main tool in the hands of PIBA to “test” the integrity and honesty of the relationship. By asking various personal questions, comparing the couple’s answers, and looking into contradictions that might indicate dishonesty, PIBA clerks can get a direct impression of the couple and their relationship.

When both partiers are together in Israel, the interview will take place in person at the PIBA office. When the foreign partner is abroad, a simultaneous interview may be required as an additional step – with the Israeli partner at their PIBA office and the foreign partner at the Israeli embassy abroad, simultaneously.

## Partner visa interview: an overview

Interviews, that are also referred to by PIBA as “hearings” (שימועים), are a crucial part of the partner visa process in Israel, that all applicants will go through. An interview is required at the beginning of the process, and then annually, for every visa renewal.

All interviews done by PIBA are subject to [procedure 5.1.0013 – “Procedure for conducting interviews”](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/policy/making_interviews_procedure/he/5.1.0013.pdf). The procedure dictates the rules and instructions for conducting interviews at PIBA. For example, it states that an interviewee is allowed to have a person accompany them, and it gives instructions about the presence of a translator, and about the documentation of the interview by the clerk.

Note: According to procedure 5.2.0008, married couples are eligible for a B1 partner visa without an interview. The first joint interview is required only before receiving the A5 visa. Check our [step-by-step guide on getting a partner visa](https://aic.org.il/partner-visa-in-israel-step-by-step-guide-to-your-first-visa/) for more information.

## Before the Interview

As you wait for your partner visa interview, there are a few things to consider and prepare for:

### The language of the interview

Interviews are typically conducted in English or Hebrew (English for the foreign partner, Hebrew for the Israeli partner). If either of you is not fluent in these languages, inform PIBA as soon as possible -preferably a few months in advance. PIBA will arrange for a translator (you cannot bring your own) or assign a specific clerk to conduct the interview in the preferred language.

Some PIBA clerks speak additional languages, such as Russian or French, allowing interviews to be conducted in those languages.

In some cases, PIBA may provide translation services over the phone. In these instances, a live translator is connected to the interview via phone. The translator listens to the conversation happening in the room and translates it in real time.

### Shared vs. separate interviews

Despite sometimes being referred to as “shared interviews”, partner visa interviews are usually conducted separately. If done separately, one person is asked to wait outside of the room while the other side is being questioned. This is a normal procedure and you shouldn’t feel alarmed by it.

### Interview documentation

Nowadays, the interview protocol is usually typed in by the clerk on a computer while the paper documentation form is less frequently used. Before beginning the interview, the clerk might read out a standard statement about the way the interview will be conducted and you will have to confirm that you understand.

## Tips on how to prepare and succeed in the Interview

Here are some things to keep in mind in preparation for your interview:

1. **Try to relax**. The interviewers are people, and they are there to do their job. They are doing dozens of interviews every week. It’s very unlikely that someone has a personal grudge against you.
2. **Don’t lie.** A self-explanatory tip. PIBA is good at catching lies. Don’t jeopardize your chances by lying in the shared interview.
3. **It’s ok to not remember something**. This isn’t a memory test. You are not expected to have inhuman memory. If you’re not sure about something, **say so**! You can say that you are not sure, or that you don’t remember. Expressing doubt is better than giving a straight answer that may be wrong.
4. **Speak slowly and clearly** to allow the interviewer to document your answers accurately.
5. **Speak to the point**. Answer what you’re asked about precisely. There’s no need to elaborate when it’s not required.
6. At the same time, **give enough details when necessary**. Providing more information about something you’re asked about, will give you and your partner a higher chance to match your answers.

For example – When asked “What was your partner wearing when you went out last night?”, you can respond with “a green dress”. But it would be better to respond with “a green dress with flowers on it”. This way, when your partner answers “a floral dress”, your answers would match.

7\. **Know what** **to expect** – In a shared community effort, we’ve gathered dozens of actual interview questions that real couples were asked during interviews! You don’t need to memorize them all or prepare coordinated answers, but it will help you get the general picture of what to expect.

## After the Interview

At the end of the interview, the interviewer will ask you to sign the protocol, which includes your written answers. Since the interview is documented by the clerk in Hebrew, if you don’t speak or read Hebrew, you can request that the interviewer translate the documentation form for you before signing.

The interview is usually the final step before PIBA decides on your application. Some offices may provide a decision “on the spot,” while others might take several weeks or even months. The clerk should be able to tell you when to expect a decision. We suggest contacting your attending office if you haven’t received a decision after 3 weeks.

Please note: In the event of a negative decision on your partner visa application, the interview protocol will only be provided **upon request**. PIBA will not share the documentation unless you specifically ask to see it

Today I did the interview with my partner in Hadera. I’m sharing my experience for the benefit of others.  
Firstly, we had hired a lawyer for the process. Our experience was very bad, with a very inprofessional person, probably also at his first experience with this type of task.  
Today I asked the lawyer to accompany us to the interview, but I can say that it was definitely bit required. I cannot say if the lawyer was helpful in the everything that was done prior to the interview, but I have the feeling that in our case it was not worth it, considering the fee he charged.  
The interview took roughly one hour per person. I was the first one being questioned. The woman was very nice, smiley and never aggressive or not polite.  
She read questions from a printed questionnaire that I had to sign at the end of the interview. Some of the questions I recall:  
– A detailed description of how we met (very 1st question)  
– Name, job and address (city) of my gf family members.  
– Name and phone numbers of three common friends (you can use your phone, you don’t have to remember them by heart thankfully 😂)  
– Last holiday together  
– Name of all my family members and some personal details  
– Our job and salary  
– Description of a typical day, for both. I’ve also described weekends.  
– If either of us owns any property  
– What bank account(s) we have in Israel  
– Our hobbies  
– Our relationship with each other’s family  
– Times we spent apart since I moved to Israel

On a general note I can say that, at least in my case, there’s nothing to worry about. As long as you are sincere and you speak the truth, it’s a very basic interview. I personally feel much more stressed at the airport, when I’m asked all sort of questions and I feel treated like a criminal. Here the experience was absolutely positive. With lots of moments of laughter. But probably it depends on who you r interviewer is.
