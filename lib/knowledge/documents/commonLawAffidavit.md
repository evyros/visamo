# Joint affidavit of both partners (תצהיר משותף של בני הזוג)

A declaration in the ministry's wording for couples applying on the basis of living together: that the details are true, that you'll report any change, and statements about each of you. Both of you sign it at a Misrad Hapnim office.

For common-law couples (ידועים בציבור), the application under procedure
5.2.0009 comes with its own affidavit, titled **בקשתי בהתאם לנוהל בני זוג על
סמך חיים משותפים - תצהיר** ("my application under the procedure for partners
based on a shared life – affidavit"). It isn't part of form AS/6. It's one
declaration for both of you: the Israeli partner and the foreign partner sign
the same page.

## How to get it

The form is on the Population and Immigration Authority's site
(https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/generalpage/visas_forms/he/5.2.0009_a.pdf).
It's in Hebrew, so it needs no translation.

1. Print it, and fill in your details: each partner's full name, the Israeli
   partner's ID number and the foreign partner's passport number. Write the
   reason for any statement you can't declare in the lines under the list.
2. **Don't sign it yet, and don't send it with the online application.** Bring
   it to your appointment at the Population and Immigration Authority office
   (לשכת רשות האוכלוסין וההגירה).
3. At the appointment, both of you sign it in front of the clerk, who
   confirms your signatures on the form.

## What it should include

- **The 13 statements**, as printed on the form. By signing, you declare
  that:
  1. the details given in the application are true, and are the basis for
     reviewing it;
  2. you'll report to the Population and Immigration Authority any change in
     your circumstances while the application is handled, and during the
     graduated procedure if it's approved;
  3. you intend to settle in Israel;
  4. you aren't addicted to hard drugs or alcohol;
  5. you don't have a mental illness;
  6. you don't have a disease that could endanger public health in Israel;
  7. you haven't committed a criminal offense (other than traffic offenses),
     there's no arrest warrant against you, and you aren't wanted by the
     police or security authorities of any country;
  8. you haven't acted against the Jewish people or the security of the
     State of Israel;
  9. you haven't been a member or a supporter, directly or indirectly,
     including financially, of a body, organization or movement acting
     against the State of Israel, its residents or its citizens;
  10. you've never had a deportation or removal order from Israel, and no
      authority has asked you to leave Israel;
  11. you have to tell the authority where you live, and about any change of
      address. The authority may make further checks of the application and
      its credibility, including a visit to your home, and not cooperating
      fully can be a reason to close the application and to cancel, or not
      renew, the foreign partner's permit;
  12. false or incomplete details, hiding details, or not reporting a change
      in your circumstances can lead to cancelling any status given, and to
      a removal order from Israel against the foreign partner (sections 11
      and 13 of the Entry into Israel Law, 1952);
  13. giving false details or a forged document is a criminal offense, and
      the authority may report to the Israel Police anything either of you
      does against the law.
- **An explanation for any statement you can't declare**, in the lines under
  the list ("אם אינך יכול/ה להצהיר…").
- **The Israeli partner's full name and ID number (תעודת זהות)**, and at
  the appointment, their signature and the date.
- **The foreign partner's full name and passport number**, and at the
  appointment, their signature and the date.
- **The office's confirmation of your signatures**, filled in by the clerk at
  the appointment: their name, role, the date and their signature.

## Questions

**Do we send it with the online application?**
No. It isn't sent with the online application. Bring it with you, filled in but
unsigned, to your appointment.

**Can we sign it at home before the appointment?**
No. You sign it at the appointment, in front of the clerk, who confirms your
signatures. If you already signed it, print a new copy and fill it in again.

**Do we also need the AS/6 affidavits?**
No. The affidavits in form AS/6 are for married couples. Common-law couples
sign this declaration instead.

**What if one of us can't declare one of the statements?**
Write the details or the reason in the lines under the list.

**Can a lawyer confirm our signatures instead?**
The form has a place only for a clerk (פקיד) at a Population and Immigration
Authority office to confirm the signatures.

**What happens if something in it isn't true?**
False or incomplete details, hiding details, or not reporting a change can
lead to cancelling any status given and to a removal order against the
foreign partner. Giving false details or a forged document is a criminal
offense.
