# Proof of the name change (אישור על שינוי השם)

An official document linking the foreign partner's previous name to their current one.

If the foreign partner's first or family name ever changed: a public
document showing the change (5.2.0008 §ד.2.ה). It links the name on their
older documents, such as the birth certificate, to the name they have
today.

## How to get it

It comes from the authority that registered the change, in the country
where it was made. Depending on how the name changed, that's usually:

- **By marriage:** the marriage certificate of that marriage.
- **By divorce, going back to a former name:** a divorce decree that
  restores the name.
- **By choice:** a court order, or a name-change certificate from the civil
  registry.

### Brazil

A name changed by marriage shows on the marriage certificate (certidão de
casamento); after a divorce, on the marriage certificate annotated with it
(averbada).

### Czech Republic

There's no name-change certificate. Use an extract from the population
register (výpis z registru obyvatel) listing the previous names, or the
marriage certificate if the name changed by marriage.

### Denmark

A residence certificate (bopælsattest) with the name history
(navnehistorik), which lists the current and previous names and the dates of
each change.

### Japan

The family register (戸籍全部事項証明): both the old one, from before the
change, and the new one.

### Mexico

A Mexican's legal name doesn't change by marriage, so there's usually nothing
to bring: the name stays as on the birth certificate.

### Russia

A certificate of change of name (свидетельство о перемене имени), from the
civil registry office (ЗАГС).

### Ukraine

A certificate of change of name (свідоцтво про зміну імені), from the civil
registry office. A certificate issued before 24 August 1991 can only get an
apostille on a notarized copy.

### United Kingdom

A deed poll (change of name deed). For an apostille, it has to be certified
by a UK solicitor or notary, unless one of them witnessed it. An enrolled
deed poll, recorded at the Royal Courts of Justice, is also a public record of
the change.

### United States

A certified copy of the court order, from the clerk of the court that
granted it. An order from a state court gets its apostille from that state;
one from a federal court is first authenticated by the US Department of
Justice.

## What it should include

- **Both names:** the name before the change, and the name after it.
- **Every change.** If the name changed more than once, bring a document for
  each, so they link the name at birth to today's name.
- **The certification** of the country that issued it: an apostille, or
  consular legalization if that country isn't in the Apostille Convention
  (see [certification.md](../certification.md)).
- **A certified translation**, unless it's in Hebrew or Arabic. In practice,
  an English document is usually accepted without one, but the clerk can
  still ask you to translate it.

## Questions

**My partner took their former spouse's name when they married. What do we bring?**
The marriage certificate of that marriage: it shows the name they took. If
they went back to their former name after the divorce, the divorce decree
or a separate document showing that change.

**My partner's name changed more than once. Is one document enough?**
No. Bring a document for each change, so together they link the name at
birth to the name they have today.

**Does the police certificate also need to show the old name?**
Yes. By the procedure, the police certificate covers every name the foreign
partner has had (5.2.0008, the definitions).
