# Visiting Israel as a tourist

This is for a visit only: the foreign partner comes to Israel as a tourist,
and goes home at the end of it, without starting the process while they're
here. If you mean to start the process during this visit, it isn't a visit:
see [entry-permit.md](entry-permit.md).

## Why visitors are refused

No foreigner has an automatic right to enter Israel, and an approved ETA-IL
doesn't guarantee entry: border control decides at the border. Having an
Israeli partner isn't a reason to refuse entry, but border control may
suspect the visitor means to stay, or to start the partner process here.
That suspicion, fear of settling (חשש להשתקעות), is one of the main reasons
visitors are refused. It isn't only about partners: a visitor coming to see
family in Israel can be suspected the same way.

The risk is higher for a citizen of a non-Western country, on a first
visit, after frequent or long visits, for a young visitor, and with weak
ties at home (no job, studies, lease or home there).

## Two ways to come

- **On their own,** with what they need to travel: a citizen of a
  visa-exempt country flies with an ETA-IL (the electronic travel
  authorization), and a citizen of a country that needs a visa to Israel
  gets a B/2 visa at the Israeli consulate where they live. They carry proof
  that it's a visit (below).
- **On a tourist invitation:** the Israeli partner asks Misrad Hapnim to
  approve the visit in advance (below).

You don't have to ask Misrad Hapnim to approve the visit in advance, even
after frequent visits, or after border control gave them a shorter stay
than usual. It's required only after a refusal. But the tourist invitation
is the safer way, especially when several of the risks above apply: the
visit is approved before the foreign partner flies, instead of decided at
the border.

## Showing it's a visit

The foreign partner carries reliable proof that they're coming for a visit
and going home, to show border control if they're stopped and asked about
their visit:

- **A return ticket,** for a date within the time they're asking to stay.
- **Where they're staying:** a hotel booking, or the address and contact
  details of whoever hosts them.
- **Proof of their ties at home:** a letter from their employer, proof of
  studies, a lease or a home they own, family they live with or care for.
- **A letter from the Israeli partner,** printed and signed: their full
  name, ID number and phone number, with a copy of their Teudat Zehut; how
  you know each other, where the visitor is staying, the purpose of the
  visit, and when they're leaving. Hand it over when it's asked for, or if
  they're taken for further questioning, not straight away.
- **Honest, consistent answers** at border control: why they're coming, for
  how long, and when they're going back. Saying they're visiting their
  partner is fine. Lying to border control is a reason to refuse entry, and
  can bring a ban on entering Israel for years.

**Repeat visits.** Coming often, leaving and coming back soon after, or long
stays in a row raise suspicion on the next visit. Leave time between
visits, and always leave before the stay border control gave runs out.

**Flights.** Without an entry permit, some airlines won't fly a foreigner to
Israel without a return ticket.

## If entry is refused at the border

The foreign partner is flown back, and should be given a paper with the
decision. Until the flight, they may be held at the detention facility at
Ben Gurion airport (מתקן יהלו"ם). The flight back is often within hours, so
an appeal made while they're still in Israel has to be made at once.

They have 30 days to appeal to the Appeals Tribunal (בית הדין לעררים), even
if they got no paper. The fee is 774 NIS (2026), and the appeal needs an
affidavit signed before a lawyer. The Authority can go on with the removal
after you appeal, unless the tribunal orders it to stop. You can still start
the partner process.

## The tourist invitation

Misrad Hapnim's approval of a visit in advance. It's the safer way to visit
(above), and it's required in two cases:

- **After a refusal at the border,** every later visit needs approval in
  advance: a visa, or a tourist invitation.
- **If the ETA-IL is refused,** the visitor can't board a flight to Israel:
  they need a B/2 visa from the Israeli consulate where they live, or a
  tourist invitation.

How it works:

- The Israeli partner applies at the visa department of their branch, at
  least 21 working days before the visit. Couples ask for it themselves: it
  doesn't need a lawyer.
- Some offices say a visitor from a visa-exempt country doesn't need one,
  since they can get an ETA-IL. The Israeli partner is entitled to ask for
  it anyway: an ETA-IL doesn't guarantee entry.
- The office may ask for a deposit, to guarantee the visitor leaves on time.
  It's returned once they leave. In practice it's often around 15,000 NIS:
  see [deposit.md](deposit.md) for how it's paid.
- It's a separate request from the entry permit, though both are called an
  "invitation" (הזמנה).

**Never start the process after a tourist invitation.** A tourist invitation
is only for a visit. With it, you told Misrad Hapnim the visit was
temporary, and you may have left a deposit that the foreign partner leaves
on time. Filing during that visit looks like an attempt to deceive the
office: it expects the process to start while the foreign partner is abroad.
It harms your credibility for the rest of the process. To come and start the
process, ask for the entry permit instead.

## Once you've filed

From the moment you file, the foreign partner can't come to Israel before
the entry permit is granted, not even for a visit: see
[application.md](application.md).
