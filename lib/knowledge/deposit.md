# The deposit

Misrad Hapnim sometimes approves a step of the process on condition that
you leave a deposit (ערבות): money, or a bank guarantee, held by the
Population and Immigration Authority. It isn't a fee. It's returned once
its conditions are met, and it's taken (forfeited, חילוט) only if they're
broken. Not every couple is asked for one, but it isn't rare.

## Why and when

- **By law:** at any stage of the application, the office may ask for a
  deposit (5.2.0008 §ג.9, 5.2.0009 §ג.6). It may also make the approval to
  start the gradual process conditional on one (§ו.2 in both procedures).
  The amount follows the Authority's general guarantees procedure, 5.1.0004.
- **In practice,** it's asked for when the office has doubts: whether the
  relationship is genuine, or whether you'll keep the conditions of the
  process, such as a shared center of life in Israel, and a relationship
  that's serious, stable and exclusive. It's asked for more often when the
  foreign partner comes from a non-Western or developing country.
- **What it's for:** to make sure the foreign partner leaves Israel if the
  office's concerns turn out to be right: the relationship ends, or turns
  out not to be genuine.
- **Usually at the start,** with the decision to approve the entry permit or
  the first visa, which says it's conditional on the deposit. Sometimes
  later, for example before the A/5, after an interview.
- **From a visa-exempt country,** the office can block the foreign partner's
  entry at the border until the deposit is in (5.1.0004 §ט.4.4).
- **Another option the office has:** when it doubts the relationship, the
  visa team head can extend the current visa by 6 months and check again at
  the end (§ו.2), instead of deciding then.

## How much

- **By the procedure,** the amount depends on who decides (5.1.0004 §ד.3):
  up to 30,000 NIS for the branch's team head, 40,000 NIS for a deputy
  branch manager, 50,000 NIS for the branch manager, and more from the
  regional manager or headquarters. Separate limits apply to some cases
  (§ו.2): up to 50,000 NIS for citizens of countries whose citizens tend to
  settle in Israel, up to 100,000 NIS for a couple who married abroad and
  ask to enter together, and up to 10,000 NIS for a child coming with the
  foreign partner.
- **In practice,** it's most often 30,000 NIS, and usually between 15,000
  and 40,000 NIS.
- **You can ask for a lower amount.** Write to the branch why you can't
  afford it, with payslips and bank statements. You don't need a lawyer for
  this.

## The decision letter

- **It comes in writing,** often by registered mail. If the office tells you
  a letter was sent and it hasn't arrived, go to the branch and ask for it
  by hand: the deadlines run from when you get it.
- **It says** the amount, the conditions, the deadline to pay (in practice,
  usually 21 to 30 days), and often how long the deposit is held. Keep it.
  When you ask for the deposit back, what counts is what's written in it,
  not what a clerk said.
- **If you can't pay in time,** ask the branch in writing for more time,
  before the deadline passes.
- **To challenge it,** file an internal appeal (ערר פנימי) in writing, within
  21 days of getting the decision (5.2.0008 §ג.22, 5.2.0009 §ג.18). In
  practice, the office can take months to decide, and an appeal past the
  branch, to the Appeals Tribunal (בית הדין לעררים), usually needs a lawyer.
  Many couples pay rather than wait, since the appeal holds up the step it's
  attached to. You can pay and withdraw the appeal.

## How to pay

Who pays is the guarantor (ערב): the foreign partner, the Israeli partner,
or someone else. The money goes back to the guarantor. By procedure
5.1.0004:

- **The ways to pay:** cash (a credit card counts as cash, up to 50,000
  NIS), a bank check (שיק בנקאי), or a bank guarantee (ערבות בנקאית),
  including a digital one. A personal check, a written promise or a third
  party's guarantee isn't accepted. A cash deposit comes back without
  interest or linkage to the index.
- **Cash or a bank check** go to Misrad Hapnim's Guarantees Unit at Ben
  Gurion airport (יחידת הערבויות בנתב"ג), with the decision, the guarantor's
  ID (a Teudat Zehut, an Israeli driving license or a passport), and a
  cancelled check or a bank account confirmation (אישור ניהול חשבון) for the
  Israeli account the money will go back to. The guarantor signs a consent
  form (טופס הסכמה להפקדת ערובה) and gets a copy stamped as received. In
  practice, you can do it by email instead, at Arvuyot45@piba.gov.il: send
  the decision, a copy of the foreign partner's passport, the Israeli
  partner's Teudat Zehut and the bank account confirmation, and pay the
  voucher (שובר) the unit sends back, online by credit card or at a Postal
  Bank (בנק הדואר) branch.
- **A bank guarantee** comes from your bank, and as a rule has to be valid
  for at least a year (5.1.0004 §ד.2), or as long as the decision says. The
  branch manager may require it to stay valid until the end of the gradual
  process (§ו.2). The Guarantees Unit takes it, and it's the only kind the
  branches take. Banks charge a fee for a guarantee for as long as it runs,
  and it can be high.

**After you pay,** bring the receipt or the confirmation to your branch, or
send it as the branch asks. The branch updates your file, and the step goes
on: for the entry permit, it's sent to the consulate, or the block at the
border is lifted (see [entry-permit.md](entry-permit.md)). Pay as soon as
you can: offices often move on within days of the payment.

## Getting it back

- **When:** whenever the decision says. In practice, it's often after a
  year, or once the foreign partner moves to the next stage, usually the A/5.
  Some decisions say the end of the process. If the decision gives no date,
  offices tend to keep it for the whole process. Asking before the date
  rarely works.
- **By the procedure,** once the conditions are met, the deposit is returned
  within 30 days (5.1.0004 §ט.6.2): cash to the bank account you gave, a
  credit card payment back to the same card, a bank guarantee back to the
  bank that issued it. **In practice,** getting the branch to release it is
  the slow part, and it often takes months past the date, sometimes years.

How to ask:

1. **Write to your branch,** the one that handles your file, not to the
   Guarantees Unit. In Hebrew: your names and ID numbers, the amount and
   date paid, the decision attached, and why its conditions are met. Email
   works to start, but in practice a printed letter handed in at the branch,
   with a copy stamped as received (נתקבל) and dated, works better.
2. **The branch approves the release** in its system. Ask for the approval
   in writing.
3. **The Guarantees Unit returns the money,** on its own, or after you send
   it the branch's approval or come to the unit and sign.

If the branch refuses, ask for the refusal in writing and appeal it, as
above. A complaint to Misrad Hapnim's public inquiries unit (פניות הציבור)
can also move a request that's stuck.

## If the conditions are broken

If the conditions in the decision aren't kept, for example the foreign
partner doesn't leave Israel when required after the relationship ends or
the application is refused, the deposit is forfeited. Before that, the
office sends the guarantor a warning letter (מכתב התראה), and the guarantor
has 14 days to respond (5.1.0004 §ט.7.3–ט.7.4). Forfeiting it doesn't end
the foreign partner's obligation to leave.

## Other deposits

- **A visit approved in advance** (a tourist invitation): the office may ask
  for a deposit that the visitor will leave on time (procedure 5.4.0001
  §ה.9), returned once they leave. In practice it's often around 15,000 NIS,
  paid to the same Guarantees Unit. See [entry-permit.md](entry-permit.md).
- **A birth before the foreign partner has public health insurance:** the
  hospital's deposit, see [pregnancy.md](pregnancy.md).
