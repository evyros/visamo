# Official Documentation from Japan

This post was written by a volunteer community member. While our volunteers do their best to share the most accurate information, there still may be errors or inaccurate details in this post. Please keep that in mind while reading!

This post includes detailed instructions on how to obtain and authenticate the official documentation and certifications from abroad, that are needed to apply for a partner or spouse visa in Israel. The basic personal documents you will be looking for are birth certificate, certificate of non-criminal record, current and/or previous personal status certificate (martial status), and name change certificate. Complete documentation about the foreign partner from their home country is prerequisite of the gradual process, and without it one cannot start the partner visa process and get a status in Israel based on their relationship.

## Birth Certificate from Japan

Name in Japan: Juri Shōmēshō Shusshō-Todoke 受理証明書　出生届 (Certificate of acceptance of birth registration)

**How to obtain?**

The birth certificate does not exist in Japan. Instead, you need to obtain “Juri Shōmēshō (受理証明書,　certificate of acceptance)” of “Shusshō-Todoke (出生届, birth registration)”. Only applicants, usually your father or mother, can obtain this certificate from the local municipality where they originally handed it in. The original submitter can send by mail the application document, ID, a postal money order, and a self-addressed stamped envelope to the municipality by domestic mail. They will send it back to them by mail.

Usually, they do not ship internationally. In my case, since I was in Israel and my father, who handed it in, and mother, who was handling all the documents in Japan for me, live at a different address, I asked my father to write a proxy letter to my mother to whom I asked to send the above documents as well as the proxy letter. Afterward, she sent all the documents to me by FedEx.

**Cost:** Depends on the municipality **(In my case, 350 yen = about 10 NIS)**

## Name change certificate from Japan

\* Please note – Name change certificate is not necessary if you did not change your first / last name.

Name in Japan: Koseki Zenbu-Jikō-Shōmēo　戸籍　全部事項証明 (Family registry, certificate of all records)

**How to obtain?**

Japan has a family registry system. In order to prove you are the same person, even though your family name was changed, you have to obtain both the old family registry where you belonged before the name change and the new family registry after the name change. Your first-degree family member should be able to obtain them for you at the municipality where your family registries are filed.

In my case, I asked my mother to obtain them. I prepared proxy letters just in case.

**Cost:** Depends on the municipality **(In my case, 450 yen = about 15 NIS per document)**

## Personal status certificate from Japan

Name in Japan: Dokushin Shōmēshō 独身証明書 (Single status certificate)

**How to obtain?**

You apply at the municipality where your Koseki (family registry) is filed.

In my case, I asked my mother to go to the municipality to obtain it for me, bringing my proxy letter, application, and her ID. You have to obtain this document before you hand in the marriage paper with your Israeli partner. Otherwise, they do not issue it.

**Cost:** Depends on the municipality **(In my case, 350 yen = about 10 NIS)**

**Attention!** After your marriage, you cannot request to apostille a single certificate that you obtained before your marriage. **MAKE SURE** to have it **BEFORE** you hand in your marriage paper in Japan!

### Marriage certificate

Name in Japan: Juri Shōmēshō Konin-Todoke 受理証明書　婚姻届 (Certificate of acceptance of marriage registration)

**How to obtain?**

The marriage certificate does not exist in Japan. Instead, you need to obtain “Juri Shōmēshō (受理証明書,　certificate of acceptance)” of “Konin-Todoke (婚姻届, marriage registration)”. You can get it when you hand in the marriage paper.

In my case, since I was in Israel, I asked my mother to obtain it when she handed in our marriage paper (Yes, you don’t have to be in Japan to submit a marriage paper if you’re a Japanese citizen!). I sent to her an application and a proxy letter just in case.

\*For the marriage status certificate, your family registry is sufficient, where your spouse appears.

**Cost:** Depends on the municipality **(In my case, 350 yen = about 10 NIS)**

## Certificate of Non-Criminal Record from Japan

Name in Japan: Hanzai Kērēki Shōmēshō (Certificate of Criminal Record)

**How to obtain?**

You make an appointment at the Japanese Embassy in Tel Aviv. Go there, fill in the application, and submit your fingerprints. They will get back to you about one month later. You have to go there again physically to obtain the document.

**Cost: Free**

## Verifying the documents with apostille

All official documentation from abroad that needs to be presented in Israel, must be verified by the country of its origin in order to be valid outside of it, in Israel. Many countries use “apostille” for this purpose. An apostille is usually a document, issued by an authorized authority which authenticates the documents in question.  This is used to demonstrate that they are valid documents that can be recognized in other countries.

### Obtaining apostille in Japan

**How to obtain?**

You send by mail the original documents you want to have apostilled to Gaimushō (Ministry of Foreign Affairs) together with applications and a self-addressed stamped envelope.

In my case, since I was in Israel, I asked my mother to write those applications and I wrote a proxy letter for her so that she can send them.

**Attention!** After your marriage, you cannot ask them to apostille the single certificate you obtained before your marriage. **MAKE SURE** to have it **BEFORE** you hand in your marriage paper in Japan!

**Cost: Free** (just cost of postage)
