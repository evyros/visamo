# Giving Birth as an A5 Visa Holder: The Bureaucracy and Your Rights

Are you an international couple with a baby on the way? First of all – congratulations!

We understand that giving birth in Israel as a foreigner, and dealing with everything that comes after, can feel overwhelming or even scary. But there’s no need to worry.

In this step-by-step guide, we’ll walk you through everything you need to know before and after giving birth. It’s specifically tailored for moms with an A5 visa, so you and your partner can understand your rights and navigate the necessary bureaucratic procedures with confidence.

**Note**: If the mother had a partner visa for fewer than 300 days before the birth, a paternity test might be required to determine if the child is eligible for Israeli citizenship, in accordance with procedure [5.2.0004 – Procedure for granting status in Israel to minors born in Israel through recognition of paternity](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/policy/procedure_for_granting_the_status_of_minors_by_acknowledging-paternity/he/5.2.0004.pdf) (full article coming soon!).

This article was co-written by Adv. Lior Beres of AIC, and guest writer Mette Silver Mikkelsen. Thank you Mette for your big contribution of information on this topic!

## Before the Birth

### Step 1: Get Form BL/303 Approved by Bituach Leumi

**Pay attention!** This is a new procedure that was created in collaboration between AIC and the maternity headquarters of Bituach Leumi. Its purpose is to eliminate the request to leave a deposit at the hospital. [Read here about how AIC made this possible](https://aic.org.il/future-mother-on-a5-enough-with-security-deposits-at-the-hospital/).

To avoid the need to leave a security check at the hospital after the birth, you will need to get form BL/303 from Bituach Leumi (National Insurance Institute) in advance.

**During the two months before the expected birth date** (from week 30), reach out to Bituach Leumi, through the Bituach Leumi website:

1. Print out [Form BL/303 (בל/303)](https://www.btl.gov.il/%D7%98%D7%A4%D7%A1%D7%99%D7%9D%20%D7%95%D7%90%D7%99%D7%A9%D7%95%D7%A8%D7%99%D7%9D/Documents/t303.pdf) and fill sections 1-3 **only**
    - Top part: fill the city of your BL branch, your status as resident, and your phone number
    - Section 1: your personal details (including Israeli ID)
    - Section 2: Partner’s personal details
    - Section 3: details of the future birth – week of pregnancy, due date, and the hospital in which you intend to give birth (attention! the exemption from leaving a deposit will only be registered in the hospital you choose here).
2. Attach any medical document that states the expected birth date
3. Upload clear scans of the completed form BL/303 and the attached medical documents to the [online document submission form](https://b2b.btl.gov.il/BTL.ILG.Payments/DocumentsInfo.aspx):

    - Select: “מחלקות הקצבאות”.
    - Select: “דמי לידה / מענק לידה” and fill in personal details in Hebrew only
    - Write the following note to the clerk in the designated field:
        - אני תושבת ישראל בעלת אשרה א5 ומבוטחת בביטוח הלאומי. אני בהיריון ועתידה ללדת בחודש —- (מצ”ב אישור רפואי). מבקשת אישור למענק אשפוז עבור הלידה העתידית – מצ”ב טופס בל303 מלא עם פרטי הלידה העתידית.

    - Choose category “טפסי תביעה”
    - Choose document type: “תביעה לתשלום מענק לידה/מענק אשפוז/קצבת לידה (בל300)”
    - Upload the filled BL/303 form and the medical document that states the expected birth date
    - Send

Processing of the application will take up to two weeks. Once the application is approved you should get a confirmation letter in your “personal area” of the Bituach Leumi website. Print the confirmation and take it with you to the hospital.

If you did not receive a confirmation after two weeks – try contacting Bituach Leumi via internal website message.

Form BL/303 – originally intended for documenting past births and completed by hospital clerks – has been specially updated to include a section for future births, thanks to AIC’s efforts. Bituach Leumi clerks at local offices have been informed about this new procedure.  
  
However, since the update is very recent, some clerks may not yet be familiar with it. If you encounter any issues during the approval process for Form BL/303, **[please contact AIC](https://aic.org.il/contact/)**.

## After the Birth

### Step 2: Registration at the Hospital (Birth Notice)

Before leaving the hospital, both parents will have to visit the hospital’s PIBA office (Population and Immigration Authority / Misrad Hapnim) to receive a Birth Notice (Hoda’at Leida / הודעת לידה), also known as form MR22.

The relevant procedure for this step is [2.2.0008 – Procedure for registering births in Israel and the inclusion of those entitled to it in the population registry.](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/policy/policy_registration_of_birth_in_general_registry/he/2.2.0008.pdf)

For the completion of the Birth Notice, you will need to present:

- The Israeli parent’s Israeli ID
- The foreign parent’s Israeli ID
- The foreign parent’s passport
- Both parents’ Israeli ID attachment (sefach / ספח)

In case the parents are not married, the father may have to fill in a [paternity form](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/generalpage/services_forms/he/MR25.pdf).

#### Number on Birth Notice

Technically, if the mother is not an Israeli citizen or permanent resident, the Birth Notice should not include any ID number. However, based on personal testimonies, some hospitals may issue a birth notice that includes a number. **In most cases, this number is not final and cannot be used as official identification for the baby.**

The decision to grant Israeli citizenship – and assign a permanent Israeli ID number – is made by your local PIBA branch (see Step 7).

**Tip!** When you get the Birth Notice, make sure that your baby’s name is spelled correctly.

### Step 3: Security Deposit or BL/303 Form Submission

After the birth, and before leaving the hospital, you are likely to be required to deposit a security check to “cover” the costs of the hospitalization. To avoid this, you can submit an approved BL/303 form that was issued by Bituach Leumi in advance, as proof that Bituach Leumi will cover the costs of the birth (see step 1).

- If you didn’t get an approved BL/303 in advance: You are likely to be asked to leave a security deposit to cover the birth’s costs. The deposit is usually submitted as a regular check, or, in some cases, by leaving credit card details. **While the amount of the deposit can vary, couples are often asked for 15,000 NIS.**  

    When making the deposit, have the hospital fill and sign form [BL/303](https://www.btl.gov.il/%D7%98%D7%A4%D7%A1%D7%99%D7%9D%20%D7%95%D7%90%D7%99%D7%A9%D7%95%D7%A8%D7%99%D7%9D/forms/maternity_forms/Pages/T303.aspx) with the details of the birth. This form will be used to annul the security deposit (see next step).  

- If you did get an approved BL/303 in advance: Submitting it to the hospital should be enough and you shouldn’t be asked to leave a deposit.

The pre-approved BL/303 form is part of a newly introduced procedure. If the hospital does not recognize or accept your approved form, please [contact AIC](https://aic.org.il/contact/).

### Step 4: National Insurance Institute 

#### Maternity Grant (Maanak Leida / מענק לידה)

While registering your baby at the hospital, the hospital will submit a claim to the National Insurance Institute (Bituach Leumi) for you to get your Maternity Grant (Maanak Leida / מענק לידה), a one-time payment given to new parents.

The grant amounts as of 2025, are as follows:

The Maternity Grant should be sent automatically from Bituach Leumi to your bank account that is registered in their system.

#### Maternity Allowance (Dmei Leida / דמי לידה)

Only mothers who were working (as an employee or as an independent worker) prior to the birth are entitled to maternity allowance. The period of the maternity allowance will be determined by the months of work in which the mother paid BL contributions prior to the cessation of work.

| Eligibility conditions | Period of Maternity Allowance |
| --- | --- |
| Paid insurance contributions for either 10 of the 14 months, or 15 of the 22 months preceding the cessation of work | 15 weeks |
| Paid insurance contributions for 6 of the 14 months preceding the cessation of work  | 8 weeks |

Read more about [eligibility for maternity allowance](https://www.btl.gov.il/benefits/maternity/Childbirth_Allowance/Pages/%D7%AA%D7%A0%D7%90%D7%99%20%D7%94%D7%96%D7%9B%D7%90%D7%95%D7%AA.aspx).

**Important!** The eligibility conditions *will* also take into account months of work and BL contributions that were made while the mother was NOT yet a resident (for example, while working on a B1 visa).

The amount of the maternity allowance will be determined by the contributions that the mother paid to BL prior to the cessation of work (the contributions are calculated as a % of the salary).

##### Apply for Maternity Allowance (Dmei Leida / דמי לידה)

Not all mothers have to manually apply for Maternity Allowance. If you **gave birth in a hospital** AND meet one of the following conditions, the National Insurance Institute will pay maternity allowance to you automatically, without having to submit a claim:

- **You are self-employed** – you will be paid a maternity allowance once the hospital sent your birth details to us. Payment will be transferred to the same bank account to which the child allowance is paid, and on a first birth – to the bank account you have indicated at the hospital.
- **Your employer has an agreement with the National Insurance Institute** – the employer transfers the claim for maternity allowance, on your behalf, directly to BL, and you will receive direct payment of maternity allowance.
- **You have received unemployment benefits until a month prior to birth** – you will be paid a maternity allowance once the hospital sent your birth details to BL. 
- **You have received a pregnancy bed rest benefit until the birth** – you will be paid a maternity allowance once the hospital sent your birth details to BL. 

In case you do not meet one of the conditions above, and you do need to file the claim yourself:

- Fill out [form 355](https://www.btl.gov.il/English%20Homepage/About/Forms%20Authorization/Forms/Maternity/Pages/355%20-%20Claim%20for%20Maternity%20Allowance%20or%20Vacation%20Pay%20for%20Adopting%20Mother.aspx) online on the National Insurance Institute’s website (the form is in Hebrew only, [contact us](https://aic.org.il/help-and-services/) if you need help).
- Both you and your employer need to provide the National Insurance Institute with information through the online form. 
- When the application is approved, you will receive the Maternity Allowance as a one-time payment in the bank account you indicated in Form 355.
- Once your application has been approved, you will receive a message on the National Insurance Institute’s website.
- If you do not hear from the National Insurance Institute within a reasonable timeframe, you are advised to call your local branch, send them a message through their website, or schedule an appointment through [MyVisit](https://myvisit.com/#!/home/) to hand in the papers in person.

#### Get Your Deposit Annulled

This step is only relevant if you made a security deposit at the hospital.

To cancel the deposit you paid at the hospital, contact your local Bituach Leumi (National Insurance Institute) branch. You can do this through the “Personal Inquiries” section on their website. Submit form BL/303, which was filled out and signed by a hospital clerk (see step 3).

Once the form is approved, you’ll receive a notification on the Bituach Leumi website. The hospital will also be informed directly.

### Step 5: Updating Your Workplace

You must notify your workplace after the birth of your child. Having a child entitles you to additional tax credit points, which means you’ll pay less in taxes.

There are two relevant forms:

- **Form 101 (Tofes Me’a Ve’achat)** – To be completed by both the mother and father for updating their tax status going forward.
- **Form 106 (Tofes Me’a Veshesh)** – provided by the employer, for requesting a retroactive tax refund, if applicable.

Both forms should be provided by your employer’s HR department and submitted through your workplace. In most cases, there’s no need to contact the Tax Authority directly.

### Step 6: Passport Picture

While PIBA (Population and Immigration Authority, Misrad HaPnim) can take your newborn’s passport photo during the appointment, they recommend bringing a photo that [meets their official guidelines](https://www.gov.il/he/pages/biometric_photo_guide).

Since getting a proper passport photo of a baby – especially one who can’t yet hold their head up – can be challenging, you may want to consider using a professional photographer familiar with passport photo requirements.

If you’re also planning to apply for a passport from the baby’s other nationality, make sure the photos meet the specifications for both Israeli passports and those of the other country.

### Step 7: PIBA (Population Authority – Ministry of Interior)

You do **not** need an appointment to register your newborn at PIBA. Simply show up at **your attending office** early in the morning.

**Note**! Registration of a baby born to a mother on A5 will only be possible at the office where you partner visa file is being processed.

While some PIBA offices require the presence of both parents and the baby, others do not. It’s a good idea to check with your local branch before the birth to find out who must be present, and how soon you should come in after the birth.

Check the [opening hours and contact information of PIBA offices](https://aic.org.il/contact-details-and-information-about-visa-departments-of-piba/) in advance.

At PIBA, you can do the following:

- Register the baby as Israel and get their ID number
- Get your baby’s birth certificate
- Get the parents’ ID attachment (Sefach) updated with the baby’s details
- Apply for the baby’s Israeli passport

You will need to bring the following documents:

- The Israeli parent’s ID
- The foreign parent’s ID
- The foreign parent’s passport
- Both parents’ ID attachment (Sefach)
- The Birth Notice (Hoda’at Leida) from the hospital
- Passport pictures of your baby (optional, see step 7)

When you receive the baby’s birth certificate, double-check that the name is spelled correctly and that all other details are accurate.

If you plan to apply for a passport from the baby’s other nationality, request an English version of the birth certificate. Again, carefully check that all the information, especially the baby’s name, is correct.

The name on the English birth certificate will be the one used on the foreign passport.

### Step 8: Registration to HMO (Kupat Holim)

**Note**: This is only possible once the baby is registered as Israeli and gets an Israeli ID number.

A baby needs to attend doctor’s checkups within the first two weeks of life, so it’s important to register your baby for health insurance as soon as possible.

If you want to register your baby with the HMO (Kupat Holim) that you or your partner are already enrolled in, you can call the pediatric clinic to do so. In some HMOs, it is also possible to register the baby online.

Even if there are delays in recognizing the baby as an Israeli citizen, the baby can still receive care and vaccinations at Tipat Halav (at the branches that are not owned by HMOs). [Appointment required](https://www.gov.il/he/service/appointment_for_family_health_centers).

### Step 9: Foreign Citizenship

Some countries automatically grant citizenship to children born to their nationals, while others require a formal application process. Contact your embassy to find out what applies in your case.

If your baby is automatically eligible for citizenship, ask the embassy which documents are needed to apply for a passport.

Keep in mind that not all countries allow dual citizenship. Be sure to check your home country’s policy before applying.
