# Supreme Judge Yechiel Kasher: “The petition is theoretical. The potential damage of the outline is limited”.

**AIC regrets to inform the community of international couples in Israel that our [petition against the “Passport Marathon” outline](https://aic.org.il/read-the-petition-aic-v-the-minister-of-interior/), its implementation and its destructive outcomes – has been dismissed in limine.**

This last May 18th, AIC in collaboration with JIJ and 5 additional private petitioners, have submitted a petition to the high court of justice against the shutdown of visa services all around Israel.

Unfortunately, and despite our repeated requests, the judge chose to not summon the sides to a hearing at court, and make a decision based on the written contents only – which he seems to have understood very poorly and superficially.

In his short verdict, the judge displays our argument superficially and in a untrue manner to the original content of our pleadings, flattening the situation and pretending that we appealed against “irregular workloads” in the offices. The judge completely and entirely ignores the main argument of the petitioners, that revolves around the illegality of the state’s decision to shutdown visa departments around the country for an extended period of time.

Later in the verdict, when displaying the arguments of the state, the judge reveals his lack of understanding of the bureaucracy, the population authority, and the background of the petition, when he mixes up the visa department with the registry department of PIBA. He says:

> “The directive to the other Bureaus of the Population Administration which do not participate in the outline, is to give priority to **registry services** that are not the issuing of passports, in order to minimize harm to those who need **other visa services** in the outline period”.

![](https://spcdn.shortpixel.ai/spio2/ret_img+q_cdnize+to_webp+s_dwebp/aic.org.il/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/image.jpg)

Finally, the judge gives his decision, based on the arguments as he understood them. We have translated the final paragraphs of the verdict for the convenience of our readers:

> After giving my opinion on the arguments of the parties, I came to the general conclusion that the petition should be rejected outright due to its being theoretical, in view of the fact that it is a short-term outline (the end of which is just around the corner), in a geographically limited area.
>
> Therefore, the potential damage that may be caused by the implementation of the outline is limited in scope from the beginning. Its essence is expressed in the fear that certain individuals who need an urgent response that does not tolerate delay, will not be able, for one reason or another, to wait for the end of the outline period, or to turn to population administration bureaus that are not one of the four major bureaus.
>
> The opening of the six email boxes for submitting special humanitarian requests seems to provide an answer to this concern, in a way that makes the present petition theoretical. In this context, I would note that I did not find correct the petitioners’ claims that the aforementioned e-mail boxes do not constitute a satisfactory solution to the problem pointed out in their petition.
>
> The present petition seeks to attack a decision of the Minister of the Interior concerning the determination of the work procedures and priorities of his office. The law is that the intervention in decisions of the aforementioned type will be done only in exceptional cases, and it is doubtful that the current case constitutes of one. Given that this is the starting point of the present petition, the petitioners’ claim that the solutions found by the respondents to deal with the claims raised in the petition – both at the general level and at the individual level of the petitioners 3-7 – are not ideal, it has no way to justify the intervention of this court.
>
> Moreover, it is presumed that the respondents will handle any request submitted to them through six email boxes in a timely and appropriate manner, according to the circumstances of each request; and that they will work to find solutions to the difficulties of the individual petitioners whose applications have not yet been completed.
>
> The bottom line: the petition must be dismissed, and in any case, the request for an interim order is also rejected. In the circumstances of the case, since it cannot be ruled out that the present petition led to an improvement in the answer given by the respondents, no order for expenses will be made.

AIC has invested tens of hours of volunteer work in this just petition, and we are sad about the way things turned out, while thousands of international couples and other people who need visa services are neglected so severely. However, the petition was in fact the direct reason of some improvements, such as the opening of the humanitarian aid mailboxes, and the supposed earlier reopening of the smaller branches.

AIC will keep fighting the outcomes of the Passport Marathon in all other fronts – the parliamentary, the media, and the public one alike. And we will do all we can to minimize the damage that this outline is causing.

We would like to thank the private petitioners 3-6, who are two couples from our community that shared their personal story and the way they got hurt in the petition, and chose to make the extra step and join us in the fight for their rights.

As a community and as an organization we will face injustices and losses, but we will never stop fighting for what we deserve. If you support our activity, please consider [making a donation](https://pay.sumit.co.il/2jaylc/2jaz2n/) to our association.
