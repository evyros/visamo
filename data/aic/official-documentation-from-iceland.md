# Official Documentation from Iceland

This post was written by a volunteer community member. While our volunteers do their best to share the most accurate information, there still may be errors or inaccurate details in this post. Please keep that in mind while reading!

This post includes detailed instructions on how to obtain and authenticate the official documentation and certifications from abroad, that are needed to apply for a partner or spouse visa in Israel. The basic personal documents you will be looking for are birth certificate, certificate of non-criminal record, current and/or previous personal status certificate (martial status), and name change certificate. Complete documentation about the foreign partner from their home country is prerequisite of the gradual process, and without it one cannot start the partner visa process and get a status in Israel based on their relationship.

## Birth Certificate from Iceland

Name in Icelandic: Fæðingarvottorð (D-110)

**How to obtain?**

Þjóðskrá registry issues birth certificate to individuals born in Iceland through its website It can be delivered to your legal domicile in Iceland (4-10 days delivery) or to their offices in Reykjavik or Akureyri (about 2 days). It can also be delivered directly to Utanríkisráðuneytið for Apostille stamp (4-10 days delivery; Coordinate before with Utanríkisráðuneyti, see below). Needs to be in English. Do not print at home from website.

**Cost: 2750 kr**

## Certificate of Non-Criminal Record from Iceland

Name in Icelandic: Sakavottorð

**How to obtain?**

Contact “Sýslumaðurinn” in your district. For Reykjavik and surroundings: “Sýslumaðurinn á Höfuðborgarsvæðinu, Hlíðarsmári 1 201 Kópavogur, sími: (+354) 458 200, Vefpóstur: [smh@syslumenn.is](mailto:smh@syslumenn.is)”. If you are abroad, contact them by email and request the certificate. You will need to send them the copy of your passport (clear picture) and transfer money to their bank account before they are able to process the application (send the bank transfer proof with the copy of the passport). You can have the certificate sent to your legal domicile in Iceland or it can be picked up from their office by you (or someone else if coordinated with them). They do not send to non-legal residence. Takes about 2 working days to process.

**Cost: 2500 kr,** if paid from abroad bank account **3150 kr (650kr paid in bank charges)**

[**Link**](http://www.syslumenn.is/)

## Verifying the documents with apostille

All official documentation from abroad that needs to be presented in Israel, must be verified by the country of its origin in order to be valid outside of it, in Israel. Many countries use “apostille” for this purpose. An apostille is usually a document, issued by an authorized authority which authenticates the documents in question.  This is used to demonstrate that they are valid documents that can be recognized in other countries.

### Obtaining apostille in Iceland

**How to obtain?**

Contact [UTN.Apostille@utn.is](mailto:UTN.Apostille@utn.is)  to arrange for the order, you need to give your personal information (name, “kennitala”, current home address etc), number of certificates needed, for which country and if you need to have them sent to Israel (extra fee for delivery 1390kr when written). All certificates (originals from Þjóðskrá and Sýslumaður) need to be either sent or brought to “Utanríkisráðuneytið”. Payment can be done through bank transfer or by phone call. If you need to have the apostille stamped certificates delivered to Israel: Prepare your address in a clear printable way in a Word document (English and Hebrew). You can have them sent by “ábyrgðarpóstur” directly by the “Utanríkisráðuneytið” to an address in Israel or you can personally arrange DHL to pick them up from “Utanríkisráðuneyti” for a delivery with DHL. If sent with “Ábyrgðarpóstur” prepare yourself for **two months delivery waiting time.** DHL promises faster delivery but much more expensive.

**Cost: 2500kr for each Apostille stamp**
