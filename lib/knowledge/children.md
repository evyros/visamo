# When the foreign partner's children move to Israel

This is for children under 18 from the foreign partner's previous
relationship who move to Israel with them (5.2.0008 calls them "accompanying
minors", קטין נלווה). Children who stay behind don't need documents.

## Documents (5.2.0008 §ד.2.י, §ה.2(9))

- **Birth certificate**, the original, certified, and translated if needed,
  listing both parents; 2 copies.
- **Passport**, valid for at least 2 more years.
- **A police certificate for each child aged 14 or older.**
- **About the other parent**, depending on their situation:
  - **Alive, with parental rights:** both of these:
    - **a consent letter signed by the other parent**, agreeing to the move;
    - **an official document with the other parent's current address.** The
      ministry also contacts the other parent itself: through the Israeli
      consulate if they live abroad, or by registered mail if they live in
      Israel (then no address document is needed).
  - **You have sole custody:** the original, certified court ruling, **and
    proof the children actually live with you** (§ה.2(9)י).
  - **The other parent has died:** their death certificate.
  - **No other parent is listed on the birth certificate:** nothing more.

## Waiting for the other parent

- After the ministry contacts the other parent, it waits up to 6 months for
  an answer. Meanwhile the child can get a B/2 permit, but not A/5.
- **In practice, no reply within 6 months counts as an objection.** (The
  procedure's text, §ה.2(9)ו, says it counts as no objection. Offices work by
  the former.)
- If the other parent objects to the move, the child's status isn't upgraded
  to A/5. If they object only to the child becoming a citizen, the child can
  get A/5, but not citizenship while under 18.

### Skipping the wait

You can avoid waiting for the ministry to reach an other parent who lives
abroad, if they give their consent in one of these ways:

- **At the Israeli consulate:** they go to the consul and sign a declaration
  that they consent.
- **Through a local notary:** they sign a consent in front of a notary where
  they live, and the document gets an apostille (or consular legalization,
  in a country outside the Apostille Convention; see
  [certification.md](certification.md)).

## School records

The AS/6 checklist asks for school confirmations and report cards for
children in the education system from age 6. They're evidence that your
household lives in Israel, so they're for **the children living with you in
Israel**: your children together, and the foreign partner's children once
they're in school here. The office may also check with the Ministry of
Education as part of the center-of-life check (§ה.2(7)).
