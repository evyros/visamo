# Pregnancy and a baby during the process

Neither procedure has special rules for a pregnancy or a child you have
together: no shortcut, same documents, same track. A pregnancy matters for
recognizing the Israeli partner as the father, and for who pays for the
birth. What applies depends on which of you is the mother, whether you're
married, when the pregnancy began compared with your application, and where
the baby is born. For a same-sex couple, recognizing the second parent works
differently and isn't described here.

## The process

- **Your child's birth certificate** is part of the proof of your
  relationship (§ד.2.ח), but doesn't replace the proof that you live
  together.
- **Marrying during the process:** a common-law couple can move to the
  married track, with no new fee. A B/1 holder is upgraded to A/5, with
  public health insurance, but still completes the full length of the
  process (5.2.0008 §ו.6(3)).
- **Already pregnant when you start:** file as early as you can, and keep
  the proof of your life together from before the pregnancy (a lease,
  bills, photos, trips). If the foreign partner is the mother, that proof is
  what recognizes the father without a court (below). A partner abroad
  still waits abroad for the entry permit: a pregnancy isn't a reason to
  come as a tourist.
- **Common-law, the foreign partner is the mother:** the wait for the
  interview and the first B/1 can be long, so the baby may be born before
  she has a visa as your partner, which makes recognizing the father harder.
  Married couples have no interview before the B/1, and reach A/5 within
  months.

## The foreign partner is the mother: recognizing the father

The baby of an Israeli father is Israeli, but the office first has to
recognize him as the father (procedure 5.2.0004, הכרה באבהות). The hospital
issues the birth notice (הודעת לידה) without a final ID number and sends
you to your branch (procedure 2.2.0008).

Both parents go to the branch in person, with ID documents and the visa the
mother entered on, **within 3 months of the birth** (§ב). What's accepted
depends on when you applied (§ג):

- **Married, and you filed before the pregnancy began,** with enough proof
  of your shared life: the father's declaration is enough.
- **Common-law, and she already had her B/1 as your partner before the
  pregnancy began:** the declaration is enough. A pending application
  doesn't count.
- **Otherwise:** objective proof that you were a family before the
  pregnancy, like living together. If that isn't enough, a family court
  ruling on paternity, as a rule with a DNA test. By the procedure the office
  asks for the proof first (§ג.4); in practice, some clerks send couples
  straight to court, so ask to show your proof.
- **No court needed** if a child you had together already got status as
  his, or with proof of fertility treatment with his sperm.

Until the baby is registered, they have no ID number and can't join a
health fund; the Ministry of Health's mother-and-baby clinics (טיפת חלב)
see every baby, free. **In practice,** registration at the branch that
handles your visa file gives the ID number and birth certificate on the
spot, and you can join a health fund the same day.

## The Israeli partner is the mother: adding the father

The baby is Israeli and registered at the hospital. A foreign father is
added at your branch, not the hospital (procedure 2.2.0007): both parents in
person, not necessarily together, with his entry visa, **within a year of
the birth**.

- **Married 300 days or more before the birth** (registered in Israel or
  not), **or common-law with an approved application filed before the
  pregnancy:** he's registered on your declaration.
- **Otherwise:** you both sign a declaration of paternity (form MR/25); the
  clerk can ask for proof or hold a hearing, and if not convinced, you need
  a court ruling.

## Born abroad

- **The baby is Israeli** if one of you is, unless the Israeli parent was
  also born abroad and is Israeli only through their own parent
  (Citizenship Law §4).
- **Register at the Israeli consulate:** the full birth certificate,
  certified (see [certification.md](certification.md)), the hospital's
  confirmation of the birth, and passports. An Israeli father shows proof of
  your relationship from before the pregnancy, or a court ruling. Some
  consulates ask an unmarried couple for proof of a shared life from at least
  300 days before the birth.
- **The baby enters Israel on an Israeli passport** (Passports Law §2A): get
  it at the consulate.

## The cost of the birth

- **Before the A/5,** the foreign partner has no public health insurance,
  and private plans usually exclude a pregnancy that began before the
  policy.
- **National Insurance pays for the birth** (the hospitalization grant and
  the birth grant) for a mother who's the wife of an insured Israeli
  resident, even if she isn't a resident herself (National Insurance Law
  §40(a)(1)). "Wife" includes a common-law partner who lives with him (§1).
- **It isn't automatic:** if either of you isn't a resident, go to a
  National Insurance branch about 2 months before the birth with form BL/303
  (בל/303), a medical document with the due date, the planned hospital, and
  the marriage certificate or the proof you live together. **In practice,**
  without an approval in advance the hospital asks for a deposit, up to
  about 15,000 NIS, cancelled once the birth is approved; some branches
  approve only after the birth.
- **On A/5:** public health insurance starts with the A/5 if the foreign
  partner has lived in Israel at least 183 days in the year that starts 6
  months before it, days on B/1 included (§2A). The basic coverage has no
  waiting period for pregnancy. In practice, the hospital may still ask for
  a deposit: ask National Insurance to approve BL/303 before the birth.
