# Documents for the first application

The documents to open your file with Misrad Hapnim. Sources: procedure
5.2.0008 §ד.2 (married couples), the checklist attached to form AS/6, and
how offices work in practice.

## Good to know first

- **Center of life:** proof that you live together in Israel has to cover
  **at least the last 12 months**.
- **Copies:** where a number of copies is given (the AS/6 checklist writes
  "2X"), bring that many.
- **Translation:** see [certification.md](certification.md). By law, only
  Hebrew and Arabic need no translation.
- **The fee** is paid with the application; it isn't a document.

## Forms

- **AS/6** (אש/6): the status application for a married couple. Common-law
  couples use the application under procedure 5.2.0009.
- **AS/1** (אש/1): the entry permit application, if the foreign partner is
  outside Israel (§ד.2.א).
- **An affidavit from each partner**, in the ministry's wording (AS/6, page
  3): that everything you submit is true, and that you'll report any change.
  **Signed in front of a lawyer.**
- **A letter about your relationship**, signed by both of you: how and when
  you met, your relationship and your life together (§ד.2.ו). Adding each of
  your life histories is recommended when it's relevant, but not required.
- **A full CV for the security check** (§ד.2.ט), only for applicants from
  some countries (see [former-ussr-and-security.md](former-ussr-and-security.md)).
  This isn't a job CV: it's a history of your employment and life, for
  security clearance.

## Identity

- **Teudat Zehut with its appendix (ספח)** of the Israeli partner (§ד.2.ג).
- **Passport photos: 3 copies of the same passport-format photo, of each
  partner** (§ד.2.ב; AS/6).
- **The foreign passport**, valid for at least 2 more years (§ד.2.ד), with
  copies of the main page, **the visa pages and the border-control stamps**.
- **Birth certificate** of the foreign partner, the original, from the
  country of birth; 2 copies (§ד.2.ה; AS/6).
- **Proof of a name change**, if the foreign partner's first or last name
  ever changed (§ד.2.ה).

## Your relationship

- **Marriage certificate** (§ד.2.ה): from the rabbinate or religious court if
  you married in Israel; otherwise from where you married, certified (see
  [certification.md](certification.md)).
- **Evidence of the relationship:** photos together, letters, and anything
  showing how you met and how long you've been together (§ד.2.ח).
- **Letters of recommendation** from family and friends about your
  relationship, **each with a copy of the writer's ID and their contact
  details** (§ד.2.ח).
- **Common-law couples who live together:** evidence of living together.

## Civil status

- **Proof of the foreign partner's civil status**, from their country, issued
  **in the last 6 months**; 2 copies (§ד.2.ה). Needed by every couple,
  including those married in Israel.
  - If the country doesn't issue one: a notarized affidavit about their
    status, signed in front of an Israeli consul, a notary abroad, a notary in
    Israel, or their country's consul in Israel.
  - If your marriage certificate is from the foreign partner's country and
    shows their status before the marriage, it can replace it.
- **Previous marriages:** a divorce decree, or the previous spouse's death
  certificate, for each marriage that ended; 2 copies (AS/6).

## Criminal record

- **A police certificate** (certificate of no criminal record) of the foreign
  partner, from their country (§ד.2.ה; AS/6). Every applicant over 14 needs
  one (§ד.2).

## Center of life (every couple)

- **Lease or purchase contract** of your home, in the Israeli partner's name:
  the original, a copy, and the lease appendix.
- **Bills** (electricity, water, arnona, phone), **in the renter's name or the
  Israeli partner's**.
- **Proof of government services** you receive: National Insurance (Bituach
  Leumi), health, education.
- **Ishur Toshav** (אישור תושב): your municipality or local council's
  confirmation that you live there, in the ministry's wording.
- **Shared finances:** a joint bank account confirmation. Without a joint
  account, other proof that you share money: transfers to each other, credit
  card statements showing household purchases or the same delivery address,
  receipts for large expenses.
- **Employment or income proof, for each partner**, depending on how you
  earn:
  - **Employed (שכיר):** a confirmation from work and **the last 6
    payslips** (tlushim).
  - **Self-employed (עצמאי):**
    - an accountant's confirmation of current income (אישור רואה חשבון על
      הכנסות נוכחיות);
    - the latest annual tax assessment from the Tax Authority (שומה שנתית
      אחרונה ממס הכנסה). A shuma is only for the self-employed, never for
      employees;
    - the confirmation of opening a business file, osek murshe or patur
      (אישור פתיחת תיק עוסק מורשה/פטור).
  - **Not working, but with an income:** bank statements showing the
    deposits.
  - **Not working and no income:** a clear, signed letter explaining your
    situation (for example, a student, between jobs, or a homemaker), stating
    that your partner financially supports your shared life in Israel.
- **The Israeli partner has to show they can support the household:** recent
  payslips and bank statements, and the shuma if self-employed. **If the
  income is borderline or tight**, add savings accounts and Kupat Holim (HMO)
  records. Utility bills also help show a stable life together.
- **Children's school records**: for children living with you in Israel, in
  school from age 6 (see [children.md](children.md)).

## Good to know

- The clerk can ask for any other document the circumstances call for (§ד.3).
