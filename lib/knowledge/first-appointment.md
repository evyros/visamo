# The first appointment

After you file the application (see [application.md](application.md)),
Misrad Hapnim (משרד הפנים) calls you to a first appointment at your branch (לשכה). There, the clerk reviews your documents.
What comes out of it depends on where the foreign partner is.

## Who goes

- **The foreign partner is in Israel:** you go together, to this
  appointment and to every one after it.
- **The foreign partner is outside Israel:** the Israeli partner goes alone.
- **You both live abroad:** you start at the Israeli consulate, not here:
  see [both-abroad.md](both-abroad.md).

No one can go to a Misrad Hapnim branch for you. You can't send someone else
in your place, to an appointment or to the walk-in hours.

## What to bring

- The original documents from abroad, certified and translated (see
  [certification.md](certification.md)), with photocopies.
- The Israeli partner's Teudat Zehut, with a copy. A driver's license isn't
  enough.
- The foreign partner's passport, with a copy. If they're outside Israel, a
  copy is enough.
- Copies of the documents you sent with the online application, and
  anything else the office asked for. The office should tell you what to
  bring; if it didn't, bring copies of everything you sent online.

## If the foreign partner is outside Israel

This is for an Israeli partner who lives in Israel. If you both live
abroad, see [both-abroad.md](both-abroad.md).

The Israeli partner submits the entry permit application (form AS/1) with
the file. At the first appointment, the clerk reviews it with the rest of
the documents. There are two ways it can go.

### Everything is in order: the entry permit

The office grants the foreign partner an entry permit (היתר כניסה), to come
to Israel and continue the process here. You may hear at the appointment, or
by phone or email a couple of weeks later. How long it takes, how the foreign
partner travels, and what to do once they arrive: see
[entry-permit.md](entry-permit.md).

### The office needs more

- **Missing or extra documents:** you get 45 days (calendar days, not
  working days) to bring them. The office tells you how to send them: by
  email, at the office, or another way.
- **A simultaneous interview** (ראיון סימולטני): the Israeli partner is
  interviewed at the office in Israel while the foreign partner is
  interviewed at the Israeli consulate where they live. In practice, it's
  often asked for when the foreign partner comes from a non-Western country
  (Eastern Europe, East Asia, South America or Africa, among others). See
  [interview.md](interview.md).

## If the foreign partner is in Israel

You both come, with the original documents from abroad and anything else
the office asked for. The foreign partner files form AS/3, the application
to change their visa category, with the file.

- **Married couples:** if all the documents are there, the B/1 visa is
  issued at this appointment, without an interview: by procedure 5.2.0008, a
  married couple's first interview comes before the A/5, not before the B/1.
  An office that asks a married couple with a complete file for an interview
  before the B/1 is going against the procedure.
- **Common-law couples:** if the documents are in order, the office sets a
  date for an interview with you both (see [interview.md](interview.md)).
  After it, the foreign partner can get the B/1 visa.
  - By procedure 5.2.0009 (clause 10, page 16), the interview is held,
    whenever possible, at the appointment where you show the original
    documents. Ask the clerk for it, and point them to that clause.
  - In practice, many branches have long queues for interviews: at the
    busiest ones, the wait for a first visa's interview can reach a year.

### On another visa

If the foreign partner is in Israel on a visa that has nothing to do with
your relationship (a student visa, a caregiver's or another worker's B/1, a
volunteer or work-holiday visa, a tourist visa):

- **File before that visa expires,** and then go to the branch, while it's
  still valid, to settle their status until the application is decided.
- **The current visa should be extended** until the decision, as long as they
  keep to its terms (still studying, still working where it allows). If it
  can't be, the office gives a B/2 (tourist) visa in between: ask for it
  before the current visa runs out, so they're never without one.
  - **A caregiver who's still employed:** bring a letter from the employer
    saying they know about the partner visa application and agree to keep
    employing them. Without an employer, the visa becomes a B/2.
  - **A visa from an earlier graduated procedure** is cancelled, and they get
    a B/2 until the decision.
- **Two applications at once:** you can't have two visa applications open at
  the same time (other than asylum, aliyah or conversion). If another one is
  still pending, you choose one.
- **A work-holiday visa:** its procedure says to leave at the end of the year
  and bans other applications, but in 2025 the appeals tribunal ruled that
  the office must review a partner application while the partner stays in
  Israel. Show the office the ruling if it insists on leaving first.
- **A tourist visa:** coming as a tourist to stay goes against Israel's
  immigration policy (see
  [entry-permit.md](entry-permit.md)). If the foreign partner is already here, the
  application is still handled and they aren't deported while it's decided,
  but the office may look at it more closely, and they can't come back in if
  they leave.

### Without a valid visa

If the foreign partner stayed in Israel past their visa (an overstay), in
practice it doesn't affect their eligibility for the partner visa at all.

- **The best way:** the foreign partner leaves Israel, and you start the
  process properly from abroad, through the entry permit: see
  [entry-permit.md](entry-permit.md).
- **Don't come back before the entry permit.** Until the office grants it,
  the foreign partner shouldn't try to enter Israel again: it's very likely
  they'll be refused entry and deported.

By procedure 5.2.0008, if they stay and apply from Israel:

- **With a court ruling ordering them to leave Israel:** as a rule, the
  application isn't handled until they leave (§ג.12). Only in very rare
  cases can the regional manager allow it to be handled first.
- **Without such a ruling:** the couple is interviewed (§ג.12).
  - If the relationship seems not to be genuine, or seems to have begun
    after their visa expired, the foreign partner is required to leave.
  - If it seems genuine, they don't have to leave until the application is
    decided, as long as the full application, with all its documents, was
    filed before any enforcement steps were taken against them.
- **Either way,** a stay in Israel without legal status sends the file to
  the branch's visa team head, who decides on further checks, and whether
  to refuse it (§ה.2).

## Getting the first visa

- **The B/1 visa** is a sticker in the passport, issued and signed by your
  branch. In 2026 it costs 205 NIS, by credit card only.
- **How long it's valid:** common-law couples usually get a year, renewed
  every year. Married couples usually get 6 months, the time the office has to
  interview you and decide on the A/5. A common-law couple offered only 6
  months can point the clerk to procedure 5.2.0009.
- **Ask for the inter-visa too.** The inter-visa (אינטר ויזה, a multiple-entry
  visa) is a second sticker that keeps the main visa valid when the foreign
  partner travels abroad. Without it, leaving Israel ends the main visa.
  Partners in the graduated procedure get it without trouble, with the same
  validity as the main visa, for another 205 NIS (2026). Get it with the
  visa, even if you don't plan to travel.
