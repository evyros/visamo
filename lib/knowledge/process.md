# The process

## What it is

A foreign partner of an Israeli gets status through the **graduated
procedure** (ההליך המדורג): years of temporary visas before permanent
status. The foreign partner typically moves from B/1 (a work permit) to A/5
(a temporary resident) and, at the end, to citizenship or permanent
residency. Each stage is renewed with updated documents.

- **Married couples:** procedure 5.2.0008.
- **Common-law couples** (ידועים בציבור), including same-sex couples:
  procedure 5.2.0009. It needs more proof of living together, and proof that
  both partners are free to marry.

## Who the Israeli side can be

- **An Israeli citizen.** The foreign partner ends with citizenship.
- **A permanent resident** (תושב קבע). This is supported. The foreign partner
  ends with permanent residency, not citizenship. Center-of-life checks are
  stricter, and many sponsors like this are from East Jerusalem.
- **A temporary resident (A/5)** generally can't sponsor a partner. The
  onboarding's "Neither" option covers them. It isn't a third status.

In the data, `is_israeli` means "the Israeli side" (citizen or permanent
resident), and `israeli_status` says which.

## Out of scope for now

- **Renewals.** Only the first application is supported. Onboarding offers
  "We already have a visa and need to renew it" and blocks finishing, with a
  "coming soon" notice.
- **Residents of the Palestinian territories, and nationals of Iran, Iraq,
  Syria, Lebanon.** They fall under the Citizenship and Entry into Israel Law
  and mostly can't use the graduated procedure. 5.2.0008 §ה.2(1) rejects them
  at the start (by age for PA residents: men under 35, women under 25).
  Onboarding doesn't screen for this yet.
- **The Law of Return route** (a Jewish foreign partner, or one with Jewish
  parents or grandparents). A different and better route. Not handled.
- **Previous sponsorship** (the Israeli sponsored a foreign partner before).
  It brings extra scrutiny, but **it doesn't change the document list**
  (practice). Not asked.
