# Official Documentation from Australia

This post was written by a volunteer community member. While our volunteers do their best to share the most accurate information, there still may be errors or inaccurate details in this post. Please keep that in mind while reading!

This post includes detailed instructions on how to obtain and authenticate the official documentation and certifications from abroad, that are needed to apply for a partner or spouse visa in Israel. The basic personal documents you will be looking for are birth certificate, certificate of non-criminal record, current and/or previous personal status certificate (martial status), and name change certificate. Complete documentation about the foreign partner from their home country is prerequisite of the gradual process, and without it one cannot start the partner visa process and get a status in Israel based on their relationship.

\*This article is covering the procedure to obtain documentation in the state of **New South Wales**. Please take into account that the described procedure may vary in other territories of Australia.

## Birth Certificate from Australia

Name in Australia: Birth Certificate

**How to obtain?**

This is individual to your state of birth. You should go onto the Births, Deaths and Marriages (BDM) website of your relevant state and follow the links. The below information is relevant for New South Wales.

**Cost: $60** standard service, **$88** priority service

**Link:** [**NSW Birth Certificate**](https://www.nsw.gov.au/family-and-relationships/births/birth-certificates)

## Name change certificate from Australia

\* Please note – Name change certificate is not necessary if you did not change your first / last name.

Name in Australia: Change of Name Certificate

**How to obtain?**

You can obtain this from BDM NSW by clicking on the link below. You will need to pay the fee and provide 3 x proof of identity as specified on the link.

**Cost**: **$60** standard service**, $88** priority service

**Link:**  [**NSW Change of Name Certificate**](https://www.nsw.gov.au/family-and-relationships/name-changes-and-corrections/replace-certificates-after-a-change-of-name)

## Personal status certificate from Australia

Name in Australia: Single Status Certificate

**How to obtain?**

Unfortunately, this one can’t be done online. You can either physically go to a NSW Births, Deaths and Marriages office, or authorize someone to do this on your behalf. Alternatively, you can apply via post. In all options you must complete the form and provide 3 x proof of identity as specified on the link.

**Important note** – even **with** priority service, current wait times (June, 2022) are approximately 8 weeks not including postage – it is essential that you are well organized with this to fit into the PIBA’s time frame.

**Cost:** NSW charges in search increments. For a ten year record it is **$60** for a standard service, **$88** for a priority service. I chose to have a longer record (back to 16 years of age) so requested a twenty-year record to be on the safe side. A 20-year search record is **$105** for a standard service, **$133** for a priority service.

**Link:** [**NSW Single Status Certificate**](https://www.service.nsw.gov.au/transaction/apply-single-status-certificate)

## Certificate of Non-Criminal Record from Australia

Name in Australia:  National Police Check

**How to obtain?**

Apply online at the link below. I note that by default the Australian Federal Police now give you a digital copy of your police check rather than a physical copy. I am not sure how the Misrad Hapnim views digital copies so I would suggest requesting an original physical copy (which you can do on the form) to be on the safe side.

**Cost: $42.00** 

**Link:** [**National Police Check**](https://www.afp.gov.au/what-we-do/services/criminal-records/national-police-checks)

\***Important Note Regarding International Post:** In case you’re already living in Israel, you can pay extra $9 to have the certificates sent internationally to your address in Israel. I strongly suggest having it sent instead to a trusted friend or family member in Australia and then using a reliable courier service (such as DHL) to deliver to your address in Israel. ALL my certificates (ordered individually) were lost by Israel Post and had to be re-ordered to my parent’s house and couriered – this caused delays in my visa process.

## Verifying the documents with apostille

All official documentation from abroad that needs to be presented in Israel, must be verified by the country of its origin in order to be valid outside of it, in Israel. Many countries use “apostille” for this purpose. An apostille is usually a document, issued by an authorized authority which authenticates the documents in question.  This is used to demonstrate that they are valid documents that can be recognized in other countries.

### Obtaining apostille in Australia

**How to obtain?**

**In Australia:** You can obtain these with an appointment at the Australian Passport Office in your nearest capital city. You can find a list of locations [**here**.](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/consular-services/notarial-services/documents-in-australia#appointment) The documents that need to be authenticated can also be submitted for authentication by post.

**In Israel:** Luckily, this can be done in-person at the Australian Embassy in Tel Aviv (Yehuda Ha Levi, 23). You need to make an appointment online at the link listed – you cannot just show up or add it on to another type of appointment at the embassy, as the person authorized to do the apostille authentication is not always present.

**Cost:**

**At the embassy: 204 ILS** per document.

**In Australia: $87** per document.

**Links:**

[**DAFT**](https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/consular-services/notarial-services/documents-in-australia)

**[Australian Embassy in Tel Aviv](https://israel.embassy.gov.au/tavv/home.html)**  or **[Book an Appointment](https://booking.setmore.com/scheduleappointment/542af82b-f52c-462f-a1c3-6768b7f3e45e)**
