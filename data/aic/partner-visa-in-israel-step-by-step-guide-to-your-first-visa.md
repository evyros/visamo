# Partner Visa in Israel: A Step-by-Step Guide to Your First Visa

If you’re reading this post, it probably means that you’ve made the decision to go for it – you and your partner have decided to start your shared life in Israel. Congratulations! This is a big step that proves your commitment to one another.

B1 General visa (In Hebrew: ב/1 כללי or רישיון זמני לעבודה ללא הגבלה), is the first visa a foreign partner of an Israeli will get, in all types of partner visa processes (married couples, non-married couples, same-sex couples, etc). B1 General visa is an Unrestricted Work Visa that allows its holder not only to live in Israel together with their Israeli partner but also **to work freely in any field**.

This post is a detailed step-by-step guide to obtaining your first couple’s visa in Israel. Clear instructions will take you from the moment you decide to apply for the visa until the foreign partner gets the visa sticker in their passport.

Don’t be alarmed by the length, specifics, and bureaucracy that you’re going to read about. Tens of thousands of couples in Israel have gone and are going through the partnership visa process, and we survived to tell. Good luck!

If you’re interested in a more general overview of the gradual process – check [this article](https://aic.org.il/the-gradual-process-birds-eye-view/) instead!

This post focuses on the partner visa process for couples in which the Israeli side is a *citizen*. The process for couples where the Israeli side is a permanent resident is similar but may vary slightly.

## Step 1: Collecting and authenticating official documents from the country of origin

The gradual process (both for married and non-married couples) calls for the submission of official and authenticated certificates from the home country of the non-Israeli partner. This includes their birth certificate, personal status certificate, certificate of non-criminal record, etc. Married couples will also have to make sure they have an original and authenticated marriage certificate. This is one of the most time-consuming steps of the process, and therefore this should be your first step.

Check [“Official Documents to Start the Israeli Partner Visa Process”](https://aic.org.il/official-documentation-for-the-partner-visa/) for elaborate information about the document collection process, including detailed instructions about more than 25 countries!

**Important!** The **original** documents must be physically present in Israel. They may be needed for the notary translation (Step 2) and must be presented to PIBA during the first meeting (Step 6). Plan ahead to ensure the authenticated originals arrive in Israel on time.

**Quick tip!** To apply for a partner visa, the passport of the non-Israel must have a minimum 2-year validity at the time of application.

## Step 2: Translation of official documents

Documents that are **originally** in Arabic, don’t need to be translated.

PIBA clerks are allowed to accept documents that aren’t in Hebrew or Arabic if the clerk fully understands the original language of the document. For that reason, PIBA will normally accept untranslated documents that are **originally in English**, mainly when they don’t contain a lot of text. Documents in other languages will not be accepted without translation. If PIBA requires an English document to be translated, they will let you know and give you time to arrange the translation.

The official documents from the country of origin (birth certificate, personal status certificate, certificate of non-criminal record, etc.), the marriage certificate (when applicable), and the apostilles attached to all those documents **must be translated into Hebrew** with a notarized translation.

The rules regarding the translation of official documentation from abroad are dictated in [procedure 1.3.0001](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/policy/policy_approve_public_certifiacte_apostille/he/1.3.0001.pdf). According to the procedure, PIBA will only accept translations done by a notary that is fluent in both languages, and not a translation that was merely approved by a notary and done by someone else. **We highly recommend doing the translation in Israel**. For notarized translations done abroad to be valid in Israel, they have to be apostilled themselves, which might complicate the process.

In case there isn’t a notary in Israel that is fluent in the language of the document, PIBA will allow a double translation – a translation abroad to a third language (for example English), and then a translation in Israel from English to Hebrew.

## Step 3: Collecting additional documents and relationship proofs

Once the official certificates from the foreign partner’s country of origin are taken care of (or while you wait for them), the next step is gathering all other required documents and proof. This includes filling out official forms, preparing necessary documents, collecting evidence of your relationship, and providing additional paperwork—mainly related to your center of life in Israel.

Visit our [list of documents required for the online application](https://aic.org.il/list-of-documents-required-for-the-partner-visa-application-renewal/). You will also find there elaborate explanations and examples for every type of document.

Keep in mind that PIBA is entitled to ask you for any other documents/proof as they deem fit.

## Step 4: Submitting an Online Application

**Quick tip!** The online application form accepts PDF and JPEG files only, with one file allowed per field. If you need to include multiple documents in one field, merge them into a single PDF before uploading. You can use this free [PDF merger](https://www.ilovepdf.com/merge_pdf).

It’s time!

Do you have everything ready? It is now time to submit an online application for a partner visa!

1. In the [online application page](https://www.gov.il/he/service/requesting-status-for-foreign-life-partner), choose the type of application:
    - **married** or **shared life** (non-married couples))
    - If married: choose between Marriage to foreigners, Same-sex marriage, or “El Salvador” marriage (proxy marriage in El Salvador only).
2. The Israeli partner has to log into the National Identification System (GovIL)\*
3. The details of the Israeli partner, including ID, phone number, email and the designated PIBA office will be displayed automatically.
4. Continue filling the form and submit your documents in the required fields.

\*In case the Israeli partner is not registered to GovIL services, they must complete the online registration before you can continue.

**Pay attention** – the application will automatically be submitted to a specific PIBA office that corresponds with the registered address of the Israeli partner (the designated office will be shown after logging in to the application form). Therefore, the address in your Israel ID **must be updated and** **correct**. In case you have moved, you must [update your registered address](https://www.gov.il/he/service/changing_address) **before** submitting the application.

The online application requires the submission of all the documents mentioned in step 3, **AND** the official documentation that you collected and translated in steps 1 and 2.

At the end of the submission, you will be required to pay **1,085 NIS** \[2026\]. This is the fee for opening a gradual process file. In the past, until 2022, only married couples had to pay this fee, whereas non-married couples only had to pay the cost of the visas themselves. This changed in early 2022 and the opening fee was equalized for non-married couples as well.

After submitting the application and paying, you will get a confirmation email and reference number. Use this number, in addition to your personal ID/Passport number, for future inquires (the number also appears in the top corner of the online application website).

### Fee exemption application (extreme cases only)

Couples who cannot afford the application fee (or other fees), can apply for an exemption via [procedure 5.1.0003](https://www.gov.il/he/departments/policies/policy_exemption_from_pay_for_residency_permit). In this case, you will have to physically go to the PIBA branch and apply for it. Keep in mind fee exemptions are given in EXTREME cases and a extensive proofs for your financial situation will have to be presented.

Pay attention – from this point onward, the non-Israeli partner is likely to be **blocked entry to Israel as a tourist**, until the immigration application is processed and an entry clearance in granted.

## Step 5: Waiting for a response

Unfortunately, we have to give the waiting time a step of its own. Waiting times are extensive in most offices and it only seems to be getting worse.

Depending on your branch, waiting times to hear back about your online application vary between **3 weeks to 4+ months**, as of summer 2026

In case you didn’t hear anything back about your application after 3 weeks, it is advisable to [write an email](https://aic.org.il/contact-details-and-information-about-visa-departments-of-piba/) to your office, include your personal details and your application reference number and ask for information. Many offices don’t respond to emails. In this case, we suggest to try and go there physically during walk-in hours (read more about [contacting PIBA in this article](https://aic.org.il/how-to-book-an-appointment-to-piba-visa-department/)).

Once you get a response – there are two possible options:

1. PIBA wants you to provide some extra documents or something you were lacking. You will usually be notified about this via email. In this case, you will have to complete the missing documents and send them back to them via [the same online application form](https://www.gov.il/he/service/requesting-status-for-foreign-life-partner) (unless instructed otherwise).
2. PIBA sets a date for your first appointment (physical submission). Appointments are usually set for a couple of months ahead. Sometimes, they might set an appointment and ask for some additional documents to bring with you.

## Step 6: First appointment (physical submission of the documents)

This step and its conclusions vary depending on your situation:

- If the non-Israeli side is in Israel – you go together.
- If the non-Israeli side lives abroad – the Israeli goes alone.

For this meeting, prepare to present and submit the following documents:

1. The **original** documents and certificates from the country of origin of the non-Israeli partner, properly authenticated and translated (steps 2 and 3), along with photocopies.
2. **Israeli ID** of the Israeli partner (a driver’s license is not enough!) + photocopy.
3. **Passport**(s) of the non-Israeli partner + photocopy. If the partner is abroad, only a photocopy of the passport is required.
4. You may also need to submit copies of the documents uploaded in the online application, along with any additional paperwork requested by PIBA.

The office should inform you of what to bring, but if they haven’t, it’s best to prepare photocopies of everything you submitted online.

### The non-Israeli partner is not in Israel

**When the non-Israeli partner is not yet in Israel**, there are two possible conclusions to the first meeting:

1. **No additional requirements** – If all the documentation is in order and there aren’t any additional requirements, PIBA will grant the non-Israeli partner a clearance to enter and continue the process in Israel. This is sometimes referred to as “Hazmana” (invitation). You can be notified about the clearance on spot, or via phonecall/email a couple of weeks after the meeting.  
    - **Citizens of [visa-waiver countries](https://aic.org.il/countries-on-a-visa-waiver-program-with-israel/)**: can fly to Israel immediately after getting the clearance, **[subject to obtaining ETA-IL](https://aic.org.il/eta-il-how-to-apply-and-everything-you-need-to-know-guide/)**, and no written proof is necessary to pass immigration in Israel\*.
    - **Citizens of countries that require a visa to enter Israel**: will have to contact the Israeli consulate in their place of residence to receive a B2 Tourist Visa sticker in their passport.  

2. **Additional requirements** – PIBA might be unsatisfied with your documents or require to conduct a simultaneous interview.  
    - In case certain **documents are missing**, or additional documents are required, you will be given 45 days (calendar days) to complete the required documents. PIBA should instruct you how to submit the additional documents – via email, physically at the office etc.
    - A **simultaneous interview** is frequently required in case the non-Israeli partner is coming from a non-western country (including but not limited to Eastern Europe, East Asia, South America, Africa etc). The simultaneous interview happens while the Israeli side is at PIBA office in Israel, and the non-Israeli side at the Israeli consulate where they live.

\*Some airlines may refuse to fly a non-Israeli person to Israel without a return ticket. Please check your airline policy before booking a one-way ticket.

#### After entry to Israel

A non-Israeli partner that got an entry clearance will enter Israel with a B2 Tourist visa as an interim status. Contact your PIBA office (via email, or in person) and notify them about the arrival of the non-Israeli partner **as soon as possible**. PIBA will summon you to the office to get the visa or schedule an interview date (see below).

Pay attention – entering Israel as a “tourist” without getting a proper clearance via the described process, with the intention of staying in Israel and living with your partner, is against Israeli immigration policy.

### The non-Israeli partner is in Israel

**When the non-Israeli partner is already in Israel**, both parties must attend the first meeting (and every other meeting) **together**:

1. **Married couples** – will be asked to present the **original** certifications from abroad and any other document as per the instructions of PIBA. If you presented all the necessary documents, a B1 visa should be issued at this meeting without an interview (**skip to step 8!**).  

2. **Non-married couples** – will be asked to present the **original** certifications from abroad and any other document as per the instructions of PIBA. If all the paperwork is in order, PIBA will set a date for a shared interview\*, after which the non-Israeli partner will be able to get the B1 visa. **PIBA is encouraged to conduct the interview “on the spot”, in the same meeting of presenting the documentation.** We suggest trying to ask for this option and referring the clerk to the following clause:

\*Pay attention that many offices are experiencing huge queues and backlogs for interviews. The waiting time for an interview for a new visa, especially in the busy offices, can even **reach a year**.

Extra Step: “Nativ” interview (citizens of former USSR countries only)

**When the non-Israeli partner is a citizen of a former USSR country**, an extra step is required before getting the B1 visa (for non-married couples\*).

\*Married couples where the non-Israeli partner is a citizen of a former USSR country, will get the B1 visa before completing this extra step, but will required to undergo this step before they can upgrade their status to A5 (temporary resident).

Nativ is a govermental office whose main goal is to examine and approve Aliyah applications of citizens of former USSR countries. They are considered the experts of soviet and post-soviet bureaucracy and documentation from the relevant countries. The reason Nativ is involved with immigration of non-Jewish spouse or partners of Israelis remains unclear, but cannot be avoided under the current policies.

Normally, once both parties are in Israel, PIBA will instruct the couple to fill an [online Nativ questionnaire](https://aliyaform.nativ.gov.il/default.aspx?piba=true).

After sending the questionnaire, Nativ will summon the couple to a short interview in their Jerusalem offices. Unfortunately, waiting times for Nativ interviews are quite long. If you haven’t heard back from Nativ after a month, we suggest trying to contact them and inquiring about the status of your application.

See more information and contact details on [Nativ’s website](https://www.gov.il/he/service/confirm_status_for_foreigner_family_member_in_israel).

## Step 7: Shared interview

Non-married couples have to go through a shared interview before getting their first partner visa (B1).

PIBA sometimes requires married couples to go through an interview before granting a B1 visa – this is breach of protocol 5.2.0008 and should not happen. If you are a married couple who submitted all the required documents, and were required to undergo a shared interview in Israel *before* getting a B1 visa, [please report it us](https://aic.org.il/help-and-services/).

The interview is the main tool in the hands of PIBA to check the honesty of your relationship. It consists of questions about your daily life, your partner and your relationship. Each side is interviewed separately, then your answers are matched with your partner’s, to make sure they correlate. Please check our article about [the Partner Visa Interview](https://aic.org.il/the-partner-visa-interview-everything-you-need-to-know/) for an in-depth view of the interview process.

## Step 8: Getting your first visa

Congratulations! You’ve made it this far.

Non-married couples who successfully passed the shared interview, and married couples who successfully submitted all necessary documents and proofs, are both eligible to get their first partner visa – a B1 General. The visa itself is a sticker in the passport, issued and signed by your PIBA office. The visa costs **205 NIS** \[2026\] which will have to be paid by credit card only.

B1 General visa is an UNRESTRICTED working visa. It differs from B1 restricted working visas (such as caretaking visa, expert visa, agriculture work visa etc.) Those visas are all restricted to a certain field of work/employee and are governed by work immigration regulations.

### Visa validity

Non-married couples normally get a B1 visa with a validity of 1 year. Married couples might also get one-year validity, but will usually get a validity of 6 months only. That’s because married couples should carry a B1 visa for **only up to 6 months**, during which PIBA must complete all their checks, invite the couple to an interview and make a decision in the couple’s application for an A5 visa.

Non-married couples will hold the B1 visa for 3 years, renewing it once a year.

## Bonus Step: Getting an Inter-Visa

This was a guide on how to get your *first* visa, but an Inter-Visa is just as important 🙂

An Inter-Visa, also known as Entry Visa or Multiple Entry Visa, is an additional visa and an additional sticker in your passport. The “job” of an Inter-Visa is to maintain the validity of your main visa (B1, A5, etc.) when going abroad. Technically, without it, your main visa will expire once you left Israel. The inter-visa allows you to leave and return to Israel freely without having to worry about your main visa expiring because you left Israel.

Partners of Israelis in the gradual process are eligible to get an Inter-Visa without hassle. Clerks will usually suggest you get it or ask you if you want it. If they don’t, we suggest asking for it. You could always ask for it at a later stage, but even if you’re not planning to go abroad, we advise getting it at the same time as getting your main visa.

The Inter-Visa costs an additional **205 NIS** \[2026\] and will be issued with the same validity as your main visa.
