# 2024: Big Updates to Partner Visa Procedures 5.2.0008 + 5.2.0009

2024 started off on a positive note when it comes to the partner visa bureaucracy.

PIBA procedures [5.2.0008](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/policy/status_foreigner_married_to_israeli_procedure/he/5.2.0008.pdf) “The procedure for granting status to a foreign spouse married to an Israeli citizen” and [5.2.0009](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/policy/israelis_couples_status_procedure/he/5.2.0009.pdf) “The procedure for granting status to partners of Israelis, including partners of the same sex” were substantially updated on January 10, 2024.

Many of these updates are significant and indicate the start of a hopefully enduring positive direction for PIBA under its new manager, Eyal Sisso. AIC extends congratulations to PIBA’s headquarters for these changes and will continue advocating for progressive and fair immigration policies for partners of Israelis.

Read our summary of the most significant updates below.

## 1\. Marriage Registration in Country of Origin No Longer A Threshold Condition

Married couples will no longer have to demonstrate that their marriage has been registered in the home country of the non-Israeli partner as a condition for obtaining a spouse visa.

Before the update, the non-Israeli partner had to supply a certificate attesting to their personal status as “married”. This requirement used to be a threshold condition that frequently caused issues for married couples applying for a spouse visa, particularly when the marriage took place in a third country, different from the origin of the non-Israeli partner. This posed challenges, especially for citizens of countries that don’t maintain a registry of offshore marriages.

Under the new policy, the registration of the marriage will not impede the granting or renewal of a spouse visa (B1/A5). However, in case the marriage was not registered in the country of origin of the non-Israeli partner throughout the process, an additional personal status certificate will be required as a condition of finishing the process.

![](https://spcdn.shortpixel.ai/spio2/ret_img+q_cdnize+to_webp+s_dwebp/aic.org.il/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/image.jpg)

Procedure 5.2.0008, page 12

This significant change eliminates the requirement for marriage registration in the non-Israeli spouse’s home country. While it is still advisable to register the marriage to avoid an extra issuance of a personal status certificate at the end of the process, the removal of this as a prerequisite marks a favorable development for couples.

\*A certificate of *previous* personal status (status before the marriage) is still required.

## 2\. Police Certificate From Additional Countries

The requirements for a police certificate or certificate of non-criminal record have been inconsistent for a considerable time. Different offices have had varied demands, leading to confusion and inequality among applicants, especially in cases of residency in additional countries apart from their country of origin. Most offices have adopted a strict approach, requiring a police certificate for every country of residence, even for short stays.

The new policy is finally clearing some of the fog and clarifies when a police certificate will be required from an additional country that is not the country of origin. Under the new policy, a police certificate will be required from additional countries, only in case of **consecutive residence of at least 6 months**.

![](https://spcdn.shortpixel.ai/spio2/ret_img+q_cdnize+to_webp+s_dwebp/aic.org.il/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/image-2.jpg)

Procedure 5.2.0008, page 9; Procedure 5.2.0009, page 7

It remains unclear whether a police certificate is needed from each country of *citizenship* for non-Israelis with multiple citizenships, particularly in instances where there has been no residency in the additional citizenship’s country. AIC will seek clarification from PIBA and update the website information accordingly.

## 3\. “Accompanying Minors” Can Be Added to Applications of Non-Married Couples

Following a petition that was made to the district court on the matter, PIBA has revised the non-married partner visa protocol to include the option of adding of “accompanying minor” to the application.

The previously exclusive option for married couples now extends to unmarried couples, under the same conditions. This option permits a child of the non-Israeli partner (from previous relationship) to obtain status in Israel gradually alongside their parent, leading to eventual permanent residency and, optionally, citizenship.

The extensive list of conditions, application procedures, and relevant remarks regarding the accompanying minor have all been incorporated into procedure 5.2.0009.

## 4\. New Rules for Partner Applications of Asylum Seekers

The partner visa procedures offer specific exemptions, primarily related to document requirements, for applicants who are also asylum seekers. However, the previous protocols lacked guidance on the course of action if the asylum application remained unanswered. Consequently, asylum seekers could be left pending with a B1 partner visa, without eligibility for an upgrade, for an indefinite amount of time.

The new policies outline procedures for handling partner visa applications **in the event of an undecided asylum application**. According to the updated policy, asylum seekers with a pending asylum application can become eligible for an A5 visa upgrade after 63 months of holding a B1 partner visa. Then, and as long as the asylum application remains undecided, the A5 visa will be extended for 2 years at a time. However, permanent status will not be granted until a decision is made on the asylum application.

![](https://spcdn.shortpixel.ai/spio2/ret_img+q_cdnize+to_webp+s_dwebp/aic.org.il/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/image-3.jpg)

Procedure 5.2.0008, page 17; Procedure 5.2.0009, page 16

The rules and conditions that apply *after* a decision was made in the asylum application remain the same. The positive change lies in the now-permissible A5 upgrade, which was previously restricted in cases where the asylum application was undecided.

## Additional Updates

Other than the significant changes listed above, the procedures underwent several minor revisions and additions. These include possible relief for center-of-life checks for Israelis who are outside of Israel due to an official mission or delegation on behalf of the country, procedural clarifications regarding the 45-day notice for document completion, addition of terminology definitions, among other adjustments.

## What’s next?

The complete implementation of significant policy changes is anticipated to take time, as the information needs to reach all individual visa clerks nationwide. Therefore, it is highly advisable to familiarize yourself with the updated procedures and, if necessary, refer the clerk to the revisions.

If your application is impacted by the changes in a way that entitles you to a visa or a visa upgrade, we strongly recommend promptly [contacting PIBA](https://aic.org.il/how-to-book-an-appointment-to-piba-visa-department/) and respectfully notifying them of the new rule. In the event that your PIBA case worker refuses to acknowledge or implement the change, please [contact AIC](https://aic.org.il/help-and-services/) with all the relevant details and we’ll try to help!
