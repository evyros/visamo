# Certification for Philippine Nationals Returning to Israel

## What Is an OEC and Who Needs It?

When leaving the Philippines, Overseas Filipino Workers (OFW) are generally required to present an OEC (Overseas Employment Certificate).

However, according to Advisory No. 155 issued by the Philippine Overseas Labor Office (POLO) in Tel Aviv, holders of **unrestricted** B1 visas (such as the B1 partner visa) are exempt from this requirement. Despite this, some immigration officers at the airport may still insist on the OEC.

As a practical solution, the Philippine Embassy in Tel Aviv can issue a **notarized certificate** confirming that a person holds an unrestricted B1 or A5 visa based on partnership with an Israeli. This certificate is intended for **presentation to Philippine Immigration Authorities** to facilitate the traveler’s return to Israel and to prove exemption from the OEC requirement.

In practice, while Filipinos who are partners of Israelis and hold a partner visa technically do **not** need an OEC, it is strongly recommended to obtain a notarized certificate from the Philippine Embassy before traveling to the Philippines as a precautionary measure.

## How to Obtain Certification From the Philippine Embassy in Tel Aviv?

Procedure follows regular notarial requirements in the embassy. No appointment is required; only walk-ins are entertained. Next of kin and authorized representatives are allowed to apply for the documents on behalf of someone else.

**Requirements**: 

1. Photocopy of passports (Filipino and Israeli Partner)
2. Photocopy of B1 / A5 Visa
3. For A5 visa holders – photocopy of Israeli ID (Teudat Zehut)
4. Authorization Letter & ID of authorized representative (when applying for someone else)

Photocopy services are available in the embassy.

**Cost: 100 NIS** (Additional 40 NIS for same-day processing). Only cash is accepted.

## Stopped at the Airport: What Should I Do?

If an immigration officer at the Philippines refuses to allow a Filipino citizen to leave the Philippines without a certificate, I recommend the following steps:

1. Politely ask the immigration officer to contact the POEA officer at the airport, who can confirm that holders of unrestricted B1 or A5 visas are exempt from the OEC requirement and do not need any additional certification.
2. If the immigration officer still insists, there may be no way to proceed at that moment. In this case, request that an authorized representative obtain the certificate on your behalf, following the steps outlined above.
