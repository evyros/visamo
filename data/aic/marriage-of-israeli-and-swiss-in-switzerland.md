# Marriage of Israeli and Swiss in Switzerland

Below you can find a description of the procedure for a civil wedding in Switzerland, between an Israeli and a Swiss citizen, with preparation on the Swiss embassy in Tel Aviv Israel.

This post was written by a volunteer community member. While our volunteers do their best to share the most accurate information, there still may be errors or inaccurate details in this post. Please keep that in mind while reading!

This procedure can be helpful for an Israeli- Swiss couple, who wants to get married and where the Swiss partner is already living in Israel (as for example a Volunteer, Student or on a B1 partner Visa). You will hand in all the required documents of you and your Israeli partner at the Swiss embassy and they will be responsible to send them to the represented registry office in Switzerland.

**Attention: this civil wedding happened in 2018 in the canton of Berne, there might be differences between the different registry office in each canton.** **There are no guarantees that the describe procedure is accute, there might be changes, only the represented registry office (Zivilstandesamt), from your hometown (Heimatort) can give you the 100% correct information about the procedure.**

## 1st step: Contact the registry office

Contact the registry office in your canton, in Switzerland, according to your hometown (Heimatort)- Easiest is to contact them the first time by phone, by e-mail is also possible, might take longer.

It is recommended to visit their website before, they have a lot of helpful information:

[https://www.zivilstand.sid.be.ch/de/start/heirat/heirat-egp.html](https://www.zivilstand.sid.be.ch/de/start/heirat/heirat-egp.html)

You will need to choose the location of the ceremony (upon availability) and they will give you a provisionally appointment for the civil wedding, which you will need to start the preparation at the Swiss embassy in Tel Aviv.

The registry office can confirm your appointment only when you complete the wedding preparation on the Swiss embassy. **Be aware that the wedding preparation might take up to three months.**

Usually, they require to hand in all the required documents, via Swiss embassy, one month before the given, provisionally appointment.

They will inform you about the further procedure of the civil wedding, after you completed the wedding preparation on the Swiss embassy.

## 2nd step: Wedding preparation at the Swiss embassy in Tel Aviv

After receiving the provisionally appointment, contact the Swiss embassy in Tel Aviv, to receive a description of the documents you need to provide for the wedding preparation.

### Documents needed from the Israeli side, for the wedding preparation on the Swiss embassy:

- Birth Certificate – תעודת לידה
- Attestation regarding marital status (never validly married) – תמצית רישום מלא
- Proof of residency – אישור תושב

You can apply for the documents mentioned above, at the Ministry of Interior (משרד הפנים) [www.pnim.gov.il](http://www.pnim.gov.il/) .

**Be aware that all the mentioned documents cannot be older than 6 months and need to be provided with an Apostille. For more information about the Apostille in Israel, see the provided document from the Swiss embassy.**

In addition, you need a valid ID and Passport (attention, names and addresses must be the same on all the documents).

### Documents needed from the Swiss side, for the wedding preparation on the Swiss embassy:

- A valid Swiss passport
- Proof of residency – Wohnsitz / Anmeldebestaetigung (you can get one from the embassy, for approximately 150 NIS)
- Email exchange with the registry office

As soon you have all the documents ready, you can contact the Swiss embassy to set an appointment for the wedding preparation.

To the appointment for the wedding preparation, you must appear with all the above-mentioned documents, and you will have to fill in a few documents for the wedding registry.

You should also clarify with your partner upfront what family name you wish after the marriage (be aware that there is no possibility anymore for double names in Switzerland).

The fees for the Embassy are around 850 NIS.

## 3rd step: Finalizing the wedding with the registry office

Again: this is an example for a civil wedding in the canton of Berne, the procedures in other cantons might be different.

The registry office will contact you as soon as they received and checked the documents from the Swiss embassy. They will register the Israeli partner in civil register of Switzerland (Personenstandsregister).

They will send you a few documents to check and to sign, and you will have to send them back.

They will finalize the details of the ceremony with you and will ask you some organizational questions.

Among other things you will need to inform them already what kind of documents / marriage certificate you want to obtain after the wedding. Therefore, it is very important that you already know what documents are required for the process to register your marriage at the Israeli ministry of interior (see different procedure).

The registry office can provide you will all the documents you will need to start the process in Israel, (except of course the Apostille) so make sure you request them upfront; it will make your process way easier.

In case the Israeli partner is not speaking German / Swiss-German (or the language of your canton) you will have to most probably look for a translator for the ceremony. It is important that the translator is not related to you or your partner. 

After you checked and approved the documents and after you finalized the details with the registry office, they will confirm your civil wedding.

## 4th step: Entry to Switzerland

As soon as you and your Israeli partner arrived in Switzerland, you will need to go with them and their passport to the counter of the registry office, so they can check their lawful residence in Switzerland.

You will also receive there already a part of the documents you required from the registry office.

And you will need to pay the fees already.

## 5th step: After the wedding ceremony

After the ceremony you will receive the marriage certificate, among the other documents you requested, and you partly already received from the registry office.

In order to start the process in Israel you will need to have an Apostille (Beglaubigung) on several of the documents (see different procedure).

In Berne you can get in in the “Staatskanzlei des Kanton Bern”, for more information [click here](https://www.sta.be.ch/de/start/dienstleistungen/dokumente-beglaubigen.html).

After this you should be all set to start the process at the Mistry of Interior in Israel.

## Costs

An estimation of the approximated costs for a civil wedding in Switzerland, please be aware that this might vary in each case:

- Preparation at the Swiss Embassy in Tel Aviv: 850 NIS (if you need a proof of residency, it will cost you an additional 150 NIS)
- Costs for the civil wedding, including the required documents for the process in Israel: approximately 350 CHF (around 1,200 NIS)

Please be aware that if you choose a special ceremony venue that there are additional costs.
