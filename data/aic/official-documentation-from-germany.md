# Official Documentation from Germany

This post was written by a volunteer community member. While our volunteers do their best to share the most accurate information, there still may be errors or inaccurate details in this post. Please keep that in mind while reading!

This post includes detailed instructions on how to obtain the official “Threshold Documents”, needed to apply for the gradual process, and how to verify them. These documents are official documentation about the foreign partner, from their home country, and they are one of the basic requirements of the gradual process. Complete documentation about the foreign partner from their home country is prerequisite of the gradual process, and without it one cannot start the gradual process and get a status in Israel based on their relationship.

Please be aware that all following information of webpages and costs are an example of one city in Germanies state of North-Rhine-Westphalia (NRW), in here Cologne. The information departs from the different states and cities. The relevant person needs to search and contact the civil/resident’s registration office of the state and city of birth or registered address in Germany.

## Birth Certificate from Germany

Name in German: 1x ***Internationale Geburtsurkunde*** (already in English language)

**How to obtain?**

- To obtain in person, by phone or online through the web page of the resident’s registration office (***Einwohnermeldeamt***) or civil registration office (***Standesamt***) of the city. The document can be picked up or will be sent to the German address.
- Delivery abroad is possible and includes additional delivery costs (check the webpage of the city’s resident’s registration office or civil registry office).
- In urgent cases the offices obtain the document directly on the spot if the request is in person.
- The request can be done by an authorized representative (family member). This person needs to submit a power of attorney (***Vollmacht***), a valid identification document of the principal (***Personalausweis***) and your own ID.

**Cost:** **18 EUR**; delivery abroad costs extra (check with your resident’s/civil registration office)

**Link:** Check the web page of your cities resident’s registration office or civil registration office. In any case check both offices! As an example, Link to the one from Cologne: **[Link](https://www.stadt-koeln.de/service/produkte/00473/index.html)**

## Personal status certificate from Germany

Name in German: ***Erweiterte Meldebescheiningung*** (extended registration certificate) contains the information about your marital status and current principal residence (***Meldeadresse***) in Germany. Make sure you ask for the extended version since this contains specifically the information about the marital status which is significant for the authorities in Israel.

**How to obtain?**

- If you are still registered in Germany, you can request the document via mail at your ***Einwohnermeldeamt*** (civil registration office). You can pick it up or you can be represented by an authorized person. This person needs to submit a power of attorney (***Vollmacht***), a valid identification document of the principal (***Personalausweis***) and your own ID.

It can also be sent by post to your family and friends.

- For Germans who are no longer registered in Germany, you can get the extended registration certificate from the civils registration office in the city where you were last registered. As a rule, you can write to them and they will send you the document by post. It is worth having the document sent to friends or family in Germany so that they can then take care of the apostille and then send it to Israel. The extended registration certificate is issued in German and must be translated into English or Hebrew by a notary in Israel.

**Cost: 9 EUR** without shipping

**[Link](https://www.stadt-koeln.de/service/produkte/00420/index.html)**

## Certificate of Non-Criminal Record from Germany

Name in German: ***Führungszeugnis*** (certificate of good conduct)

**How to obtain?**

- If you already have the electronical identity card (***Personalausweis***) you can apply for the certificate of good conduct through the online portal of ***Bundesamt für Justiz*** (federal office of justice).
- If you don’t have the electronical identity card you can apply for the certificate of good conduct per post or in person.For that you need to fill in the application form ***Antrag auf Erteilung eines Führungszeugnisses.***

You can find the form [online](https://www.bundesjustizamt.de/DE/Themen/Buergerdienste/BZR/Ausland/Antrag/FAQ_node.html#faq5504894) in German, English and French language.

For personal appearance (you get it on the spot – but call them first! They are open Monday-Friday 8:00-14:00) – address:

– Besucherservice –  
Adenauerallee 99 – 103  
53113 Bonn*

For written application – address:

*Bundesamt für Justiz**  
Referat IV 2  
53094 Bonn*

You can directly apply in advance for a ***Führungszeugnis*** in English language.

**The federal office of justice offers further information for abroad cases:**

## Verifying the documents with apostille

All official documentation from abroad that needs to be presented in Israel, must be verified by the country of its origin in order to be valid outside of it, in Israel. Many countries use “apostille” for this purpose. An apostille is usually a document, issued by an authorized authority which authenticates the documents in question.  This is used to demonstrate that they are valid documents that can be recognized in other countries.

### Obtaining apostille in Germany

**How to obtain an apostille for the *Führungszeugnis* (certificate of good conduct)**

By the ***Landesgericht*** (regional court) of the respective federal state, in this example for NRW – North-Rhine-Westphalia with its regional court in Cologne

- A: per post through an application for an apostille/legalization (to be find on their webpage – link below)
- B: Apply personally
- C: The application for an apostille to be issued by the Federal Office of Administration in Cologne can be sent to the Federal Ministry of Justice at the same time. Simply send an informal letter in which you ask that the certificate of good conduct should be sent on to the Federal Office of Administration in Cologne immediately after it is issued. You can find the form for issuing the apostille on the website of the Federal Office of Administration in Cologne. Payment for this is made separately by cash on delivery. The administrative office then sends the document to a private address in Germany.

**Cost:** 25 EUR per document

[**Link**](https://www.lg-koeln.nrw.de/aufgaben/060_dienste/030_apostillen_legalisationen/index.php)

**How to obtain an apostille for the *Internationale Geburtsurkunde* (**international birth certificate), ***beglaubigte Abschrift aus dem Geburtsregister*** (certified extract from birth registration) and ***Erweiterte Meldebescheiningung*** (extended registration certificate)?

By the ***Bezirksregierung*** (district government) of the respective federal state or city, in this example for NRW – North-Rhine-Westphalia with its district government in Cologne.

Please also ask directly in your civil registration office. They can advice further.

- By postal services through an application for an apostille/legalization (to be find on their webpage – link below)
- Apply personally with appointment!

**Cost: 25 EUR per document**

## **Further recommendations**

**When requesting the documents from Israel, the following procedure is recommended:**

- Have the official documents sent to family or friends in Germany. They should then take care of the apostille for you and then send the document (along with all other documents) to Israel.
- Avoid having the documents mailed directly to Israel from official agencies, as this usually takes a very long time for regular mail to get to Israel, if it gets there at all.
