# Visiting Israel as a tourist

This is for a visit only: the foreign partner comes to Israel as a tourist,
and goes home at the end of it, without starting the process while they're
here. If you mean to start the process during this visit, it isn't a visit:
see [entry-permit.md](entry-permit.md).

## Showing it's a visit

No foreigner has an automatic right to enter Israel, and an approved ETA-IL
doesn't guarantee entry. Having an Israeli partner isn't a reason to refuse
entry, but border control may suspect the visitor means to stay, or to start
the partner process here. The risk is higher for a citizen of a non-Western
country, on a first visit, after frequent or long visits, for a young
visitor, and with weak ties at home.

You don't need to ask Misrad Hapnim to approve the visit in advance, even
after frequent visits, or after border control gave them a shorter stay
than usual. That's only needed after a refusal (below). The foreign partner
carries reliable proof that they're coming for a visit and going home, to
show border control if they're stopped and asked about their visit:

- **A return ticket,** for a date within the time they're asking to stay.
- **Proof of their ties at home:** a letter from their employer, proof of
  studies, a lease or a home they own, family they live with or care for.
- **Honest, consistent answers** at border control: why they're coming, for
  how long, and when they're going back.

**Flights.** Without an entry permit, some airlines won't fly a foreigner to
Israel without a return ticket.

## If entry is refused at the border

The foreign partner is flown back, and should be given a paper with the
decision. They have 30 days to appeal to the Appeals Tribunal (בית הדין
לעררים), even if they got no paper. The fee is 774 NIS (2026), and the
appeal needs an affidavit signed before a lawyer: in practice, hire an
immigration lawyer. The Authority can go on with the removal after you
appeal, unless the tribunal orders it to stop. You can still start the
partner process.

## Visiting after a refusal

Once the foreign partner was refused entry, every later visit needs approval
in advance: a visa, or a tourist invitation from Misrad Hapnim.

- The Israeli partner applies at the visa department of their branch, at
  least 21 working days before the visit. Couples ask for it themselves: it
  doesn't need a lawyer.
- The office may ask for a deposit, to guarantee the visitor leaves on time.
  It's returned once they leave. In practice it's often around 15,000 NIS:
  see [deposit.md](deposit.md) for how it's paid.
- It's a separate request from the entry permit, though both are called an
  "invitation" (הזמנה).

**Never start the process after a tourist invitation.** A tourist invitation
is only for a visit. With it, you told Misrad Hapnim the visit was
temporary, and you may have left a deposit that the foreign partner leaves
on time. Filing during that visit looks like an attempt to deceive the
office: it expects the process to start while the foreign partner is abroad.
It harms your credibility for the rest of the process. To come and start the
process, ask for the entry permit instead.

## Once you've filed

From the moment you file, the foreign partner can't come to Israel before
the entry permit is granted, not even for a visit: see
[application.md](application.md).
