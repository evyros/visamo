# The other parent's consent (הסכמת ההורה השני)

The other parent's consent to the children moving to Israel and getting Israeli status. They can give it in person at the Misrad Hapnim office, by signing the application form, or in a signed letter. The ministry also contacts them itself, and no reply within 6 months counts as an objection. To skip that wait, the other parent can sign a consent declaration at the Israeli consulate, or sign a consent in front of a local notary and have it apostilled.

When the other parent is alive and has parental rights: their consent, both
to the children moving to Israel and to them getting Israeli citizenship or
permanent residency (AS/6 asks for consent to the status; the ministry asks
the other parent about both). It comes together with an official document
with their current address (see [children.md](../children.md)).

The other parent can give it:

- in person, at the Misrad Hapnim (משרד הפנים) office;
- by signing the application form (AS/6, in the application, under the children coming along);
- in a signed consent letter, attached to the application;
- at the Israeli consulate, or in front of a local notary with an apostille
  (below).

**Skipping the wait.** You can avoid waiting for the ministry to reach an
other parent who lives abroad, if they give their consent in one of these
ways:

- **At the Israeli consulate:** they go to the consul and sign a declaration
  that they consent.
- **Through a local notary:** they sign a consent in front of a notary where
  they live, and the document gets an apostille (or consular legalization,
  in a country outside the Apostille Convention; see
  [certification.md](../certification.md)).
