# Letters of recommendation (מכתבי המלצה)

At least 3 letters in Hebrew from Israeli family and friends about your relationship, each with a copy of the writer's Teudat Zehut and their contact details.

Recommendation letters are letters written by family members and friends of
the couple. They're used as proof that the relationship between the Israeli
partner and the foreign partner is real and honest: an honest relationship
is the basis on which the foreign partner can get legal status in Israel.
The procedure asks for letters from family and friends about how you met,
each with a copy of the writer's ID and their contact details (5.2.0008
§ד.2.ח).

In practice, Misrad Hapnim insists on these letters. You must extend your
efforts into finding people that will do it for you: ask your family and
friends early, and follow up until each one has written theirs.

## How to get it

Ask family members and friends who know you as a couple to write one, in
Hebrew. Israelis are highly preferred. Each writer signs their letter, and
gives you a copy of their ID to attach: their Teudat Zehut, or any official
ID for a writer who isn't Israeli.

Visamo has a free tool that makes this easier: you create one link and send
it to your friends. Each friend writes their own letter with a short guide,
signs on screen and adds a photo of their Teudat Zehut, and you get a signed
PDF in Hebrew with the copy of their ID. It's emailed to you, or your friend
downloads it and sends it to you. Visamo doesn't keep the letters or the ID
photos. In English:
https://www.visamo.co.il/en/tools/recommendation-letter, in Hebrew:
https://www.visamo.co.il/he/tools/recommendation-letter.

## What it should include

- **At least 3 letters,** from different writers. It's recommended to ask
  different kinds of people: family members, close and further, and friends
  from different circles.
- **From Israelis, highly preferred:** family members or friends who are
  Israeli. A letter from someone who isn't Israeli but has seen you together
  is also accepted.
- **In Hebrew,** also from a writer who isn't Israeli: it's strongly
  preferred.
- **Who the writer is,** and how they know you.
- **What they know of your relationship:** how you met, how long you've been
  together, and what they've seen of your life together.
- **Usually about 3 paragraphs long.** The shorter your relationship, or
  the less other proof you have that you live together, the more detailed
  the letters should be in order to build a strong file.
- **The writer's signature and the date.**
- **The writer's contact details,** and **a copy of their ID** with the
  letter: their Teudat Zehut, or any official ID for a writer who isn't
  Israeli.
- **If you haven't lived together:** the letters should say that the writer
  saw you together. They're part of the stronger case you'll need (see
  [documents.md](../documents.md)).

## If you don't have it

**If you can't find people to write them, or don't know enough people:**
ask Israelis further out who have seen you together: neighbours, colleagues,
friends you're less close with. What matters is that they've seen you as a
couple, not how close they are to you. If you still can't reach 3, ask
people who aren't Israeli but have seen you together. Their letters need the
same things: a signature, their contact details and a copy of their ID.
Hebrew is strongly preferred.

## Questions

**Are the letters really needed?**
Yes. In practice, Misrad Hapnim insists on them. If you can't get them from
the people closest to you, ask others who have seen you together (see above).
