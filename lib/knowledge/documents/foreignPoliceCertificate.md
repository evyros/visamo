# Police certificate (תעודת יושר)

A certificate of no criminal record from the country.

An official document from the foreign country about the foreign partner's
criminal record, or that they have none. Every applicant over 14 needs one.

**From which countries:**

- **Their country of citizenship.** If they have more than one citizenship,
  offices usually ask for one from each, even a country they never lived in.
- **Each other country they lived in for at least 6 months in a row**
  (5.2.0008 and 5.2.0009, since January 2024). Shorter stays, and visits,
  don't count.
- **Not a country they lived in only as a child under 14,** if they can show
  it.

## How to get it

It comes from the national authority that keeps criminal records, in each
country. Ask for the paper original, not one you print at home: many offices
reject a digital one, and many countries can't apostille it. Below, what the
common countries issue.

### Argentina

A certificate of criminal record (certificado de antecedentes penales), from
the National Recidivism Registry (Registro Nacional de Reincidencia), online.
The apostille comes from the foreign ministry (Cancillería).

### Australia

A National Police Check, from the Australian Federal Police (AFP), online. It
comes digital by default: ask for the printed original on the form. The
apostille comes from the Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade (DFAT), or
the Australian embassy in Tel Aviv by appointment.

### Austria

A criminal record certificate (Strafregisterbescheinigung), from the state
police headquarters (Landespolizeidirektion). Its apostille comes from the
Ministry of the Interior (Innenministerium).

### Belgium

An extract from the criminal record (uittreksel uit het strafregister, extrait
de casier judiciaire), from the municipality where they live, or online with
an e-ID. The apostille comes from the Federal Public Service Foreign Affairs.

### Bolivia

A certificate of criminal record (certificado de antecedentes penales), from
the departmental court of justice (Palacio de Justicia), in person or through
someone with a notarized power of attorney. The apostille is ordered there
too.

### Brazil

A criminal record certificate (certidão de antecedentes criminais), from
the Federal Police (Polícia Federal). It's issued free, online, with an
electronic verification code. Any registry office (cartório) adds the
apostille.

### Canada

A certified criminal record check from the Royal Canadian Mounted Police
(RCMP), based on fingerprints. It's applied for through a company the RCMP
accredits: from abroad, the fingerprints are taken on paper and sent to
one.

### Chile

A criminal record certificate for special purposes (certificado de
antecedentes para fines especiales), from the civil registry (Registro Civil),
at a branch or online. The civil registry also apostilles it.

### China

A certificate of no criminal record (无犯罪记录证明), from the local Public
Security Bureau, often through its WeChat service. Ask for the paper
certificate by post, not the online one. It's notarized, then apostilled by the
foreign ministry or a local foreign affairs office.

### Croatia

A certificate from the criminal record (potvrda iz kaznene evidencije), not
the certificate of no conviction (potvrda o nekažnjavanju), from the Ministry
of Justice, in person, usually on the spot. The apostille comes from the
municipal civil court.

### Cuba

A criminal record certificate (certificación de antecedentes penales), from
the Central Register of Convicted Persons (Registro Central de Sancionados)
of the Ministry of Justice. For use abroad, it's a kind of its own
("Exterior Personal"), obtained and legalized only through the
international law firms, such as the Consultoría Jurídica Internacional
(CJI). A Cuban living abroad asks for it through a Cuban consulate, which
can take months. It also lists sentences of foreign courts against Cuban
citizens. In Cuba it's valid for a year, but Misrad Hapnim asks for one
issued in the last 6 months. Get it on paper, not the digital certificate
from the online service: Israel accepts a digital police certificate only
with an electronic apostille (1.3.0001 §4.3.3), and Cuba issues none.

Cuba isn't in the Apostille Convention, and Israel has no embassy in Cuba,
so the legalization goes on through Canada (see
[certification.md](../certification.md)): the Cuban Ministry of Justice
(MINJUS), then the Canadian embassy in Havana, then Global Affairs Canada,
and last the Israeli embassy in Canada. It's filed through an international
law firm in Cuba, such as the CJI, or through a Cuban consulate abroad.

### Cyprus

A clean criminal record certificate (πιστοποιητικό λευκού ποινικού
μητρώου), from the Cyprus Police's Previous Convictions Registry. It's
issued to Cypriot citizens, and to foreigners who lived legally in Cyprus
for at least 6 months. Apply at the registry at police headquarters in
Nicosia, or at a divisional police headquarters, in person or through
someone you authorize in writing. Bring a copy of your ID card or passport;
a foreigner brings their passport and residence permit (the pink or yellow
slip, or the alien book). It costs 20 euros and usually takes 3 working
days, longer for citizens of other EU countries. The police's online
service (DIGIPOL) doesn't issue a certificate that needs an apostille, so
don't order it there. From abroad, send the application form to the police
headquarters in Nicosia, with the fee paid at a Cypriot embassy or by bank
transfer. The apostille comes from the Ministry of Justice and Public
Order, in Nicosia or Limassol; a Cypriot embassy can forward the
certificate there for you.

### Czech Republic

An extract from the criminal register (výpis z rejstříku trestů), best from the
Criminal Register (Rejstřík trestů) in Prague, saying it's for an apostille so
they use the right stamp. One from a CzechPOINT office can be harder to
apostille. It's also issued at Czech embassies. The apostille comes from the
Ministry of Foreign Affairs.

### Denmark

A criminal record certificate (straffeattest), in English, from any police
station, with a handwritten signature and the signer's name. A digital one
from borger.dk can't get a physical apostille. The apostille comes from the
Ministry of Foreign Affairs in Copenhagen.

### France

An extract of the criminal record, bulletin no. 3 (extrait de casier
judiciaire, bulletin n° 3), from the national criminal record office
(Casier judiciaire national). It's free, online. Since May 2025, the
apostille comes from the notaries' apostille centres, not the Rennes Court
of Appeal: apply online, or at one of the centres. The extract has to be
less than 3 months old.

### Germany

A certificate of good conduct (Führungszeugnis), from the Federal Office of
Justice (Bundesamt für Justiz). For an apostille, first apply for it online
with the Federal Foreign Affairs Office (Bundesamt für Auswärtige
Angelegenheiten), then request the certificate with the number it gives
you.

### Greece

A copy of the criminal record (αντίγραφο ποινικού μητρώου). It's free, and
the office that issues it depends on where you were born, whether or not
you're Greek:

- **Born in Greece:** the criminal records department of the public
  prosecutor's office at the court of first instance (Εισαγγελία
  Πρωτοδικών), or a Citizen Service Centre (KEP). The apostille comes from
  the court of first instance (Πρωτοδικείο) of that area.
- **Born abroad:** the Criminal Records Department of the Ministry of
  Justice (Αυτοτελές Τμήμα Ποινικού Μητρώου), in Athens. The apostille comes
  from the Decentralized Administration (Αποκεντρωμένη Διοίκηση), and only
  while the copy is less than 3 months old.

It can also be downloaded from gov.gr, but only a paper copy signed by the
office gets an apostille. Greek consulates don't issue it: apply in Greece,
yourself or through someone you authorize there. It's in Greek, and few
notaries in Israel translate from Greek, so find one early.

### Hungary

An official certificate of good conduct (hatósági erkölcsi bizonyítvány), at
any post office in Hungary, online through Ügyfélkapu, or at the Hungarian
embassy. The apostille comes from the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, or the
embassy.

### Iceland

A criminal record certificate (sakavottorð), from the district commissioner
(sýslumaður), on request by email from abroad. The apostille comes from the
Ministry for Foreign Affairs.

### India

A police clearance certificate (PCC), through Passport Seva.

### Italy

Two certificates: the criminal record certificate (certificato del casellario
giudiziale) and the certificate of pending charges (certificato dei carichi
pendenti). You ask for each in writing at a public prosecutor's office
(Procura della Repubblica). Their apostille comes from the prosecutor's office
at the court (Procura della Repubblica presso il Tribunale), not from the
prefecture that apostilles other Italian documents.

### Japan

A certificate of criminal record (犯罪経歴証明書), through the Japanese embassy
in Israel: you apply in person, with your fingerprints, and collect it about a
month later.

### Mexico

The federal certificate of no criminal record (constancia de antecedentes
penales federales), issued only in Mexico City, by appointment. You need a
letter from the Israeli embassy in Mexico showing it's for use abroad; from
Israel, go through the Mexican embassy. Its apostille is federal, from the
Ministry of the Interior (SEGOB) in Mexico City, unlike other Mexican
documents.

### Nepal

A police clearance certificate, from the Nepal Police headquarters in Naxal,
Kathmandu. It's applied for online, in Nepali or English, through the Nepal
Police's online police clearance system, and it's free.

Nepal isn't in the Apostille Convention, so it's legalized (see
[certification.md](../certification.md)): the foreign ministry's Department
of Consular Services in Tripureshwor, Kathmandu, then the Israeli embassy in
Kathmandu. The Israeli embassy's legalization is filed through VFS in
Kathmandu: you hand in the original there, with the fee, and don't visit the
embassy.

### Netherlands

A certificate of conduct (verklaring omtrent gedrag, VOG), from Justis. You
request it yourself, attaching a printout of the Israeli government's page
that shows you need it. Any court (rechtbank) adds the apostille.

### Peru

A certificate of criminal record (certificado de antecedentes penales), from
the judiciary's National Register of Convictions (Registro Nacional de
Condenas del Poder Judicial). Get it on paper, at a Poder Judicial office. A
Peruvian living abroad can ask for it through a Peruvian consulate, or have
someone in Peru apply for it with their written authorization or a power of
attorney. The police (PNP) issue a separate certificate of police record
(certificado de antecedentes policiales), about police records rather than
convictions. The apostille comes from the foreign ministry (Cancillería).

### Philippines

An NBI clearance, from the National Bureau of Investigation. The apostille
is put only on the original, printed on the NBI's security paper.

### Portugal

A criminal record certificate (certificado do registo criminal), at any
citizen's office (Loja do Cidadão) or court. Choose a wide purpose, such as
work abroad (exercício de profissão/atividade no estrangeiro). The apostille
comes from the regional prosecutor's office (Procuradoria-Geral Regional).

### Russia

A certificate of no criminal record (справка об отсутствии судимости), from
the Ministry of Internal Affairs (МВД), or through Gosuslugi. Order it on
paper with the apostille: an electronic certificate can't get one.

### Singapore

A certificate of clearance (COC), from the Singapore Police Force, online
through Singpass. The apostille comes from the Singapore Academy of Law.

### Slovenia

A certificate from the criminal record (potrdilo iz kazenske evidence), from
the Ministry of Justice, in person, by post, or by email for those registered
as living abroad. The apostille comes from a district court.

### South Africa

A police clearance certificate (PCC), from the South African Police Service,
with your fingerprints. You can apply from abroad by courier, with fingerprints
taken at a local police station or the embassy. It takes 8 to 12 weeks, plus
6 to 8 weeks for the apostille from the Department of International Relations
(DIRCO), and it's valid for 6 months: plan the timing.

### Spain

A criminal record certificate (certificado de antecedentes penales), by
appointment at the Ministry of Justice or a government sub-delegation, with
form 790 (the fee, paid at a bank). The apostille is done on the spot at the
same office: bring the birth certificate and the civil-status document with
you, and they apostille those too.

### Sweden

An extract from the criminal register (utdrag ur belastningsregistret), from
the Police (Polisen), online. Any notary public (notarius publicus) adds the
apostille.

### Switzerland

An extract from the criminal record (Strafregisterauszug, extrait du casier
judiciaire), from the Federal Office of Justice, online or at a Swiss Post
branch. The apostille comes from the cantonal chancellery or the Federal
Chancellery.

### Tanzania

A police clearance certificate (also called a certificate of good conduct),
from the Forensic Bureau of the police's Criminal Investigation Department,
at its headquarters in Dar es Salaam. In Tanzania, apply there in person, with
a passport or national ID card: your fingerprints and palm prints are taken
there. From abroad, have the police where you live take your fingerprints,
and send them to the Forensic Bureau with a letter asking for the
certificate, giving the last place you lived in Tanzania (district, town,
street and house number), a copy of your passport's details page, two
passport photos, and proof that you paid the 25-dollar fee. Police records
for Zanzibar are kept apart: ask the Criminal Investigation Department at
the police headquarters in Zanzibar, the same way.

Tanzania isn't in the Apostille Convention, so it's legalized (see
[certification.md](../certification.md)): Tanzania's Ministry of Foreign
Affairs and East African Cooperation, then the Israeli embassy. Before the
ministry, the office that issued the document verifies it: the ministry
certifies a document only after that. Israel has no embassy in Tanzania: its
embassy in Nairobi, Kenya, also serves Tanzania, and the chain ends there.
Hand in the original at the embassy in Nairobi, by appointment; someone else
can hand it in for you.

### Thailand

A police clearance certificate (หนังสือรับรองความประพฤติ), from the Police
Clearance Service Center of the Royal Thai Police, at its headquarters in
Bangkok. It takes a few days. It can also be issued in English, which takes
a bit longer. Thailand isn't in the Apostille Convention, so it's
translated and certified by the Consular Affairs Department of the Thai
Ministry of Foreign Affairs, then by the Israeli embassy.

### Turkmenistan

A certificate of no criminal liability (справка о непривлечении к уголовной
ответственности), from an information unit of the Ministry of Internal
Affairs (МВД). From abroad, it's asked for through a Turkmen embassy or
consulate, in person or with a notarized power of attorney, and takes up to
3 months.

Turkmenistan isn't in the Apostille Convention, so it's legalized (see
[certification.md](../certification.md)), ending at the Israeli embassy in
Ashgabat.

### Ukraine

A certificate of no criminal record (довідка про несудимість), from the
Ministry of Internal Affairs (МВС), at its service centers or through Diia.
The apostille goes on the original, not a copy.

### United Kingdom

A police certificate from ACRO, the UK's criminal records office, with your
address history for the last 10 years. It isn't a DBS check. ACRO doesn't add
the apostille: it comes from the Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office
(FCDO).

### United States

An FBI Identity History Summary, from the FBI. You apply with your
fingerprints. It's a federal document, so its apostille comes from the US
Department of State, not from a state.

The State Department issues the apostille only by mail, or in person in
Washington, DC, by appointment. If someone you trust lives in the United
States, send them the certificate and have them submit it for the apostille.
The full process is on the State Department's website, under requesting
authentication services.

## What it should include

- **Issued in the last 6 months.**
- **Every name they've had.** By the procedure's definition, it covers all
  the foreign partner's name changes.
- **One from each country:** each country of citizenship, and each other
  country they lived in for at least 6 months in a row (above).
- **The certification** of the country that issued it: an apostille, or
  consular legalization if that country isn't in the Apostille Convention
  (see [certification.md](../certification.md)).
- **A notarized translation (תרגום נוטריוני) into Hebrew**, by a notary in
  Israel, unless it's in Hebrew or Arabic: a translator's translation, even a
  certified one, will be rejected. It's an official document, unlike bills, letters or bank
  statements, which you can translate yourselves. In practice,
  an English document is usually accepted without one, but the clerk can
  still ask you to translate it.

## After you get it

In practice, from the day the police certificate is issued until the
foreign partner gets the B/1 visa, they shouldn't travel to another country.
Misrad Hapnim (משרד הפנים) knows every exit from Israel and every entry into it. A trip
abroad after the certificate was issued is time it doesn't cover, so the
office can no longer be sure no offense was committed abroad. If they did
travel, they need a new police certificate from their country of origin.

## Questions

**My partner has a criminal record abroad. What happens?**
It's a complicated case, and we recommend going through the process with a
lawyer who handles partner visas. The office asks for detailed information about it, from the authorities in
Israel and abroad, the court's sentence (גזר דין) from their country, and
any other documents the clerk asks for (5.2.0008 §ה.2(6)). By the
procedure, a criminal past isn't only a conviction: being wanted or
suspected of an offense that points to a danger counts too.

**Can my partner travel abroad after getting the police certificate?**
In practice, not until they have the B/1 visa: a trip after it's issued is
time it doesn't cover (see above).

**My partner already traveled abroad after getting it. What now?**
They need a new police certificate from their country of origin.

**My partner changed their name. Does the police certificate need to show the old one?**
Yes. By the procedure, it covers every name the foreign partner has had.

**My partner lived abroad for a few months as a student. Do we need one from there?**
Only if they lived there for at least 6 months in a row. Shorter stays don't
count.

**My partner has two citizenships. Do we need one from both countries?**
Usually yes, even from a country they never lived in: offices ask for one
from each country of citizenship.

**Do the children need one too?**
Each child over 14 who moves to Israel needs their own police certificate
(see [children.md](../children.md)).
