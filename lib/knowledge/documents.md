# Documents for the first application

The full list is `lib/documents/catalog.ts`. This file records what each
document is, where the requirement comes from, and every correction, so the
reasoning isn't lost. Sources: procedure 5.2.0008 (edition 16) §ד.2, the AS/6
checklist, and practice.

## General rules

- **Center of life:** proof covering **at least the last 12 months** (the
  procedure says "at least the last year"; practice confirmed 12 months).
- **Copies** ("2X" on the AS/6 checklist means 2 copies). Copies are shown to
  the couple only. They don't change which documents are needed.
- **Translation:** by law, only Hebrew and Arabic need none. English is usually
  accepted in practice, but a clerk can ask for a translation. Others need a
  certified (notarized) translation, which itself needs no apostille. See
  certification.md.
- **The fee is not a document.** It isn't on the list (practice).
- **"Both partners must be present"** at the appointment is on the AS/6
  checklist. It's ignored: not a document (practice).

## Forms

- **AS/6** (אש/6): the status application for a married couple. Common-law
  couples use a different application (5.2.0009; number not confirmed).
- **AS/1** (אש/1): the entry permit application, when the foreign partner is
  outside Israel (§ד.2.א).
- **Affidavits** of each partner, in the ministry's wording (AS/6 page 3):
  that the documents are true, and that they'll report any change. **Signed
  in front of a lawyer** (practice. The form only says "in the attached
  wording.")
- **A letter about the relationship**, signed by both: how and when you met,
  the relationship, your life together (§ד.2.ו). Adding each person's life
  history is **recommended when relevant, not required** (practice).
- **Full CV for the security check** (§ד.2.ט): **not a job CV.** A history of
  employment and life, for applicants from sensitive countries. See
  former-ussr-and-security.md.

## Identity

- **Teudat Zehut with the appendix (ספח)** (§ד.2.ג).
- **Passport photos: 3 copies of the same passport-format photo, of each
  partner.** An earlier correction said the foreign partner only; the
  procedure (§ד.2.ב: two of each partner) and the AS/6 checklist (three of
  the invitee and the inviter) both say both partners, and practice agreed:
  3 of each.
- **Foreign passport**, valid for at least 2 more years (§ד.2.ד), with copies
  of the main page, **the visa pages and the border-control stamps** (AS/6).
  This replaced a separate "current visa" item.
- **Birth certificate**, original, from the country of birth; 2 copies
  (§ד.2.ה; AS/6). For the former-USSR exemption, see
  former-ussr-and-security.md.
- **Proof of a name change**, if the name changed (§ד.2.ה).
- **An explanation of the stay** when the foreign partner is in Israel without
  a valid visa: not confirmed by any source yet.

## The relationship

- **Marriage certificate** (§ד.2.ה). From the rabbinate or religious court if
  married in Israel; otherwise from the country or authority that married
  them, certified (see certification.md).
- **Evidence of the relationship:** photos together, letters, anything showing
  how they met and how long they've been together (§ד.2.ח).
- **Letters of recommendation** from family and friends, **each with a copy of
  the writer's ID and contact details** (§ד.2.ח; practice confirmed).
- **Common-law, living together:** evidence of living together (5.2.0009, not
  checked yet).

## Civil status

- **Proof of the foreign partner's civil status, for every couple**, including
  those married in Israel (practice). Issued **in the last 6 months** (§ד.2.ה),
  2 copies. If the country doesn't issue one: a notarized affidavit. If the
  marriage certificate is from the foreign partner's country and records
  their status before the marriage, it can replace it.
- **Previous marriages** ("previous and current civil status", AS/6): a
  divorce decree or a spouse's death certificate for each ending, 2 copies.
  Also the Israeli partner's (not confirmed by a source yet).

## Criminal record

- **Police certificate** from the foreign partner's country (§ד.2.ה, AS/6:
  "from the invitee's country"). Every applicant over 14 (§ד.2).
- From each other country lived in: in the catalog, **not confirmed yet**.

## Center of life (every couple)

- **Lease or purchase contract** of the home, in the Israeli partner's name:
  original, copy and the lease appendix (AS/6; §ד.2.ח).
- **Bills** (electricity, water, arnona, phone), **in the renter's name or
  the Israeli partner's** (AS/6; practice).
- **Proof of government services:** National Insurance (Bituach Leumi),
  health, education (AS/6; §ד.2.ח).
- **Ishur Toshav** (אישור תושב): the municipality or local council's
  confirmation of living there, in the ministry's wording (AS/6).
- **Shared finances: a joint bank account.** Without one, other proof of shared
  money: transfers to each other, credit-card statements with household
  purchases or the same delivery address, receipts for large expenses
  (practice; AS/6 lists the joint account).
- **Employment or income proof, for each partner**, depending on how they
  earn (practice. The AS/6 checklist says workplace confirmations and 3
  payslips only, and is wrong on both counts.):
  - **Employed (שכיר):** a confirmation from work and **the last 6 payslips**.
  - **Self-employed (עצמאי):**
    - an accountant's confirmation of current income (אישור רואה חשבון על
      הכנסות נוכחיות);
    - the latest annual tax assessment from the Tax Authority (שומה שנתית
      אחרונה ממס הכנסה);
    - the confirmation of opening a business file, osek murshe or patur
      (אישור פתיחת תיק עוסק מורשה/פטור).
  - **Not working, but with an income:** bank statements showing the
    deposits.
  - **Not working and no income:** a clear, signed letter explaining the
    person's situation (for example a student, between jobs, or a homemaker),
    stating explicitly that their partner financially supports their shared
    life in Israel.
  - **The Israeli partner (the sponsor) has to show they can support the
    household:** recent payslips (tlushim) and bank statements, and the tax
    assessment (shuma) if self-employed.
  - **A shuma is only for the self-employed (עצמאי)**, never for employees
    (practice).
  - **If the sponsor's income is borderline or tight**, add alternative
    assets or support: savings accounts and Kupat Holim (HMO) records. Utility
    bills (already their own document) help show a stable center of life
    together.
  - These are Israeli documents. For a foreign partner who is self-employed
    abroad, the equivalents aren't known yet (see open-questions.md).
  - Onboarding doesn't ask how each partner earns, and won't: one income-proof
    item per partner whose description lists all three cases (decided with
    the product owner).
- **School records** for children in school in Israel from age 6 (AS/6). See
  children.md.

Earlier, center-of-life proof was one item only for permanent residents and
people who lived abroad. That was wrong: it's for **every** couple, and it's
several documents.
