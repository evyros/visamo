# Getting Married While In the Gradual Process – Implications and Procedure

Are you an unmarried international couple going through the partner visa process in Israel and have decided to get married? Congratulations!

Here you will find everything you need to know! 🙂

## Where to Get Married?

As you probably already know, the State of Israel does not conduct civil marriages, and the only marriages legally recognized are religious marriages conducted by one of the official religions in Israel. Therefore, in order to legally marry in Israel, you must meet the requirements of the relevant religious authority.

This usually means that both partners must belong to the same religion legally. If they do not, marriage in Israel may be very complicated or even impossible. In such cases, couples can marry abroad. Israel will recognize valid marriage certificates from abroad and will register you as a married couple.

From 2024, the option of marriage via video conference in Utah was also [fully recognized for partner visa procedures](https://aic.org.il/protocol-5-2-0008-update-utah-zoom-weddings-to-be-treated-same-as-other-proxy-marriages/). This means couples can get married without having to leave Israel, or even without being in the same place. Check our [step-by-step guide to Utah Zoom weddings](https://aic.org.il/utah-zoom-wedding-step-by-step-guide/)!

Israel also offers an option for civil relationship registration called “Brit Hazugiot.” This option is available to Israeli **residents** who are both registered in the population registry as “No-Religion.” This process, unlike civil marriages abroad, **does not grant the same rights as marriage and is specifically not recognized for immigration purposes or the gradual process**. Therefore, it is not recommended for international couples.

## We Got Married! What’s Next?

Congratulations!

According to [Israeli law](https://www.nevo.co.il/law_html/law01/289_001.htm#Seif17), Israeli residents must notify the authorities about their marriage within 30 days of marriage\*.

In the case of international couples in the gradual process, the registration of the marriage will be usually done through the visa department, which is handling your partner visa file. It is advisable to **[contact PIBA](https://aic.org.il/contact-details-and-information-about-visa-departments-of-piba/) as soon as possible after the marriage**, so you can get an appointment quickly and convert your file to a married couple’s file.

You may be instructed to complete the marriage registration at the registry department first, before converting your file at the visa department.

### Required Documents

In order to change your file into a marriage visa file, you will need to provide documents according to procedure 5.2.0008\*\*:

- **Marriage certificate** – authenticated (apostilled), and translated (marriage certificates originally in English will usually be accepted without translation). The marriage certificate will be checked according to procedure 2.11.0001.
- [**Form MR6**](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/generalpage/services_forms/he/MR6.pdf) – Notice of change of personal status.
- **[Form AS6](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/generalpage/visas_forms/he/AS6.pdf)** – This form includes two [affidavits](https://aic.org.il/affidavits-in-israel/). The full form is not always required – consult PIBA when making the appointment.
- **Certificate of current personal status** – The requirement to prove that the marriage has been registered in the foreign partner’s home country was canceled in January 2024. However, we highly recommend registering the marriage, if possible, and submitting the appropriate certificate to avoid additional requirements at the end of the process. [Read more information here.](https://aic.org.il/2024-big-updates-to-partner-visa-procedures-5-2-0008-5-2-0009/)
- **Certificate of previous personal status (single status)** – An updated version of this certificate is not always required, as it has been submitted by the couple at the beginning of the process.
- **Opening Fee Payment** – Only if you did not pay the application opening fee as an unmarried couple (applications submitted before 2022).

\*This rule is not strictly enforced and you can proceed with the registration normally even if 30 days have passed.  
\*\*PIBA is allowed to ask for further documents and proofs.

## How Does Marriage Affect Your Partner Visa?

The million-dollar question: **Will getting married speed up our path to Israeli citizenship?** **Does marriage shorten the gradual process?**

The answer? **It depends!** It all comes down to what stage of the process you were in when you got married.

Here’s a reminder of the timeline [gradual process](https://aic.org.il/the-gradual-process-birds-eye-view/), according to the marital status of the couple:

- **Heterosexual Married Couples:** B1 visa as an interim status for up to 6 months — A5 visa for a minimum of 4 years → A choice between Israeli citizenship or permanent residency.
- **Same-Sex Married Couples:** B1 visa as an interim status for up to 6 months — A5 visa for a minimum of 4 years → Permanent residency → An option to apply for citizenship.
- **Non-Married Couples:** B1 visa for a minimum of 3 years → A5 visa for a minimum of 4 years → Permanent residency → An option to apply for citizenship.

As you can see, the main difference between the timelines is the time spent on a B1 visa: **up to 6 months when married but a minimum of 3 years when not married.** This means that if you got married while already holding a B1 visa (for example, after a year), you should be eligible for an A5 visa immediately, subject to an interview and document submission.

However, if you were already holding A5 status at the time of your marriage, the marriage will not significantly impact your process timeline. You will still need to complete 4 years on an A5 visa before becoming eligible for Israeli citizenship. As a married couple, though, you can apply for citizenship immediately after completing 4 years on an A5 visa, without first obtaining permanent resident status. This can save you several months on the path to citizenship.

Unfortunately, if you are a same-sex couple, getting married while already on an A5 visa will not speed up the process. Same-sex couples must obtain permanent residency before applying for Israeli citizenship, just like unmarried couples (read more [here](https://aic.org.il/israeli-citizenship-for-partners-of-israelis-application-and-procedures/)).
