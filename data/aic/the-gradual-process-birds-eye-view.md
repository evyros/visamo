# Partner Visa or Spouse Visa in Israel – Overview

You’ve made the decision that you want to live together in Israel? Congratulations!

In this article we will give you a quick (but thorough) overview of the Israeli “partner visa”, which can also be referred to as a “spouse visa”, “marriage visa”, “shared life visa” etc.

The partner visa is specifically made for international couples, where the foreign partner does not qualify for immigration to Israel under the Law of Return, meaning non-Jewish partners of Israelis who don’t have Jewish ancestry.

Foreign partners of all Israelis (both Israeli citizens and Israeli Permanent Residents) can be eligible for a partner visa. This article will focus on the gradual process for couples in which the Israeli side is an Israeli *citizen*.

## What is a Partner Visa / Spouse Visa?

The term “partner visa” is a general name for various visa categories that a non-Israeli partner of an Israeli can obtain in Israel. **All types of partner visas** allow their holders to legally work in Israel (both as an employee and as an independent worker) and live in Israel, together with their Israeli partner, as long as the relationship lasts.

Partner visas are obtained gradually, as a part of a complete naturalization process that finally results in a permanent status in Israel – Either a Permanent Resident status or Israeli citizenship. This process is called “The Gradual Process” (Halich Medorag – הליך מדורג). The gradual process for married couples is also called “family reunification” (Ihud Mishpahot – איחוד משפחות / אחמ”ש), and the gradual process for non-married couples is also called “shared life” (Haim Meshutafim – חיים משותפים).

### The Different Types of Partner Visas

During the gradual process, a non-Israeli partner of an Israeli will receive 2 different types of visas, depending on the stage of the process.

- **B1 General (ב/1 כללי) – temp. working permit (רישיון זמני לעבודה)** – This is an unrestricted work visa, and the initial status the non-Israeli partner will get. It is imprinted with the words “בן/בת זוג” (“partner”) and it allows its holder to work in Israel unrestrictedly. Not to be confused with restricted B1 visas, which are granted to work immigrants and are restricted to a specific field/employer (caregiving, agriculture, expert, etc.)
- **A5 (א/5) – Generally Temporary (ארעי כללי)** – This visa is a residency visa, that grants the status of a temporary resident in Israel and an Israeli ID. In addition to the right to work, it also grants its holder full social rights in Israel, including state health insurance, tax credit points and much more.

### Who is Eligible for a Partner visa in Israel?

Most couples consisting of an Israeli and a non-Israeli can apply for a partner visa, which will allow the non-Israeli partner to live in Israel with their Israeli partner. This includes married couples and unmarried couples. Same-sex couples are also welcome, and they too can apply as both married or unmarried couples.

The partner visa is available for foreign citizens of any country in the world, with a few exceptions. Citizens of Iran, Iraq, Syria, and Lebanon are not eligible to get a status in Israel as partners of Israelis. In addition, heavy restrictions are applied to residents of the Judea-Samaria region and Gaza.

## What is The Gradual Process?

The gradual process is a name for a long bureaucratic process, through which foreign spouses or partners of Israelis can obtain stay permits in Israel (partner visas), in a gradual manner.

In the gradual process, the couple will be required to periodically prove the honesty of the relationship and their shared center of life in Israel. This is done through interviews, submission of relationship proofs (such as shared photos and recommendation letters), and center-of-life documents.

The process in its entirety (from initial visa until eligibility to Israeli citizenship or permanent residency) ranges between ~4.5 years to 7+ years in length, depending on the marital status of the couple.

The gradual process is taking place at the visa department of the Population and Immigration Authority (PIBA). It is dictated by two official PIBA procedures – [Procedure 5.2.0008](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/policy/status_foreigner_married_to_israeli_procedure/he/5.2.0008.pdf) for married couples, and [Procedure 5.2.0009](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/policy/israelis_couples_status_procedure/he/5.2.0009.pdf) for non-married couples and for same-sex couples.

## The Stages of the Gradual Process

The length of each stage of the gradual process differs between married and unmarried couples. In addition, there is a small difference in the process between heterosexual married couples and homosexual married couples.

However, all couples in the gradual process go through these stages:

**B****1 General Visa**\*

**↓**

**A5 Visa (temporary residency)**

↓

**Permanent status  
(Permanent residency or Israeli citizenship)**

**\*For married couples, the B1 status is not a part of the gradual process.** The B1 visa will be granted as an interim status only, outside of the process’ timeline, for a period of **up to 6 months**. Within those 6 months or less, PIBA must complete this checks, interviews, etc. and provide a decision regarding the A5 visa.

##### Gradual Process for Heterosexual Married Couples:

(B1 up to 6 months as interim status) — A5 visa for 4 years **→** A choice between Israeli citizenship or permanent residency

##### Gradual Process for Same-Sex Married Couples:

(B1 up to 6 months as interim status) — A5 visa for 4 years **→** Permanent residency **→** An option to apply for an Israeli citizenship

##### Gradual Process for Non-Married Couples:

B1 visa for 3 years **→** A5 visa for 4 years **→** Permanent residency **→** An option to apply for an Israeli citizenship

B1 and A5 visas are valid for up to one year and must be renewed annually.

## How do we start?

Glad you asked!

There are 3 possible scenarios when deciding to start the Israeli partner visa process, and each scenario results in a slightly different application process.

1. **Both partners are in Israel:** If the foreign partner already resides in Israel under a different status (e.g., student, work immigrant, refugee), the application is submitted online.
2. **Israeli partner is in Israel, non-Israeli partner is abroad:** The application is submitted online. An entry clearance for the non-Israeli partner will be granted once the initial application is approved.
3. **Both partners are abroad:** An entry clearance request is submitted to the Israeli consulate in the country of residence. The consulate forwards the application to PIBA for a decision on entry. Once in Israel, the application is submitted online.

**Ready to start?**

For scenarios 1 and 2, jump over to our amazing [step-by-step guide](https://aic.org.il/partner-visa-in-israel-step-by-step-guide-to-your-first-visa/), to find a detailed, step-by-step breakdown of how to start the gradual process and obtain your first partner visa!

For scenario 3, please contact the [Israeli embassy](https://www.gov.il/he/Departments/dynamiccollectors/israeli-consular-services?skip=0) in your country of residence, to get information and instructions on how to initiate the gradual process from abroad.

## Conclusion

Obtaining a partner visa or spousal visa in Israel is a lengthy bureaucratic process that requires diligence and patience. However, the gradual process remains the most reliable path for international couples seeking to establish their center of life in Israel. For those not eligible for Aliyah, it is one of the few ways to eventually obtain Israeli citizenship.
