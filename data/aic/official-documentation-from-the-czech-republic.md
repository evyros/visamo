# Official Documentation from the Czech Republic

This post was written by a volunteer community member. While our volunteers do their best to share the most accurate information, there still may be errors or inaccurate details in this post. Please keep that in mind while reading!

This post includes detailed instructions on how to obtain and authenticate the official documentation and certifications from abroad, that are needed to apply for a partner or spouse visa in Israel. The basic personal documents you will be looking for are birth certificate, certificate of non-criminal record, current and/or previous personal status certificate (martial status), and name change certificate. Complete documentation about the foreign partner from their home country is prerequisite of the gradual process, and without it one cannot start the partner visa process and get a status in Israel based on their relationship.

## Birth Certificate from the Czech Republic

Name in the Czech Republic: Rodný list

**How to obtain?**

Personally, at the registry office of the city hall (“oddělení matrik na městském úřadě”) ONLY in the place of birth. It is important to get a new birth certificate because it is generally not possible to get an apostille for the original birth certificate from the year of your birth. In case you can not attend personally, a member of your immediate family can get this document for you without the power of attorney. Other people, who are not your family members, will need a power of attorney with your notarized signature.

IMPORTANT: When asking for this document, you need to tell them that you intend to use the document abroad, the clerks in the office should put a different stamp on the document, one that can later be apostilled. It is advised to always mention this information to the clerk, no matter which document you are requesting.

**Cost: 100 CZK**

**Link:** More information and the request form at the website of the municipality of your birth. For example, municipality of Prague 1: [**Link**](https://www.praha1.cz/app/uploads/2018/09/Zadost_o_matricni_doklad_vypis.pdf)

## Name change certificate from the Czech Republic

\* Please note – Name change certificate is not necessary if you did not change your first / last name.

Name in the Czech Republic: Výpis z registru obyvatel

**How to obtain?**

Czechia does not issue a name change certificate. One way to go around it is if you get married in Czechia and change name because of it, the information about it will be on the marriage certificate, which is sometimes enough for Misrad Hapnim.

Other way is to get the extract from the population register, “výpis z registru obyvatel”. It is possible to get this many ways – at the “matrika” of any city hall, some post offices (CzechPOINT), at the ministry of interior, … For our purpose (for getting an apostille) it should ideally be from the city hall with a specific stamp that can be apostillized, but the information is not clear (maybe depends on the clerk?).

More information in the link below. You fill in an application where you ask for relevant information from the register (most importantly previous names, but also your identifying information).

**Cost: 50 CZK** at “obecní úřad obce s rozšířenou působností”

[L**ink**](https://www.mvcr.cz/clanek/vypis-udaju-z-registru-obyvatel.aspx)

## Personal status certificate from the Czech Republic

Name in the Czech Republic: Výpis z registru obyvatel o osobním stavu, or Vysvědčení o právní způsobilosti k uzavření manželství

**How to obtain?**

Same as for name change, Czechia does not issue a personal status certificate. For current personal status, get the extract from the population register, as described above. In the application, ask mainly for your personal status (osobní stav), but also your identifying information.

Second option is the certificate of no impediment to marriage (Vysvědčení o právní způsobilosti k uzavření manželství), obtained before marriage. It includes information about current personal status and it is an original in 4 languages including English, so there should be no need to translate it to Hebrew. It can happen that this document is not issued immediately (from personal experience, I had to come pick it up the following day).

Third option is a declaration of your personal status signed with an Israeli notary. This will probably be needed for married couples who did not get one of the two documents above before getting married.

**Costs**

Výpis z registru obyvatel: **50 CZK**

Vysvědčení o právní způsobilosti k uzavření manželství: **500 CZK**

**Links**

Výpis z registru obyvatel: [**Link**](https://www.mvcr.cz/clanek/vypis-udaju-z-registru-obyvatel.aspx)

Vysvědčení o právní způsobilosti k uzavření manželství: [**Link**](https://www.mvcr.cz/clanek/vysvedceni-o-pravni-zpusobilosti-k-uzavreni-manzelstvi.aspx)

## Certificate of Non-Criminal Record from the Czech Republic

Name in the Czech Republic: Výpis z rejstříku trestů

**How to obtain?**

Personally or via power of attorney at Rejstřík trestů (Soudní street, Prague 4), at some post offices (CzechPOINT), at Czech embassies.

Personal experience: I got this document at CzechPOINT. When I went to the ministry to get the apostille, the clerk said he will try to give me the appostile, but that it is sometimes problematic because they use different stamps at CzechPOINT. In the end he was able to give the apostille, but it is advised to get the document in Prague at Rejstřík trestů and to tell them about the intention of getting an apostille later, so that they put the correct stamp (blue square stamp at the bottom left corner of the document).

**Cost: 100 CZK** (at Czech embassies abroad it is 200 CZK)

[**Link**](https://portal.gov.cz/sluzby-vs/vypis-z-rejstriku-trestu-S8893)

## Verifying the documents with apostille

All official documentation from abroad that needs to be presented in Israel, must be verified by the country of its origin in order to be valid outside of it, in Israel. Many countries use “apostille” for this purpose. An apostille is usually a document, issued by an authorized authority which authenticates the documents in question.  This is used to demonstrate that they are valid documents that can be recognized in other countries.

### Obtaining apostille in the Czech Republic

**How to obtain?**

For some of the documents, including the birth certificate and certificate of no impediment to marriage, it is required to get one more stamp before getting the apostille. This is called “vyšší ověření” and it is possible to get at “krajský úřad”.

The apostilles are issued at the ministries. The ministry of foreign affairs in Prague issues apostilles for the documents required by Misrad Hapnim. Anyone can obtain an apostille, there is no need for power of attorney. For a long time, it was possible to pay only by “kolková známka” obtained beforehand at the post office. Now it should be able to also pay by card.

Other ministries issue apostilles for different documents – for example, ministry of education for university diplomas etc.

**Cost: 300 CZK** for an apostille, **100 CZK** for “vyšší ověření”
