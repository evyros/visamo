# Official Documentation from Italy

This post was written by a volunteer community member. While our volunteers do their best to share the most accurate information, there still may be errors or inaccurate details in this post. Please keep that in mind while reading!

This post includes detailed instructions on how to obtain and authenticate the official documentation and certifications from abroad, that are needed to apply for a partner or spouse visa in Israel. The basic personal documents you will be looking for are birth certificate, certificate of non-criminal record, current and/or previous personal status certificate (martial status), and name change certificate. Complete documentation about the foreign partner from their home country is prerequisite of the gradual process, and without it one cannot start the partner visa process and get a status in Israel based on their relationship.

## Birth Certificate from Italy

Name in Italy: Certificato di nascita

**How to obtain?**

From the municipality of birth in the “registry office” (*ufficio dell’anagrafe*) with a copy of the applicant’s ID.

Otherwise from the link below if the applicant, or in some cases a family member, has an active “digital identity” (Spid, Carta d’identita’ elettronica, Cns)

**Cost:** **0.52 EUR**

[**Link**](https://www.anpr.interno.it/)

## Name change certificate from Italy

\* Please note – Name change certificate is not necessary if you did not change your first / last name.

Name in Italy: Certificato di cambio nome/cognome

## Personal status certificate from Italy

Name in Italy: Certificato di stato libero

**How to obtain?**

From the registry office (*ufficio dell’anagrafe*) of the applicant’s municipality with the applicant’s ID, full details and the reason of the request. It can be requested also from people who are not the applicant in his name.

Otherwise from the link below if the applicant, or in some cases a family member, has an active “digital identity” (Spid, Carta d’identita’ elettronica, Cns)

**Cost: 22 ILS / 6 EUR** at the Italian embassy in Tel Aviv **OR 2.50 EUR** in Italy

[**Link**](https://www.anpr.interno.it/)

## Certificate of Non-Criminal Record from Italy

Name in Italy: Certificato del casellario giudiziale + certificato dei carichi pendenti (2 certificates in total)

**How to obtain?**

The application, in writing, must be sent to a Public Prosecutor’s office (*Procura della Repubblica*) and specify the following:

1. the personal details of the person concerned (i.e. the person to whom the certificate relates), stating clearly – in block capital letters – their family name/surname and forename(s)/given name(s), as well as their place (town and country) and date of birth;
2. the address where the certificate is to be sent; and
3. if the certificate is requested with exemption from payment of any duty and fee adoption proceedings, labour disputes, legal aid applications, judicial redress) or with exemption from payment of *marca da bollo* \[stamp duty\] (for applications, documents, certificates of Non-Profit Organizations, scholarship), the purpose for which it is requested proving entitlement to the exemption.

The application must be signed by the person concerned and specify if is for a *certificato del* *casellario giudiziale* \[certificate of the judicial records\] or a *certificato dei carichi pendenti* \[certificate of outstanding criminal proceedings\].

Certificates are issued in the Italian language and in the German language if issued by the Office in Bolzano/Bozen.

\*Please note – you will need both certificates: Certificato del casellario giudiziale + certificato dei carichi pendenti (2 certificates in total).

**Cost: 19.92 EUR each** (of which € 16 for Apostille stamp and € 3.92 for the certificate)**.**

If the certificate is exempted from the payment of the stamp, only **3.92 EUR** are to be paid foo each one.

In addition, postage costs may apply.

Please note that the acceptable payment methods (eg. international bankers draft, postal cheque, eurogiro etc) of the amount due for issuing a certificate (stamp duty and/or certificate fee and postage costs) differ according to the country from which the certificate is requested and relevant applicable Conventions. It is therefore necessary to ask the *Ufficio del casellario* \[Judicial Records Office\] to which the application is going to be sent what is the appropriate payment method to use.

Public administrations of the Member States not connected as above, and those of the third countries who have signed specific agreements, have to send their requests to the *Ufficio centrale del Casellario* (Central Criminal Record Office) to the following e-mail address: [serviziinternazionali.casellario.centrale@giustizia.it](mailto:serviziinternazionali.casellario.centrale@giustizia.it!).

[**Link**](https://www.giustizia.it/giustizia/it/3_3_1.page) **(in both Italian and English)**

## Verifying the documents with apostille

All official documentation from abroad that needs to be presented in Israel, must be verified by the country of its origin in order to be valid outside of it, in Israel. Many countries use “apostille” for this purpose. An apostille is usually a document, issued by an authorized authority which authenticates the documents in question.  This is used to demonstrate that they are valid documents that can be recognized in other countries.

### Obtaining apostille in Italy

**How to obtain?**

Most of the documents are apostilled by the Prefecture (*Prefettura*) of the same city or province where the document has been released.

For the Certificates of Non-Criminal Records (*Certificato del casellario giudiziale* and *certificato dei carichi pendenti*) the apostille has to to be done at the Public Prosecutor’s office at the Law court (*Procura della Repubblica presso il Tribunale*) of the same city or province where the document has been released.

**Cost: 16 EUR per apostille stamp**

**[Link](https://www.tecnitrad.it/apostille/)**
