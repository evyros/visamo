# Affidavit about children from previous relationships (תצהיר לגבי ילדים מקשרים קודמים)

The foreign partner's affidavit (תצהיר) about children from before your relationship. It must show:

A foreign partner married to an Israeli brings it, with children or without
(5.2.0008 §ד.2.ה): it lists their children from before the marriage or from
a previous marriage, and who has guardianship of them, or states that they
have none. Children who stay behind are listed too. Common-law couples
(ידועים בציבור) don't bring it.

By the procedure, it's part of the file. In practice, a foreign partner who
is abroad signs it once they're in Israel with the entry permit: the online
application can be filed without it.

## How to get it

It's an affidavit (תצהיר), "an affidavit about children from before the
marriage or from a previous marriage, and guardianship of the children"
(תצהיר לגבי ילדים מרווקות או מנישואין קודמים, ואפוטרופסות על הילדים). A
lawyer writes it, or you write it and the foreign partner signs it in front
of a lawyer (עו"ד), who confirms it (see [affidavits.md](../affidavits.md)).

**Signed abroad:** in front of an Israeli consul, or in front of a local
notary with that country's apostille or legalization (see
[certification.md](../certification.md)).

**Declare the personal status in it too.** It's best to make it one
affidavit about both the foreign partner's personal status (מצב אישי) and
their children, like the statement below. If their country issues strong
proof of their children, or that they have none, an affidavit about the
children alone is enough. It doesn't replace the document of their civil
status: only if their country doesn't issue one can this one affidavit
declare their status instead.

## What it should include

- **Each child** from before the marriage or from a previous marriage, with
  their date of birth and who has guardianship of them; or, without
  children, a statement that they have none.
- **The foreign partner's full name and passport number**, their signature,
  and the date.
- **The lawyer's confirmation**, with their stamp and signature.

Children who move to Israel also need their own documents (see
[children.md](../children.md)).

**The statement.** Fill in the details in square brackets, and leave the
signature empty until the foreign partner is in front of the lawyer: only
they sign it, not the Israeli partner. It's written for a
foreign partner without children. With children, replace the line "I don't
have any children" with each child and their details: their full name, date
of birth, and who has guardianship of them.

Statement

I the undersigned [Title] [Full Name] holder of [Country] Passport number [Passport Number], have been instructed to tell the entire truth and nothing but the truth and should we fail to do so, will be subject to penalties applicable by law, and hereby declare the following:

a. I was born on [Date of Birth] in [Place of Birth, Country].

b. On [Marriage Date] me and [Spouse’s Title] [Spouse’s Full Name] got married in [Place of Marriage, Country].

c. I hereby declare that during my marriageable age and until now I had not been married to anyone else.

d. I hereby declare by this affidavit that [Spouse’s Title] [Spouse’s Full Name] [Spouse’s Nationality] ID number [Spouse’s ID Number] is my one and only [husband/wife].

e. I don’t have any children.

f. I affirm and agree that my statement is made for the process of receiving a visa, from Ministry of Interior of Israel.

____________________      ________________
Signature                            Date

## Questions

**My partner has no children. Do we still need it?**
Yes, if you're married. Without children, it states that they have none.

**We're common-law partners. Do we need it?**
No. Common-law couples (ידועים בציבור) don't bring it.

**Does the Israeli partner sign it too?**
No. Only the foreign partner signs it, in front of the lawyer.

**How do we write it?**
Use the statement above: fill in your details. With children, list each one
with their details instead of the line "I don't have any children".

**My partner's children aren't moving to Israel. Do they go in it?**
Yes. It's about all their children from before the marriage or a previous
marriage, and who has guardianship of them.

**My partner is still abroad. Do they have to sign it there?**
No. In practice, they sign it once they're in Israel, in front of an Israeli
lawyer: you can file without it. If a clerk asks for it earlier, they can
sign it abroad, in front of an Israeli consul or a local notary with an
apostille.
