# Bituach Leumi Qualification After Expiry of Visa

Visa holders living in Israel often face great uncertainty around their rights in case of losing their visa or having a visa-gap. The concerns intensify when dealing with residency visas (e.g., A5 temporary resident visa), which provide additional social rights such as National Insurance coverage and public health services.

The internal processes at Bituach Leumi following the expiration of a residency visa lack public accessibility and transparency. To address this, AIC has contacted Bituach Leumi to elucidate the information and the disqualification procedures behind the scenes. This information that is brought to you by AIC for the first time, will help Israelis and their non-Israeli partners who live in Israel with a visa have a better understanding of their rights and possible consequences.

Important terminology for this article:

- “**Qualifying visa**” – a visa that entitles its holder to Bituach Leumi coverage, following a residency determination procedure. [Read more about Bituach Leumi eligibility here](https://aic.org.il/bituach-leumi-eligibility-and-registration-for-new-residents/).
- **Expiry (of visa)** – refers to the date recorded in digital governmental systems, determining the validity of the visa. The expiry date may differ from what’s written on the physical visa sticker in the passport (in case the visa was digitally extended or revoked).

## 45 Days Before Visa Expiry Date – Warning Letter

As many of you are already aware, 45 days **before** the expiry date of a qualifying visa, Bituach Leumi automatically sends a notification letter about the upcoming visa expiration, warning the insured individual about a termination of services in case the visa is not extended.

Although it looks threatening, this letter is sent automatically and does not mean that your visa or residency are at risk. This letter is merely sent by Bituach Leumi as a “service” to visa holders who may not have noticed that their status is nearing expiration.

When receiving this letter, make sure that you have a scheduled [visa renewal meeting](https://aic.org.il/partner-visa-b1-or-a5-renewal-procedures/) before your current visa expires. You do not need to contact Bituach Leumi or notify them of the upcoming meeting, as they will get the updated visa information automatically once it is renewed and updated on PIBA’s systems.

## Visa Expiry Date – Grace Period

Legally, residents’ eligibility for Bituach Leumi services primarily depends on the existence of a valid qualifying visa. Without it, Bituach Leumi is prevented by law from including a person as an insured individual.

Unfortunately, sometimes unforeseen circumstances lead to an unavoidable visa expiration or visa gap. Bituach Leumi recognizes the possibility of complications around visa renewal, alongside the major importance of their services to people’s lives, and would therefore not disqualify a visa holder from coverage immediately. Despite the visa expiry, an insured individual would not immediately lose their eligibility for grants, public health insurance, and allowances from Bituach Leumi as soon as their visa expires.

Normally, no one should reach this situation, and we urge every visa holder in Israel to plan ahead, apply for a visa renewal meeting at least 3 months in advance, and ensure their visa renewal meeting precedes the expiry date of the current visa.

## One Month After the Visa Expired – Appeal Period

Approximately **1 month after** the expiry of the visa, Bituach Leumi would send a termination notice titled “Notification about Cancellation of Residency Status.” The letter includes an explanation about the expiry of your visa and notifies you about your ability to appeal this decision.

From the moment the letter is sent, a countdown of 45 days begins. The internal technical term of this period is “Not Resident in Appeal” / “לא תושב בערעור.”

From this point, usually starting around 1 month after the expiry of the visa, the individual would have the final 45 days to settle their status before losing national insurance coverage. In case a qualifying visa was obtained during this period, Bituach Leumi would get the information from PIBA, and the status as a resident at Bituach Leumi would update automatically\*.

\*In case of re-obtaining a visa close to the end of the 45-day appeal period, Bituach Leumi suggests manually sending a copy of the new visa to a clerk via their website to ensure the information is received on time.

## 45 Days After Termination Notice – Termination

After the 45-day “appeal period” comes to an end, the Bituach Leumi systems would digitally check the visa holder’s status. Failing to regain a qualifying visa before the time runs out would mean termination of your eligibility for Bituach Leumi services, without further notice. This termination happens automatically, based on the data pulled from PIBA.

This means that you will stop receiving any allowances, lose access to public health services, etc.

## Regaining Visa After Termination

When a qualifying visa is regained after disqualification from National Insurance, and **less than 6 months have passed since the expiration of the previous qualifying visa**, the process of rejoining Bituach Leumi is typically faster and simpler.

Regaining the status within 6 months will allow the visa holder to skip the registration process, the recognition of residency process (form BL/625), and the 183-day waiting period. This means that the visa holder is entitled to gain Bituach Leumi services immediately after getting the new visa and without any lengthy procedure or delay.

**In case more than 6 months have passed since the expiry of the last visa**, a full [registration process](https://aic.org.il/bituach-leumi-eligibility-and-registration-for-new-residents/) (including a waiting period) will have to take place before the visa holder will be entitled to coverage again.
